SNL parodies Anthropic's Amodei over AI-doom warnings

Saturday Night Live's Weekend Update parodied Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei's warnings about AI extinction risk, with cast member Jane Wickline playing him opposite host Michael Che, TechCrunch reported.

Saturday Night Live's Weekend Update aired a sketch mocking Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei's recent warnings that artificial intelligence could destroy humanity, TechCrunch reported. Cast member Jane Wickline played Amodei in a wig, delivering halting, awkward answers to host Michael Che during the Sept. 26 broadcast.

The sketch has Wickline's Amodei call artificial intelligence "the devil" and himself "its maker," before insisting it is "not a weapon" but rather "a tool for building weapons," TechCrunch reported. At one point the character breaks into a Gollum-style exchange with a darker side of itself, the outlet said.

The bit also has the fictional Amodei predict a rosy future for cancer treatment, putting the odds at "about a 10% chance" within a decade. He also tells viewers, in the bit, that AI executives "do not condone" what the industry is doing, according to TechCrunch. No other outlet had reported on the sketch as of Sunday, the outlet said.

Amodei has spent recent weeks warning that frontier AI systems could pose catastrophic risks. Those comments have drawn scrutiny from lawmakers, and now from comedy writers too. Neither Anthropic nor NBC issued a statement on the sketch, TechCrunch said.

Meta brings its Muse AI agent to Instagram profiles

Meta's Alexandr Wang said users can now display their Muse AI agent on Instagram profiles, as TestingCatalog reports a dedicated screen-browsing tab and updated voice mode are coming next.

Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang said Sunday that users can now display their Muse AI companion on their Instagram profile. "You can now add your muse to your Instagram profile," Wang wrote on X. He added that people can "show off your superintelligent sidekick to all your friends."

https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2104282526684787044

TestingCatalog reported separately that Muse is getting a dedicated tab for browsing its virtual-machine screen. The tab would let users track what the agent is doing as it works. The outlet said Muse's web voice mode has also changed, now appearing as a voice-calling widget. Neither feature is public yet, and it was not clear when a wider rollout might follow.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2104270840980189266

Muse is Meta's consumer AI agent. The company introduced it at Meta Connect in late September as a chatbot that scans linked accounts for unclaimed funds, unused subscriptions and duplicate charges, TechCrunch reported. It operates through a phone app and a Tamagotchi-style wearable device, positioning it against products such as OpenAI's own consumer assistant.

The rollout comes as TechCrunch separately reported that Muse faces skepticism over Meta's ad-driven business model. Sean O'Kane, who tested the agent, said Meta "will make the argument that the more they know about you, the more accurate and interesting the ads will be." That tension remains unaddressed by the company, TechCrunch said.
