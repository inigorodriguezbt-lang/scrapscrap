Theo says he built a Rust TypeScript compiler with agents, reading none of it

Developer Theo says agents wrote tsc-rs, an open-source Rust rewrite of TypeScript's compiler and language server, for about $20,000 of Opus tokens after roughly $400,000 of Codex spend got nowhere.

The IntCyberDigest account reports Samsung's Galaxy S26 was hacked three times on day one of Pwn2Own Ireland, with $388,500 paid for 32 zero-days, including bugs in LiteLLM and OpenAI Codex.

AI developer tooling now sits on the contest's target list beside phones and smart-home gear, a sign of how widely agent software is deployed.


Bloomberg's business account reports CoreWeave is entering India with 240 megawatts of AI data centres on an Adani campus; the paper has not seen terms or a company statement.

It would be the GPU cloud's first Indian footprint, putting Nvidia-based capacity into one of the largest markets for AI app users.


Korean media reports, relayed by analyst Jukan, say Samsung asked AMD to place foundry orders in return for supplying HBM memory; a senior industry official says AMD sees the foundry move as too risky.

HBM supply is the bottleneck for AI accelerators, so memory makers are tying it to demands for chip-manufacturing contracts.


Theo says the tsc-rs rewrite consumed the equivalent of about ten weeks of usage on a $200 Claude subscription, a figure he gave on top of his token-spend estimates.

It is an unusual public data point on how much flat-rate subscription capacity a large agent-built project can consume.
