Google is testing Gemini 4 variants Argon, Barium and Carbon, Business Insider reports via The Decoder; an employee compared Carbon's coding to Opus 5.5, though it needs more testing and no launch date is set.

Carbon runs on Google's internal coding platform Jetski, and a name-change to Argon is unconfirmed.


Latent Space's AINews says TypeSafe's Jev decision model passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue at a $7.5 billion valuation three weeks after launch, citing a leaked Sequoia case study it could not confirm.

Jev is the single-pass decision model that Microsoft, OpenAI and Cloudflare have since answered with their own, which makes the unconfirmed revenue figure notable.


Hesamation says that after six months away from Claude Code, Opus 5.5 is efficient, burning 50 percent of a Max 20x weekly allowance on long tasks, and that 'ultracode' found a memory-leak cause another tool missed.

The post is one user's account, but it adds a field report on Opus 5.5's usage cost and on ultracode, which the poster advises against for implementation work.


International Cyber Digest relays a Nikkei Asia report that Apple told some suppliers to cut October iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max parts orders by at least 15 percent, with prices $100 higher as AI data centres lift memory costs.

If accurate, the report ties weaker phone demand to AI memory costs; we did not open Nikkei's article.


Simon Willison notes Dwarf Fortress now uses version control, after creator Tarn Adams said in 2013 and 2016 he avoided it and in 2023 mentioned having 'version control stuff to use now'.

The long-running game's codebase is famously complex, which makes the late move to version control a small piece of programming folklore.
