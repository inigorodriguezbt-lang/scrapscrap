Nvidia's seven-year-old Shield TV Pro now costs $299.99 after a roughly 50% price rise, and the entry-level Shield TV is gone, Tom's Hardware reported, blaming the AI memory shortage.

Wired also reports a $100 increase, a sign that AI demand for memory is raising prices on ordinary consumer electronics.


AWS chief Matt Garman said Amazon no longer uses nondisclosure agreements with government agencies on data-center projects, and claims it put more than $1 billion into host communities over three years, TechCrunch reported.

Critics say NDAs kept neighbours in the dark, and over 100 data-center moratoriums are under consideration across the US.


Pop!_OS maker System76 has banned AI-generated code from many of its COSMIC desktop codebases, Neowin reported; the story reached 56 points on the Hacker News front page.

It joins open-source projects drawing a line against AI-written contributions, a split that maintainers are now settling repo by repo.


The X account mark_k says Gemini Pro models will drop off Google's cheaper Plus plan and stay on Pro and Ultra only, a move the account reads as preparing for Gemini 4 Argon.

The Gemini 4 Argon link is his inference, not a Google statement; the plan change itself is unconfirmed by Google in the post.


AMD's Adrenalin 26.9.2 driver shipped with unreleased codenames ATlite3, AT2 and Gainsborough, which leaker Kepler_L2 says may be the PlayStation 6 GPU and the Steam Deck 2 chip, according to Int Cyber Digest.

The identifications are a leaker's guesses from naming patterns, and neither Sony, Valve nor AMD has confirmed them.
