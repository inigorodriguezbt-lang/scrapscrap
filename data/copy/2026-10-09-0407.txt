Qwen3.8-Max has a 1M-token context, and Wan3.0 makes video up to 30 seconds with audio, according to GMI's own page.


Reuters reports USA Today Co. has sued OpenAI for copyright infringement over AI training; The Verge says the filing seeks more than $250 million and names local papers including The Tennessean.

It joins suits by the New York Times, Ziff Davis, Britannica and nearly 400 local newspapers; OpenAI had not commented when The Verge published.

Elon Musk said on X that Grok Bots will manage Grokipedia, a post that drew roughly 1,800 likes within hours.

Grokipedia is xAI's Wikipedia rival, so the post suggests its upkeep moves from human editors to bots; Musk gave no timeline or detail.


Tom's Hardware reports Ukrainian drones hit Yandex data centers in Sasovo, Russia, which house two Nvidia-powered supercomputers; their fate is unknown.

GlobalFoundries will make silicon interposers for TSMC's CoWoS packaging in the US under a five-year deal valued at $2 billion, Tom's Hardware reports.

CoWoS packaging is the bottleneck behind Nvidia and AMD AI accelerators, so adding a US interposer source widens that supply chain.


Lumentum's chief executive says its optical components for AI servers are completely sold out through early 2029, Bloomberg reports.

Optical links join GPUs and memory on the list of AI data-center parts that buyers are booking years ahead.


An American faces 18 months in prison and an $8 million fine for using 10,000 bots to stream his own AI-generated songs, in the first criminal case of its kind, Tom's Hardware reports.

The case sets a precedent for treating AI-assisted streaming fraud as a crime, not a terms-of-service breach.
