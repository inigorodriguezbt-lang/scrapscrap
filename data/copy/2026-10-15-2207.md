## Apple publishes speeds for Meta's 816B Muse Spark Open on linked Mac Studios

**Apple's research team says four M5 Ultra Mac Studios linked over Thunderbolt 5 decode Meta's 816-billion-parameter Muse Spark Open at 36.73 tokens a second.**

Apple's machine-learning team published benchmarks on Thursday for running huge language models on Mac Studios joined by RDMA over Thunderbolt 5. The post covers Meta's Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 42 billion active. Apple says it needs about 493GB at 4-bit, so it cannot fit on one machine.

By Apple's measurements, decoding at an 8,192-token context reached 15.74 tokens a second on two M5 Ultra machines using pipeline parallelism. Tensor parallelism gave 24.99, and four machines reached 36.73. Apple also tested Qwen 3.8 (27 billion parameters), which hit 50.89 tokens a second on four machines, and Mistral Medium 3.5 (128 billion), at 23.37.

Against the M3 Ultra, Apple says a single M5 Ultra produces its first token up to 4.6 times faster and decodes about 1.6 times faster. It credits the M5 Ultra's 1.2 TB/s memory bandwidth, against 819 GB/s. It lists peak power of about 300W against about 200W. These figures are Apple's own and have not been reproduced.

The clustering needs macOS 26.2, which added a low-latency RDMA driver over Thunderbolt 5, and MLX 0.30.1 or later. Apple says doubling the cables between each pair of machines cut time to first token at 65,536 context from about 50.5 seconds to about 40.6 on four machines. Meta calls the model the first in its class to run on a desktop with two M5 Ultra Mac Studios, according to The Verge.

## Bluesky's Attie now builds games, quizzes and sites that live on the AT Protocol

**Bluesky's CEO calls it 'vibe coding for your community': Attie, once a feed-building tool, now lets people make sites, polls and games on the AT Protocol with a model of their choice.**

Bluesky announced on Thursday at TechCrunch Disrupt that Attie, its experimental AI app, can now build websites, quizzes, games, polls and other interactive projects, TechCrunch reported. Attie began as a tool for creating custom feeds. Chief executive Toni Schneider called the new feature "social vibe coding" and "vibe coding for your community".

According to TechCrunch, creators connect through their AT Protocol identity, and Attie suggests project ideas drawn from their public Bluesky profile. Users can bring their own AI model or pick open-weight ones. Finished projects live on the AT Protocol, which underpins Bluesky and other apps, and can draw on Bluesky's audience.

Schneider told the outlet that vibe coding today is "a single-user, solo experience" and that Bluesky wants people to build together. Chief operating officer Rose Wang said Attie's appeal is that it "comes with the Bluesky distribution" rather than standing alone. TechCrunch put Bluesky at about 47 million registered users, with engagement down as some returned to X.

Support for private data, which developers have asked for most, is in alpha testing, TechCrunch reported, with a rollout planned by the end of 2026. The report gives no pricing or rollout detail. We have not tried the feature, and Bluesky's description of what it can build is the company's own.

## Schaller used Claude to write a Linux scanner driver and fix a laptop camera

**Schaller says Claude Code and a USB-trace harness produced a Linux driver for a Plustek film scanner that beat the upstream backend at full resolution, 34.6 seconds against 54.4.**

Christian Schaller, a longtime GNOME and Red Hat developer, published a post on Thursday describing how he used Claude, including Claude Code, to reverse engineer hardware drivers. The post reached the Hacker News front page, with 64 points and 68 comments when read. He does not name a model version.

His method, released as a tool called hw-harness, records the vendor's Windows driver in a KVM virtual machine with the device passed through. It captures USB traffic on the Linux host, repeats the operation with a Linux driver and compares the traces. Where there is no bus to tap, it reads Windows INF files, registry entries and ACPI tables.

The first result was a SANE backend for the Plustek OpticFilm 8200i, whose Genesys GL128 controller had no Linux support. Schaller says it covers colour, grey and infrared modes from 300 to 7200 dpi. He reports a full-resolution scan in 34.6 seconds against 54.4 for upstream SANE's genesys backend, plus infrared dust removal. Upstream offers more resolution steps.

He also wrote Negative Vibes, a Rust GNOME app for scanning negatives. He says he fixed kernel and libcamera bugs on a ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 14's camera, raising low-light frame brightness from 31.1 to 90.9. By his account the results are imperfect. Blue stayed about 17 per cent short, and each comparison is a single unit, which he says may not generalise.

## ChatGPT in Chrome can read open tabs and, in Work mode, act on them

**OpenAI says its Chrome extension, open to all ChatGPT plans, can compare tabs for you and, in Work, send messages, fill forms and download files in your browser.**

OpenAI said on Thursday that it is bringing computer use to anyone who uses Chrome. In a post on the ChatGPT account, which had more than 2,500 likes when read at about 22:00 UTC, the company said ChatGPT can now use open tabs to make answers more useful. It says the extension is available on all ChatGPT plans.

In Chat, OpenAI says, ChatGPT can look across the tabs a user has open and gather information. In Work, it says ChatGPT can control Chrome tabs to send messages, fill in forms or download files, and can group a pile of tabs. A second post says the full ChatGPT, including its history, sits beside the page in a side panel.

https://x.com/ChatGPT/status/2110823738531037651

OpenAI's examples include comparing three couches open in tabs by dimensions, material, delivery time and return policy. It also suggests summarising an article's argument and evidence, or filling a registration form from an event brief and stopping before submitting. These are the company's descriptions, and we have not tested them.

OpenAI's documentation says ChatGPT asks before acting on a new site, with choices to allow once, allow for that site, allow for all sites, or decline. It advises treating page content as untrusted. Digital Trends reported earlier updates that let the extension read tabs, so this announcement is the company's account of the latest upgrade.
