OpenAI fires 3 safety researchers over alleged leaks, WSJ reports

OpenAI confirmed violations of its rules on sensitive information, the Wall Street Journal reports, after firing alignment researchers Jasmine Wang, Mikita Balesni and Tomek Korbak. David Robinson also left.


Sean Parker says Stability AI is rebuilding around music tools for professionals

Parker says he is "playing by the rules this time", TechCrunch reports, as Stability AI ships three audio models and music-editing software after $76 million from Sony, Warner and Universal.

SNIPPETS
------------------------------------------------------------

Ai2 open-sourced AstaBrief, an 8-billion-parameter report-generation model built on Qwen3-8B. Ai2 says its fast mode averages 51.1 seconds per report against 178.5 for a Claude-powered comparison system.

A small model that runs on a user's own hardware suits institutions that cannot send sensitive research to a hosted service, though the speed comparison is the maker's own.


Elon Musk said Tesla's Austin robotaxi service now runs until 11pm instead of 10pm, adding the hard part is not running over pets at night: "grey kittens on grey tarmac in the dark."

The post, which drew about 11,000 likes by early morning, is a rare public account of a specific night-driving problem the robotaxi team says it is solving.


Gemini's mobile apps now have a Map tool that adds a chosen area to your prompt as context, 9to5Google reports, and Google is testing swapping the slash for @ to call skills.

The tool sits beside Photos, Camera and Files in the prompt carousel on Android and iOS, so location-aware questions no longer need the area typed out.


Tom's Hardware reports Sony is bringing AI-powered upscaling called QSSR to the standard PS5, built with AMD on a streamlined neural network, to offer what it calls a taste of the PS5 Pro.

It would give owners of the base console some of the Pro's neural upscaling, though game support and release timing were not visible in the report excerpt.


Ars Technica reports the seven-year-old Nvidia Shield TV is now $100 more expensive, with the increase attributed to AI.

It is a small, concrete sign of AI-driven component demand reaching consumer hardware that has nothing to do with AI itself.


Tom's Hardware reports Raspberry Pi prices are rising by up to 23%, the third hike this year, as LPDDR4 and LPDDR5 memory costs climb amid component shortages.

Hobbyist boards used for small local AI projects are getting pricier alongside memory shortages that have also hit gaming and server hardware.


Tom's Hardware reports a hardware vice president says OpenAI's Jalapeño ASICs are deployed alongside AMD EPYC Turin CPUs as hosts, with Nvidia's standalone Vera "a little bit behind" on maturity.

It is a glimpse of how OpenAI's own chips are being paired with AMD host processors, and a remark on Nvidia's rival host part.
