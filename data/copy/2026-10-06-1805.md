## Google releases Apache 2.0 EmbeddingGemma 2 for text, images, audio and video

**Google DeepMind says its 740M-parameter EmbeddingGemma 2 maps text, code, images, video and audio into one space, runs in about 567MB of RAM on a Pixel 11 Pro, and ships with open weights.**

Google DeepMind released EmbeddingGemma 2 on Tuesday, calling it its first natively multimodal open model for on-device embeddings. According to Google's announcement, the 740 million-parameter model puts text, code, images, video and audio into a single shared embedding space. The weights are under an Apache 2.0 licence and are on Hugging Face and Kaggle.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2107522559109476786

Google's developer blog describes a modular design: a 270 million-parameter text model, plus a 170 million-parameter vision encoder and a 300 million-parameter audio encoder, according to Unsloth's summary. The context window is 8K tokens, four times the first version's. Google says outputs are 768 dimensions and can be truncated to 512, 256 or 128.

Google claims the model is best in class for its size on its MTEB and MAEB benchmarks, and says it matches or beats many larger models across text, vision and audio tasks. It reports a 9.92-point gain on MTEB Code, from 68.76 to 78.68. These are Google's figures against models the company says are more than twice its size. No outside benchmark had appeared at publication.

The pitch is private search on a phone or laptop. Google says developers can find moments in a video from a voice memo, or pair the model with Gemma 4 for on-device retrieval. It puts memory use at roughly 191MB for the quantised text-only model and 567MB for the full multimodal version on a Pixel 11 Pro. Unsloth says the full model runs in 0.5GB of RAM.

Support arrived quickly. Georgi Gerganov, creator of llama.cpp, said the library has day-0 support, and Unsloth posted GGUF files and a fine-tuning guide. Google also lists LiteRT, MediaPipe and transformers.js as ways to run it. TestingCatalog posted an early hands-on video of the model, though Google's benchmark claims have not been independently checked.

## Claude moves into Google Docs, Sheets and Slides with an editing sidebar

**Anthropic says Claude now sits beside open Google files, reads them and edits in place with approval for each change, and Google files can open inside Claude, in beta on paid plans.**

Anthropic said on Tuesday that Claude now works inside Google Docs, Sheets and Slides, and that those files also open inside Claude. In Google Workspace, the company says, Claude sits in a sidebar next to the file, reads what the user has open and edits it in place. Anthropic's post said users can approve each edit before it lands.

https://x.com/claudeai/status/2107522596845822135

The reverse direction is in the Claude app itself. Anthropic says a user can paste a Google file link, or ask for a new doc, sheet or deck, and it opens beside the chat so the user and Claude can edit together. The company says access follows the person's existing Google sharing permissions, so Claude sees only what that user could already open.

Anthropic describes the feature as a beta available on all paid plans. It did not say in the posts how many Workspace accounts are eligible, whether administrators can switch it off, or how edits are logged. Those are open questions for companies that keep contracts and financial models in Google files.

Anthropic's launch post drew more than 3,500 likes on X within about an hour. No independent testing of the feature had been published at the time of writing, so any claim about how well Claude edits spreadsheets or slide layouts is the company's own.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-claude-google-workspace/

## AI-designed open-source openTPU runs Qwen3-0.6B on an FPGA card

**A GitHub project says AI agents designed an open accelerator from RTL to compiler, and it decodes 30.7 tokens a second on Qwen3-0.6B on an FPGA card.**

A GitHub repository called openTPU describes itself as "an open-source AI accelerator, developed by AI". Its author, who goes by FeSens, says AI agents took part in the design of the chip, the SystemVerilog logic, the instruction set, a simulator, a compiler and a profiler, all in one repo. The README asks how far agents can go at hardware design, and whether they can build the chip that runs their own inference.

The README says the design runs on a Kintex-7 PCIe card, an Inspur YPCB-00338. It lists 82.1 tokens a second for decoding and 335.4 for prefill on LFM2.5-230M at 4-bit, and 30.7 decode and 103.4 prefill on Qwen3-0.6B at 4-bit. These are the author's own measurements.

The author also reports that the simulator and the physical card match bit for bit, and that decoding reaches 82 to 85 per cent of the card's peak DRAM bandwidth. The README ties each result to a specific build date between 29 September and 1 October, and says performance varies with the quantisation scheme and model size.

It follows an earlier project by the same author, auto-arch-tournament, in which agents competed on architecture decisions, according to the README. The repository had 108 stars, five forks and no open issues when read on Tuesday. The post reached 103 points and 73 comments on the Hacker News front page.

Nobody else appears to have reproduced the numbers. They are small models on a modest FPGA card, nowhere near datacentre silicon, and the README does not say how much of the design the agents wrote unaided. As a demonstration of agents working through a full chip toolchain, it is the part people on Hacker News were discussing.

## Google to strip Flash and Pro from free Gemini on 9 October

**Engadget reports that from 9 October free Gemini users lose Flash and Pro and keep only Flash-Lite, while the $5 AI Plus plan loses Gemini 3.1 Pro and a new effort-level setting arrives.**

Google is removing free access to its Flash and Pro Gemini models on 9 October, Engadget reported on Tuesday. After that date, the outlet says, free users will be limited to Flash-Lite, the company's most basic model. Free users could previously choose between three models in the app. Google had not been quoted in the report at the time of reading.

The cut also reaches paying customers. According to Engadget, subscribers to Google AI Plus, the $5 a month tier, lose access to Gemini 3.1 Pro. Anyone who wants Pro will need AI Pro at $20 a month or AI Ultra, which Engadget lists at $100 and up.

Google is adding something in return. Engadget says Gemini gets an effort-level setting with low, medium and high options, which controls how deeply it works on a task and how many resources it uses. AI Ultra subscribers get an exclusive Deep Think option for the most demanding prompts, the report says.

Engadget describes Pro as the model for complex maths and coding prompts and for understanding text, files, images and video. That is the model free users are about to lose, which makes the change one of the larger tightenings of free AI access this year. These details come from Engadget, not from a Google statement.

What is not clear is whether the limits apply in every country, or whether existing free chats using Pro will carry on. Google has not said, according to the reports. The date is three days away, so the company's own help pages should show the final terms soon.

## Anthropic gives startups a free year of Claude Team and $1,000 in API credits

**Anthropic says startups founded in the last five years or funded in the last two can get a year of Claude Team, $1,000 in API credits and up to $45,000 in partner discounts.**

Anthropic announced an expanded Claude Startups program on Tuesday, during San Francisco Tech Week. According to TechCrunch, members get a free year of Claude Team, the paid group plan, with up to five premium seats. Developers also receive a one-time $1,000 in API credits, which Anthropic's post says arrives as soon as a company is approved.

https://x.com/claudeai/status/2107513494493179954

Eligibility is broad. Anthropic says companies founded in the last five years, or funded in the last two, can apply. The company also says members can book virtual office hours with its Applied AI team and join events such as Founder House, hackathons and meetups as space allows. TechCrunch adds that members get access to Claude Marketplace for building plug-ins.

The programme adds a Claude Startup Stack of offers from companies building with Claude, including Linear, Lovable, ElevenLabs, Granola and Hex. Anthropic says these are worth discounts and credits of up to $45,000 on tools for sales, design and data engineering. Its posts also promise up to $7,000 in Claude products and credits.

Anthropic told TechCrunch it built the program because "the benefits of AI will reach most people through the companies that build on top of models." The offer ties new companies to Claude early. Rivals compete for the same developers, and Anthropic did not name any.

Anthropic did not say how many startups it expects to admit or how approvals are decided. The figures above are the company's own, per its posts and TechCrunch. Nobody outside the company has yet reported on how the application process works in practice.
