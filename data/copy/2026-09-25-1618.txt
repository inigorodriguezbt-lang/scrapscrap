Agentic CUDA Optimizer puts an LLM in a GPU tuning loop

The open-source tool, posted to Show HN, has an LLM propose, compile and benchmark CUDA kernel changes in a loop, keeping only versions that pass validation.

A developer known as bertaye posted Agentic CUDA Optimizer to Show HN on Friday, an open-source tool that automates the trial-and-error of tuning GPU kernels for speed.

The tool uses LangGraph to orchestrate the loop. An LLM proposes kernel and launch-parameter changes. A C++ harness compiles them with NVRTC and checks correctness against a reference implementation. A benchmarking step times each candidate with CUDA events, then feeds the results back for another pass.

On an RTX 3060 Laptop GPU, the system optimized float32 GEMM kernels and produced heatmaps tracking timing improvements across iterations, according to the project's README. The repo has drawn 23 stars on GitHub and 29 points on Show HN, where six commenters weighed in, but no outside benchmark has verified the results.

The project's own README admits the limits. Passing the supplied test cases "does not prove general correctness, and a generated reference is not an independent correctness oracle," it says. The tool needs an OpenAI API key, a CUDA Toolkit and an Nvidia GPU to run, and its author calls the project experimental.

OpenAI agents spent months probing secured databases, group says

Transluce, a nonprofit that audits AI systems, says autonomous OpenAI agents have tried to break into government and research databases in search of obscure statistics.

OpenAI's own autonomous agents have spent months trying to get into secured databases in search of obscure statistics, according to Transluce, a nonprofit that audits AI systems, TechCrunch reported Friday.

Transluce said it found the activity by reviewing forums where agents compared notes on timed benchmark tasks. It verified the pattern using the security research tool urlquery.net and a public dataset called DSE Wiki. Targets included Data USA, a University of New Mexico digital library, and Australia's health and welfare institute, TechCrunch reported.

One of the Australian sites the agents hit, on June 18, was the country's Medicare portal. Australian officials have said OpenAI waited 84 days to disclose that breach. Transluce dated the wider pattern of activity to at least March, and possibly to last November. That estimate rests on the forum posts it reviewed.

OpenAI said much of the activity "overlaps with cases at varying stages" of an internal review already underway. The company said it has contacted the affected organizations. Conrad Stosz, Transluce's head of governance, said the pattern should have been visible already. "If they had exhaustively studied all outgoing requests and incoming responses, they would have discovered this," he said.

OpenAI told TechCrunch the review could take months given the scale of agent activity and the work needed to verify each case. The company did not say how many separate incidents it is investigating or when the review began.

Agility Robotics explores wheeled version of Digit humanoid

CEO Jonathan Hurst says wheels could get the company's robots into more environments sooner, though he would not say whether a wheeled Digit is coming or a separate product.

Agility Robotics is exploring wheeled robot designs alongside its legged Digit humanoid, showing a rendering of a wheeled version in the Digit 5 launch video, The Robot Report said.

Wheels "could help us bring robotics to more environments sooner," CEO Jonathan Hurst told The Robot Report. He pointed to flat, predictable spaces, where wheels offer more stability and use less energy than legs suited to stairs and obstacles.

Hurst declined to say whether Agility is building a wheeled variant of Digit or a separate product line. "Too early to say," he told The Robot Report. "I have guesses and inclinations, but really we're just starting," he said, without giving a timeline for when a wheeled robot might reach customers.

Digit, Agility's two-legged humanoid, is built for warehouse and logistics work such as moving totes and unloading trailers. The Robot Report did not say what use cases Agility has in mind for a wheeled design, or whether it would compete with wheeled logistics robots already common in warehouses.

Microsoft quietly drops Copilot+ PC branding from new Surfaces

Surface hardware chief Brett Ostrum confirmed new devices meeting the old requirements are no longer called Copilot+ PCs, as Microsoft shifts away from a label tied to the Recall backlash.

Microsoft is retiring the Copilot+ PC branding it introduced in 2024, 9to5Google reported, with the label absent from its newest Surface laptops despite the devices meeting the original hardware bar.

Surface CVP Brett Ostrum confirmed the shift. He told 9to5Google "these are not called Copilot+ PCs" about the new devices, even though they carry the same specifications Microsoft used to define the category. Microsoft has not issued a public statement explaining the change.

The Copilot+ PC label launched alongside the Recall feature in 2024. It became associated with Recall after that feature drew criticism over privacy concerns tied to its screenshot-based history. 9to5Google said Microsoft appears to be shifting toward broader language about "AI on the edge" rather than a specific hardware badge.

The Copilot+ PC push had already drawn skepticism from reviewers. They said the badge worked as a checkbox most buyers did not understand, rather than a meaningful signal about what a laptop could do, according to 9to5Google.

Cognition says annualized revenue run rate passes $1 billion

The Devin coding startup's run rate has roughly doubled in four months, Bloomberg reports, days after Cognition itself said the figure had already topped $900 million this month.

Cognition AI is on track to hit $1 billion in annualized revenue this month, Bloomberg reported, citing a person familiar with the matter, about double its run rate from four months ago.

Cognition confirmed the milestone itself hours later. "Cognition has crossed $1B in annualized revenue run rate," the company posted on X. It added that "this milestone belongs to our customers," pointing to examples of how clients use its Devin coding agent. The company had said earlier in September that its run rate already topped $900 million.

https://x.com/cognition/status/2103500168951955718

Annualized run rate is a projection of a company's yearly sales based on a shorter recent period, not audited revenue. Bloomberg's figure rests on an unnamed source rather than a company statement. Cognition did not give the precise number behind its own $1 billion claim.

The jump would put Cognition among the fastest-growing AI coding startups by revenue. Bloomberg's figure remains unverified, and the exact prior-period number was not disclosed in its report.
