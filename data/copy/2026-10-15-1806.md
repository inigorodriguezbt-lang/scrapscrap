## Meta releases Muse Spark Open, an 816B model it calls America's best open one

**Meta says Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a 1M-token context, is the most capable US open-weight model to date, and the weights are on Hugging Face today.**

Meta released Muse Spark Open on Thursday, an open-weight model that its AI research arm calls "the most capable US open-weight model to date." It has 816 billion total parameters, 42 billion active per token, and a 1M-token context window. Meta says it takes text, image, video and audio as input and was built for coding and agentic work.

https://x.com/AIatMeta/status/2110779754274292212

Hugging Face's launch post, which describes the architecture, lists 256 experts per layer with eight chosen per token, 62 layers, and a separate 1.8B-parameter encoder for images and video. The model always reasons: effort levels run from low to xhigh, and the post says reasoning cannot be turned off. Output is text only.

The weights are on Hugging Face, Amazon SageMaker AI, Google Cloud Model Garden and Microsoft Foundry. Meta says hosted versions run on Baseten, Fireworks AI, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter and Together AI. It says the licence is the Muse Model License, with limited commercial terms that let many developers use the model for free.

https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2110780784051474666

The claims are Meta's. Engadget reports that Meta's own benchmarks suggest the model beats GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 on agentic tasks and visual reasoning, but the figures come from the company. Meta's blog post links an evaluation report without giving scores in its text, and says a full safety evaluation report is coming soon.

Meta says it ran the model through its Superintelligence Scaling Framework and that it met the release thresholds, with extra safety post-training because the weights are open. Nobody outside Meta has published independent tests yet. The BF16 checkpoint is about 1.5 TB, according to Hugging Face, so most people will reach it through hosted APIs.

## Perplexity open-sources SPACE, the Firecracker sandbox behind its Computer agent

**Perplexity has released SPACE under Apache 2.0, the microVM sandbox platform it says runs every Perplexity Computer task, and reports no escapes from the VM in 108 red-team attempts.**

Perplexity open-sourced SPACE on Thursday, the sandbox platform that it says runs every task in Perplexity Computer. In a post on X the company said each agent task gets its own Firecracker microVM, with credentials and outbound traffic controlled from outside the VM. The code is on GitHub under the Apache 2.0 licence.

https://x.com/perplexity_ai/status/2110778478312481167

The repository describes SPACE as Sandboxed Platform for Agentic Code Execution, written in Rust, with an HTTP API to create Linux environments, run commands and collect output. It supports pausing, suspending and waking sandboxes, warm pools on Kubernetes, and a compatibility wrapper for the E2B Python SDK. At the time of reading it had 67 stars.

Perplexity chief executive Aravind Srinivas said sandboxes are a commodity but serving millions of them under production traffic takes a lot of detail. Unite.AI, summarising Perplexity's own materials, reports the company says creation is three to five times faster than with the commercial provider it replaced in July. The company has also given a median creation time of 60 milliseconds.

The security figures are Perplexity's. Per Unite.AI, testers recorded no escape from the VM to the host across 108 attempts, and some attempts did find ways around network restrictions that Perplexity says it fixed. It also says it reported related weaknesses to other sandbox vendors. None of this has been independently verified.

Unite.AI reports that CoreWeave and Akamai have launched SPACE on their clouds and that Crusoe is adding it to its managed AI services. Those partner claims come from the same Perplexity materials. The repository's own page shows no benchmark or red-team results, only links to security policy and smoke tests.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/perplexity-space-open-source/

## Anthropic gives six METR staff employee-like access and no veto

**Anthropic says a six-person METR team now has the access of its internal risk reviewers, and that it cannot block METR's findings, only redact narrow categories.**

Anthropic said on Thursday that a team from the evaluation group METR now has employee-like access to its frontier models. In a post on independent evaluators, the company says six METR staff get access comparable to its internal risk teams, including company hardware, office space and internal tools. It also says it signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence.

Anthropic says it cannot veto what METR publishes. It can require redactions in narrow, predefined categories, and METR may say publicly whether it thinks a redaction was justified. Customer data, attorney-client material and model architecture details are the protected categories that staff need not share. METR's own account of the arrangement was not among the sources reviewed.

The post sets out three access tiers. Tier one covers narrow investigations whose publication Anthropic must approve; tier two allows publication without approval; tier three matches METR's arrangement. SecureBio is reviewing the biological and chemical weapons chapter of Anthropic's August Risk Report, and Accenture and Faculty are pursuing a broader partnership on red-teaming.

Anthropic says Hugging Face and several other groups are in early talks. It lists what it wants from evaluators: expertise in frontier models, independence including no equity stake in Anthropic, enough access, and freedom to publish. It adds, "We're embedding evaluators voluntarily, and we believe every frontier AI company should do the same."

Commentator Andrew Curran called it the kind of post a lab writes before releasing an unusually powerful model, which is speculation and not reporting. The timing sits beside reports of an Anthropic IPO, and NYC's comptroller was reported to be urging a pause. Anthropic's post does not mention a release or a listing.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/anthropic-metr-employee-access/

## Accenture caps Claude funding at $29 a month per employee, 404 Media says

**A memo seen by 404 Media says Accenture will fund only $29 of Claude use per person a month, though some staff had been spending up to $1,000.**

Accenture has told employees it will centrally fund only $29 a month per person for Claude, 404 Media reported on Thursday. The outlet saw an internal announcement titled "Claude Enterprise Users: Important Updates to Licensing and Usage Costs." It says the change took effect on October 1. No other outlet has confirmed the memo.

Costs are consumption-based. According to the memo, anyone who goes over $29 must charge the extra to a specific project or team, tracked through a work breakdown structure code. Premium seats include $250 of token usage a week, the memo says, and many employees are not on Premium.

404 Media says some employees had been spending as much as $1,000 a month, and one reported costs above $500 for several months. The memo warns that "Providing a WBS and obtaining a license does not guarantee indefinite or ongoing access to Claude." One employee told the outlet their access had been revoked outright.

Licences may be reassessed or reallocated, the memo says. An Accenture spokesperson told 404 Media, "At the foundation, we make AI available to all our people." The article reviewed does not say how many employees are affected.

The memo gives a rare number for what heavy use costs a large employer: a four-figure monthly bill for a single worker. Accenture is also one of the evaluator partners named in Anthropic's update on Thursday, though nothing in the article links the two.

## Goodfire essay argues neural networks are already clean, not tangled

**Goodfire's Nick Cammarata says superposition was the wrong explanation: retrained image models reach about 99% interpretable neurons, and a rough Qwen3-32B audit finds about 90% have one main job.**

Goodfire researcher Nick Cammarata published an essay titled "Neural Networks Are Already Clean," which reached the front page of Hacker News with 118 points. It argues that early evidence of messy, polysemantic neurons came mostly from fixable problems: architecture, weak tools and poor explanations. He calls it an informal working hypothesis.

The essay says the canonical "cat and car" neuron in InceptionV1 is better described as a V-shape detector. It reports that the model's last layer was roughly 5% interpretable with 2017-era tools, about 50% with better ones and about 70% when retrained with modern regularisation, while a same-size ConvNeXt reaches about 99%.

Cammarata tested the rival superposition idea by squeezing InceptionV1's layer 3b from 480 neurons to 10. If superposition drove the mess, he says, the neurons should have become superposed; instead they looked coarser. The squeezed model still scored 58.35% top-1 on ImageNet, according to the essay.

For language models, the essay reports a preliminary audit of Qwen3-32B. It says about 90% of neuron poles have one main job, about 7% have two related jobs and about 2 to 4% are not yet understood. Cammarata stresses these are rough and depend on thresholds, and that agents did much of the investigating.

A technical companion paper, which the essay says will include results on models up to trillions of parameters, is forthcoming. The claim runs against the wide use of sparse autoencoders, and the essay says a sparse autoencoder recovered curve detectors poorly in its test. None of this has been replicated outside Goodfire yet.
