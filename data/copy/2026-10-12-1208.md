## DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus reaches Science with nine Erdős problems solved

**Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus, a Gemini-plus-Lean proof agent, solved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems, two unsolved for 56 years, in a peer-reviewed Science paper.**

Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus has been published in the journal Science, according to The Decoder, which reported on Monday that the system solved nine open Erdős problems, two of which had gone unsolved for 56 years. DeepMind attempted 353 of the problems, so the success rate is about 2.5 percent.

The system also proved 44 conjectures from the OEIS integer-sequence database, settled an open question in algebraic geometry and improved a known bound in min-max optimization, The Decoder says. An earlier Decoder report from May, on the preprint, put the OEIS attempt at 492 conjectures and described the algebraic-geometry question as 15 years old.

Per The Decoder, Nexus is built on Gemini 3.1 Pro, which drafts and revises proofs in the formal language Lean. Lean checks every draft after each edit, and its error messages steer the next attempt. For harder sub-problems the agent can call AlphaProof. DeepMind reports that it solved the nine Erdős problems even without AlphaProof, and the May report said a simpler variant could also manage all nine.

DeepMind has put the Lean proofs and prose versions in a public repository, google-deepmind/alphaproof-nexus-results, under Apache-2.0 and CC-BY 4.0. The repository's README says anyone can verify the proofs by building the project with Lean's lake tool. At the time of writing it showed 312 stars. The README states it is not an official Google product.

The Decoder sets the paper against OpenAI, which posted hundreds of AI-written proofs on GitHub without journal review. The Decoder reports that the Association for Human Mathematics, which it says is led by Terence Tao, called for a boycott. The May report adds caveats: successes cluster where the Mathlib library is mature, and most Erdős problems stayed out of reach. We have not independently read the Science paper.

## Microsoft's Brad Smith says a washing machine faces more safety rules than AI

**Microsoft president Brad Smith told ITV that AI is less regulated than a carton of milk, and that the UK could lead on rules Washington has not written.**

Microsoft president Brad Smith said in an ITV interview that AI is subject to fewer safety rules than everyday goods, Engadget reported on Monday. "Right now, your washing machine is subject to more safety rules than AI," Smith said, adding that every carton of milk in a shop is subject to safety rules and inspection.

Smith argued that the lack of US action leaves the UK an opening to lead. According to Engadget, he said Washington does not view London as it views Brussels. He called that an advantage and said he hopes Microsoft and the UK government can take on the role.

On the most powerful models, those that could put global cybersecurity at risk, Smith said "the moment has arrived" for action. Engadget gives no detail of what rules he wants. We could not view the interview, so these remarks rest on that one report.

Engadget sets the remarks against a split in the industry. It says Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind have reportedly voiced support for slowing development. President Trump has said the US industry should accelerate to stay ahead of China and favours self-regulation. Engadget also describes a summit pact, signed by industry leaders, that calls for self-regulation and independent auditors. Critics cited by the BBC call it a distraction.
