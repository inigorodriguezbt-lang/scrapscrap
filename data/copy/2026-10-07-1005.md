## Common Sense Media calls ChatGPT for Teens an 'unacceptable risk'

**Testers set off parental alerts only four times in about 1,440 prompts, including hour-long talks on suicide and self-harm, Common Sense Media says. OpenAI disputes the method.**

Common Sense Media, a US nonprofit that reviews apps and services with a focus on youth safety, said on 7 October that OpenAI's ChatGPT for Teens is an "unacceptable risk", The Verge and Bloomberg reported. OpenAI introduced the teen version in August as the default way for 13 to 17-year-olds to use ChatGPT. The group's Youth AI Safety Institute ran the tests.

According to Futurism's account of the report, parental alerts fired just four times across more than 1,440 test prompts. Testers talked about suicide, self-harm and eating disorders for up to an hour on accounts linked to a parent. Another write-up, by Mezha, says the system missed more than a quarter of the cases in which testers thought a teen should be pointed to a crisis service.

Study mode, the feature meant to keep teens learning rather than copying, also fell short, Futurism reports. Testers say a "show me the answer" button gave homework answers during blocked study hours, and teens could switch the mode off despite parental limits. The group also says age prediction proved unreliable and that the chatbot still used language implying it is conscious.

OpenAI disputed the method. Futurism quotes the company as saying "the bulk of their testing may have begun and concluded before activation of parental controls was complete", and that alerts can take several hours to start on newly linked accounts. Mezha reports OpenAI also said age detection can take up to two weeks and called the study-mode bypass an intended flexibility.

Common Sense Media replied that the new information does not change its conclusion that parental alerts are unreliable in a crisis, Futurism reports. It urges OpenAI to market ChatGPT as an adults-only product until the guardrails work consistently. The paper has not seen the full report, and the test figures differ between the outlets that have.
