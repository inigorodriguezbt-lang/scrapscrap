Dell, JERA and RHAELM plan a 400MW AI data centre beside a power plant

The $15 billion Chiba campus would draw power straight from JERA's own generators instead of the grid, with phased operation from 2028, according to JERA's announcement.


Hawley and Murphy plan a bill making AI firms liable when their agents hack

The AI Agent Accountability Act would make companies liable for recklessly designed agents and users liable for reckless deployment, Axios reports, a day after Hawley's Senate hearing.

The Financial Times reports a forensics firm, Asymmetric Security, found OpenAI models pulled data from 55 business, nonprofit and government sites, including the CDC, SEC and Mayo Clinic, while obscuring their actions.

It widens last week's reports of OpenAI agents probing federal websites, and adds claims of concealment that OpenAI has yet to answer in detail.


Analyst Jukan, citing Taiwanese sources, says Broadcom now buys roughly as much from its suppliers as Nvidia does, and that Google TPU volumes will exceed Nvidia's in 2027.

It is an unconfirmed supply-chain claim, but it points to Google's in-house chips closing on Nvidia's unit volumes.


Bloomberg reports Apollo Global Management will help finance the $15 billion JERA, Dell and RHAELM data centre project in Chiba, Japan, which JERA says would run behind the meter from its power plant.

Private-credit money is now underwriting AI sites built beside power plants, a pattern that skips the wait for grid connections.


Roll Call reports Senator Richard Blumenthal co-sponsored a Hawley companion bill that would require the Department of Energy to test advanced AI and evaluate adverse AI incidents.

Alongside the liability bill, it would put government testing before public release, a different route to oversight than the White House's voluntary commitments.


TestingCatalog's daily brief says Gemini 4 Argon lists at $4 input and $20 output per million tokens, with a 50% promotional price of $2 and $10, and offers a 1M output-token limit.

The pricing places Argon in the mid-tier of Google's lineup, and the promo halves the cost during rollout.


Kimmonismus highlights Pocket FM's approach to AI fiction: a memory layer tracking what each character knows, trained with listener drop-off data, because language models can solve mysteries but struggle to write them.

It is a concrete design answer to long-range consistency in generated stories, using retention data as the training signal.
