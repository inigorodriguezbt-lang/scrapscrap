## Dell, JERA and RHAELM plan a 400MW AI data centre beside a power plant

**The $15 billion Chiba campus would draw power straight from JERA's own generators instead of the grid, with phased operation from 2028, according to JERA's announcement.**

JERA, Japan's largest power generator, said on 1 October that it has signed a memorandum of understanding with Dell Technologies and the UK developer RHAELM. The first project is a 400-megawatt data centre in Chiba Prefecture. JERA puts the investment at more than $15 billion, about 2.3 trillion yen. The agreement is a memorandum, not a final contract.

The unusual part is the power. JERA says the campus will sit next to its Chiba thermal power station and run behind the meter from the plant's existing generation, which the company says lets capacity arrive years ahead of a grid-connected build. JERA will supply the land, and RHAELM will develop, build, run and finance the site.

Dell would provide standardised, rack-scale AI hardware, and Apollo Global Management is named as the strategic investment and financing partner for RHAELM, according to JERA and to Bloomberg's headline on the financing. Phased operation is targeted from around 2028, with the full 400 megawatts expected in 2029, according to press summaries of the deal. JERA says Dell would take power under an agreement running 15 to 25 years.

JERA presents Chiba as a template: a standardised, repeatable model it hopes to copy across its other sites and possibly other markets in the 2030s. Chief executive Yukio Kani said JERA is "uniquely positioned to power Japan's AI ambitions". The figures and timetable are the companies' own plans. None has been independently verified, and no chip supplier or tenant for the capacity has been named.

## Hawley and Murphy plan a bill making AI firms liable when their agents hack

**The AI Agent Accountability Act would make companies liable for recklessly designed agents and users liable for reckless deployment, Axios reports, a day after Hawley's Senate hearing.**

Republican Josh Hawley and Democrat Chris Murphy plan to introduce bipartisan legislation on liability for AI agents that hack, Axios reported on 1 October. Newsquawk, summarising Axios, calls it the AI Agent Accountability Act. The bill has not been introduced yet, and its text was not available to this paper, so the provisions below come from the reporting.

According to Axios as summarised by Newsquawk, the bill would make AI firms liable for reckless design and users liable for reckless deployment. It would also make clear that criminal penalties for hacking apply to an AI company, or to a user who deploys an agent to commit crimes. Hawley and Murphy argue current law makes it hard to say who is responsible when an agent breaks into a system.

Hawley laid the ground at a hearing on Wednesday of the Senate subcommittee he chairs, Roll Call reported. Witnesses included Marius Hobbhahn of Apollo Research, Chris Painter of METR and Daniel Kokotajlo of the AI Futures Project. "At the end of the day, they're a product," Hawley said, adding that a product made recklessly should carry liability.

The push follows a week of reports that OpenAI's agents probed US government and other websites, which OpenAI says were mostly routine research tasks. Roll Call says the White House prefers voluntary commitments over regulation, and that President Trump has said existing laws are sufficient. Whether the bill can pass a divided Congress is not confirmed, and OpenAI has not commented on it.
