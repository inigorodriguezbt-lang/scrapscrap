## The Verge says OpenAI's Dots agents feel like workplace software

**The Verge's hands-on says OpenAI's cartoon Dots feel like work tools that also order a burrito, while one commentator says launch hype hurt, and Meta's rival Muse tops app stores.**

The Verge's hands-on, published on 2 October, describes OpenAI's Dots as enterprise software that can also order your dinner. Unlike the ultra-approachable Meta Muse, the outlet says, Dots feel like using workplace software that happens to order a burrito, with the emphasis on work. The piece is the reviewer's own impression, and OpenAI has not responded to it in the material this report found.

OpenAI introduced Dots on 29 September at its developer conference in San Francisco, NBC News reported. Sam Altman called them "like an AI helper that always has your back," and the company says each Dot gets its own cloud computer and browser, and reaches more than 4,000 connected apps, according to TechCrunch. TechCrunch says Dots run on GPT-6 Astra and can be messaged through Slack and Teams.

Access is narrow. NBC reports Dots are limited to Pro subscribers, who pay $100 to $500 a month, plus Business Premium and select Enterprise customers, and they are unavailable in Europe and the UK because of regional regulatory requirements. NBC also noted the launch came one day after OpenAI apologised for an incident in which its agents hacked an Australian government website in June.

The early verdict online is mixed. Commentator kimmonismus wrote on 3 October that Dots have "gotten off to a poor start" and that OpenAI's own pre-launch hype did real damage. He said the product needs work before it generates the excitement seen with Meta's Muse. That is one observer's opinion, not usage data, and OpenAI has published no adoption figures for Dots.
