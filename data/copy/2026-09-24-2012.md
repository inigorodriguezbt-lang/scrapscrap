## Black Forest Labs posts open robotics model, says it beats larger rivals

**The 7-billion-parameter FLUX 3 Action takes camera frames and a text instruction and returns a robot's next actions, under a licence the company wrote itself.**

Black Forest Labs released FLUX 3 Action on Thursday, a 7-billion-parameter model for robots that the company says sets record success rates on the RoboLab-120 leaderboard. The weights are on Hugging Face under the FLUX Kommunity License v.1.0, and the collection carries two policies and a shared base.

The model card describes what the model does in plain terms. It takes camera frames, the robot's state and a text instruction, and returns the next chunk of actions. The SO-101 policy has 34 likes and 206 downloads in the past month; the DROID policy has 21 likes and 121 downloads.

The performance claims are the company's own. Black Forest Labs said the model is less than half the size of the previous best open model on RoboLab-120 and runs up to 3.95 times faster, according to The Decoder's report of the launch. No independent test of either figure has been published yet.

The setup in the model card is narrower than the leaderboard claim may suggest. The SO-101 policy reads two cameras, one on the scene and one on the wrist, and takes six state and action dimensions. It predicts 42 actions, executes 32 of them at 30 Hz, then replans.

Black Forest Labs is known for image generation, and this is its first robotics release. The company said large reasoning models plan well but robotics needs efficiency for work on the device itself, and pointed to digital uses such as testing navigation and computer-control agents.

## Google gives Gemini 3.8 Live a face, for enterprise customers only

**Live Avatar generates a lip-syncing animated persona while the model talks, in 97 languages, and Google is holding it to Gemini Enterprise at launch.**

Google announced Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar on Wednesday, adding a generated face to the live dialogue model it shipped the week before. The feature reaches customers through Gemini Enterprise and the Google Cloud console, and not through the consumer Gemini app.

Google said the avatar delivers precise lip-syncing, natural expressions and fluid turn-taking, and that its speech-to-speech synchronisation is native across 97 languages, with the lip-sync adapting as the language changes. The wording is the company's own. It published no latency figure beyond calling the system near real-time.

Organisations may generate a custom avatar from a reference image, which Google said preserves reference likeness, brand styling or character identity. That path is currently available only through enterprise allowlisting, according to the announcement, and Google did not say when or whether it opens more widely.

Every output carries SynthID, which Google describes as an imperceptible watermark embedded in the audio and video so generated material can be identified. The model can also run tool calls in the background while the conversation continues, the announcement said. Google listed no limitations and no pricing.

## Perplexity adds Fast Search to its API, says most results land in 230ms

**A new Rust retrieval engine called Photon serves 95 per cent of results in 230 milliseconds or less, according to the company, which has published no comparison.**

Perplexity said on Wednesday it had added Fast Search to its Search API, and that the option returns 95 per cent of results in 230 milliseconds or less. The company's API documentation now lists a fast search type, described there as lower-latency, lower-cost web search.

Both figures come from Perplexity. Aravind Srinivas, the chief executive, put the number differently, saying the engine reaches sub-250ms latency at the 95th percentile while still scoring well on relevance and accuracy benchmarks. Neither he nor the company named those benchmarks or published the comparison.

The engine is called Photon, which Perplexity describes as a Rust-based retrieval and ranking service. The company said a small team of engineers built it with hundreds of agents. Srinivas said those agents were auto-research loops run by a more capable internal version of Perplexity Computer.

The two accounts differ on the latency. Perplexity's post gives 230 milliseconds for 95 per cent of results. Srinivas cites sub-250ms at the 95th percentile. The documentation carries neither number, and lists no price for the fast tier. Nobody outside Perplexity has measured either claim.

## GitHub opens an agent that writes fuzz harnesses and triages the crashes

**The Security Lab's new fuzzing taskflow runs AFL++ against C and C++ projects without a person in the loop, and defaults to Claude Sonnet 5.**

GitHub published a fuzzing taskflow for its Security Lab Taskflow Agent on Thursday. According to the post, the pipeline finds entry points in C and C++ code, writes the fuzz harnesses, runs AFL++, reads the coverage report and triages the crashes, with no person in the loop while it works.

The code is public, at GitHubSecurityLab/seclab-taskflows-fuzzing. The post is by Antonio Morales of the Security Lab. He says the default model is Claude Sonnet 5, which he picked because it passed every one of the team's internal tests, and that a configuration file may point the taskflow at another model.

Morales says to run the taskflow only inside a disposable environment, such as a Codespace or a throwaway virtual machine, and without elevated privileges. The reason he gives is that the agent could run arbitrary build commands, and the binaries it produces, directly on the host.

Getting started takes a single command against a repository, which the post demonstrates on the cJSON library. The agent marks the patches it proposes review required, and Morales says a person has to verify them. The post reports no bug counts from live campaigns, so how well it works in the field is untested here.

## PrismML shows a 1-bit 2B model running on Qualcomm's smart-glasses chip

**The Caltech-founded startup says its compressed Bonsai model is a quarter of the size and keeps nearly all its benchmark scores, though no glasses ship with it yet.**

PrismML demonstrated a 1-bit version of its 2-billion-parameter Bonsai model running locally on Qualcomm's Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 platform, the chip inside several smart-glasses designs. The demonstration took place at Qualcomm's Snapdragon Summit on Wednesday, TechCrunch reported on Thursday. No other outlet has the story yet.

The compression claim is the company's. PrismML said the 1-bit build is a quarter of the size of the original and retains nearly all of its performance on benchmarks. It did not name those benchmarks, and TechCrunch's report carries no measurement of its own.

The model is tuned for vision and language tasks, which is what glasses ask of a model: read the scene, answer a question about it. Caltech researchers founded PrismML, and Ion Stoica of the University of California, Berkeley, advises it, according to TechCrunch.

No product exists yet. TechCrunch said no smart glasses carry PrismML's models so far, and gave the company's stated goal as open-weight AI that runs on devices and makes better use of the computing power those devices already have.

## Google Research shows a 10-minute video from a multi-agent pipeline

**Four components handle storyboarding, generation and critique, and Google says the system holds characters and places steady across a video far longer than a clip.**

Google Research published work on Thursday on a multi-agent system for long-form video, together with a 10-minute continuous video it says the system generated with visual consistency held throughout. The post is by Yale Song and Yiwen Song, research scientists at Google.

The system has four named parts, according to the post. AI Video Co-Director treats storytelling as a global optimisation and uses multi-armed bandit algorithms to choose among creative directions. CANVAS keeps a persistent visual memory of characters and of the state of objects, so transitions inside a setting hold rather than drift.

A²RD turns storyboards into minute-long video through a loop that retrieves, then synthesises and refines, switching between extrapolation and interpolation as it goes. VQQA generates visual questions about the output and feeds a vision-language model's critiques back as what Google calls semantic gradients for prompt optimisation.

The scores are Google's own. It reports 81.4 on GenAD-Bench for the Co-Director, continuity gains for CANVAS on HardContinuityBench, and improvements on T2V-CompBench and VBench2 from VQQA. The papers are on arXiv and the 10-minute video is on YouTube. No code or public demo appears to be offered.

## White House asks OpenAI, Anthropic to hold models from UK testers

**Politico reports that a senior official wants US review to come first, and that Anthropic appears to have complied while OpenAI has not said what it will do.**

The White House has asked OpenAI and Anthropic not to share new models with the UK government's testing agency until those models have been through US testing, Politico reported on Thursday. Sophia Cai, who wrote the story with Joe Bambridge, set out its findings on X.

Cai quoted a senior administration official on the reason. "Because they're American companies and this has been our policy with every new frontier model that comes out," the official told her. The official is not named in her account, and the exchange is the only quotation she gives.

The sequence the White House wants runs US review, then securing US systems, then sharing the model with US partners, according to Cai. Anthropic appears to have complied, she wrote. OpenAI has not said whether it will, and neither company has commented publicly on the request.

Samuel Hammond, a policy writer who reads on the US side of this argument, said he was not opposed in principle, but that the US institute should then be resourced at least as well as the UK's. No other outlet has matched the story so far.

## Oracle sends force majeure notice on its New Mexico Stargate site

**A gas pipeline to Project Jupiter slipped by nearly six months after permit denials, and Oracle says the campus is still on its planned schedule.**

Oracle has sent a force majeure notice to Blue Owl Capital over Project Jupiter, the 2.45-gigawatt Stargate data centre campus in New Mexico. Bloomberg had the story first and TechCrunch wrote it up on Thursday, reporting that the notice would let Oracle delay payments if the site misses its 2028 target.

The trigger is power. An Energy Transfer gas pipeline meant to fuel the site slipped by nearly six months, to 1 February 2027, after repeated permit denials, according to TechCrunch. An air-quality permit for the fuel cell system is still pending, with a state decision due by 23 November.

Oracle said Project Jupiter remains on its planned schedule and that the company is fully committed to New Mexico. TechCrunch reported that the notice is not an exit, and that it protects Oracle from payment obligations should the construction run late.

Stargate is the data centre programme Oracle runs with OpenAI and SoftBank, and Oracle is the main tenant at Jupiter. Blue Owl Capital is the developer and Bloom Energy supplies the fuel cells. Oracle did not put a figure on what a delay could cost, and TechCrunch reported none.
