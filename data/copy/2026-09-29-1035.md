## Anthropic's seven co-founders would keep 50.1% of votes after IPO

**A draft IPO filing reviewed by Reuters gives Anthropic's seven co-founders one Class F share carrying 50.1% of the vote, held through a new Founder LLC.**

Anthropic's seven co-founders will hold 50.1% of the company's voting power after it lists, through a new entity called the Founder LLC, according to a draft IPO filing that Reuters says it reviewed. Anthropic declined to comment to Reuters, and the filing has not been published in final form.

Under the plan as reported, the co-founders decide by majority vote how to cast one Class F share. It carries 50.1% of the vote on major decisions, including the selection of some board members. Publicly sold Class A stock carries one vote per share, and strategic partners' shares carry minimal voting power, according to The Next Web.

The filing describes the founders as people "distinctly equipped to be stewards of our mission". The group includes chief executive Dario Amodei, his sister and president Daniela Amodei, and Tom Brown, along with Chris Olah. Membership ends on departure, death, excessive share sales or termination for cause, according to the reported filing.

Anthropic would remain a Delaware public benefit corporation, and the filing warns that its decisions may conflict with shareholders' financial interests and could harm the value of Class A stock, according to The Next Web. All seven co-founders have pledged 80% of their Anthropic stakes to charity, the reports say.

The board would include Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown and one unnamed director elected by Class F and Class A holders. Four more seats go to the Long-Term Benefit Trust, which includes Ben Bernanke. Reported 2025 pay was about $18 million for Dario Amodei and $16.4 million for Daniela Amodei.

## Pope Leo says AI risk warnings are not 'fake news', criticises Nvidia's Huang

**On the papal flight from Metz to Rome, Pope Leo XIV said AI experts' concerns "should be taken seriously" and questioned Jensen Huang, who he said opposes limits and regulation.**

Pope Leo XIV said on Monday that concerns about AI destroying the world are not "fake news" and should be taken seriously, Reuters reported. He spoke to reporters aboard the papal plane as he left France at the end of a four-day visit, in what several outlets read as a rebuke of President Donald Trump.

https://x.com/Reuters/status/2104854946642329627

"I do think that the concerns raised by many of the experts, specialists in AI, should be taken seriously," the pope said, according to Al Jazeera. "I don't think that that is fake news, as some have said, to try and cause whether financial or some other kind of benefit." Trump has called AI safety fears a "hoax".

The pope also named Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang. He said he had read that day that Huang had announced a way to insert guardrails into certain AI models. But Huang is "the same one, however, that says there should be no limits placed and no government regulation", the pope said, according to the reports.

Huang told CBS News this month that he agreed with Trump that AI safety concerns were overblown, the reports say. Nvidia launched an agent safety platform on Monday that it says can contain rogue AI agents within milliseconds. Nvidia has not commented on the pope's remarks in the coverage reviewed.

Leo has made AI a signature issue. Al Jazeera reports he launched an encyclical on the subject in May alongside Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah. He told reporters the world cannot "just sit back and pretend nothing's going to happen", and urged leaders to promote dialogue and study.

## Meta gives its Muse agent a small-business version wired into Shopify and Stripe

**Meta's Muse for Small Business links its AI agent to 15 work apps plus Instagram, Facebook and ad accounts, free for most features in the US and Canada.**

Meta on Tuesday launched Muse for Small Business, a version of its Muse personal AI agent aimed at owners running a shop or service, according to Meta's announcement. CNBC reported it as a push by Mark Zuckerberg beyond the consumer market, a day after Meta announced a separate enterprise platform.

Owners can connect Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe and Zoom, Meta says. It also links Instagram professional accounts, Facebook Pages and Meta ad accounts. Custom connectors are supported for services not on the list.

Meta lists uses including annual growth planning from sales and campaign data, prioritising email and calendar, analysing ad and content performance and drafting campaigns, and a monthly financial review. Meta says the agent flags emails that need replies and drafts content, and that it asks for approval before publishing, sending or spending.

Availability is limited to the US and Canada. Meta says most features are free, with subscription plans on top, and did not give pricing details, according to CNBC. Quotes in the announcement come from a grocery owner and Canva co-founder Cameron Adams, so no independent test of the agent has been published.

The launch follows an earlier report that Muse botched a sale and apologised on a user's behalf. Meta's own examples describe a supervised agent, and how often owners will approve its drafts unedited is not yet known. Nobody outside Meta has published usage figures for the small-business version.

## Google sues EU over orders opening Android's Gemini features to rivals

**Google filed two suits in Luxembourg on Monday against EU orders to give rival AI assistants the same 11 Android features as Gemini by August 2027 and to share anonymised search data from January 2027.**

Google filed two lawsuits at the EU General Court in Luxembourg on Monday, challenging orders the European Commission issued in July under the Digital Markets Act, according to The Next Web. One covers Android access for rival AI assistants, the other covers sharing search data with competitors.

The Android order requires Google to give rival assistants access by August 2027 to 11 features now exclusive to Gemini, including voice activation and the ability to act inside apps, according to the report. The search order requires anonymised query data to be shared with eligible search engines and AI chatbots from January 2027.

Oliver Bethell, Google's senior director of competition, said in a statement: "Mandating we share these personal queries without adequate safeguards would cause irreversible harm to user privacy." Google also argues the orders could weaken Android security. It says Android is already open and interoperable.

The Commission maintains that both decisions protect privacy and device security, according to the report. It says the anonymisation measures were developed with privacy experts. Google can refuse access to companies that pose a genuine threat. DuckDuckGo backed the EU, saying legal challenges only delay implementation.

Filing a suit does not automatically suspend the decisions, so the 2027 deadlines stand unless the court intervenes. The orders do not decide whether Google breached the Digital Markets Act and carry no fines, and both remain open to judicial review, according to the reports.
