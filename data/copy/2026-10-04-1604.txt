Commentator kimmonismus wrote on X that GPT-6.1 Sol feels slow compared with Opus 5.5 and a little lazy, needing prompts to take action, while he finds Astra more eager. It is one user's impression.

Early impressions of a new model often diverge from benchmark results, and this one shows how heavy users are comparing the two labs' latest releases.


AlphaXiv highlights a paper, Finetuning with Sampling, which uses MCMC to rewrite expert traces into answers closer to the base model's own style. The authors say plain SFT then beats RL while forgetting less.

If it holds up, it would make plain supervised fine-tuning, which is cheaper than reinforcement learning, a stronger option for post-training models.


Stephen Wolfram published an essay on the future of pure mathematics research as AI automates parts of it, arguing that formalisation is hard and that human imagination still decides which questions to ask.

It is a mathematician-builder's view on where automated proof tools stop, from the creator of Wolfram Language and Mathematica.


The Wall Street Journal profiles Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang, the company's first Gen Z senior executive, saying he has succeeded in building hype for Muse. This paper has not read the full piece.

Meta's Muse model family and its pocket AI devices have become the company's main AI bet, and Wang is the executive fronting them.
