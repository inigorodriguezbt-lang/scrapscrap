## Grok Bots can now be used by passengers in Tesla's Cybercab

**X user Sawyer Merritt says he added laundry detergent to an Amazon cart by voice in a Cybercab, using a Grok Bot that signs out when he leaves.**

Passengers can now use Grok Bots inside a Tesla Cybercab, according to a post on X by Sawyer Merritt that had about 800 likes when read on Monday morning. Merritt wrote that he tried it on a ride on Sunday night. The post includes a video of the feature.

https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2109490463635914867

Merritt says a rider signs into Grok once, after which their Bots are available on all future rides and are logged out automatically when they leave the vehicle, to protect privacy. He says the next ride signs the passenger back into apps they used before.

His test was adding laundry detergent to an Amazon cart by voice. He says the Bots can also send emails, place a coffee order and manage a calendar, finances or other tasks. These are Merritt's descriptions of what he tried or was told; Tesla has not published a feature list in anything seen for this story.

Elon Musk replied to the news on X with the words "Try Grok @Bot", which drew about 1,800 likes. Neither post says where the feature is live, which riders have it, or whether it is limited to a pilot group. No second rider has described it yet.

The claim rests on one rider's account and one clip. Tesla has not published a release note that the paper could find, and the privacy behaviour Merritt describes, the automatic sign-out at the end of a ride, has not been independently tested.

## Amazon in talks to buy world-model startup Decart for about $7 billion

**The Wall Street Journal reports Amazon is in talks to buy Decart for around $7 billion, after Anthropic reportedly walked away from a $6 billion deal.**

Amazon is in talks to buy the AI startup Decart for around $7 billion, the Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday, according to a post relaying the report on X by the account Negligible Cap. The account added that Anthropic was said to have walked away from a $6 billion Decart acquisition. The reasons were not specified.

Negligible Cap speculated that Decart may have wanted more money. That is the account's guess, not reporting. The Journal's article itself was not available to this paper, so both figures rest on a secondhand summary.

Another X user, himanshustwts, says Anthropic cancelled during due diligence and describes Decart as a lab that builds world and video models. The same post says the company was valued at about $4 billion a quarter ago. That valuation was not confirmed by any source this paper opened.

Earlier reports, including one from Bloomberg in August, had Anthropic pursuing Decart at about $6 billion, and a later report said the deal was nearing signature at around $7 billion. Those reports named Nvidia as an existing investor. Neither Anthropic, Amazon nor Decart is quoted in anything seen for this story.

The reported turnaround is unconfirmed. Nothing seen here shows a signed agreement with Amazon, and the earlier accounts of an Anthropic deal conflict with the new one. Any of the parties could comment, and the terms could change.

## US restores UK safety institute access to unreleased frontier AI models

**The White House has reportedly reversed a decision that cut the UK's AI Security Institute off from unreleased models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google.**

The White House has reinstated the UK's access to frontier AI models for safety testing after lobbying by British ministers, according to an exclusive posted on X by the journalist Tony Diver on Sunday. The US had earlier decided to restrict access to the latest models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google.

Diver reports that the restriction was meant to stop the UK's AI Safety Institute from testing models before release. He says AI minister Kanishka Narayan was involved in the lobbying, and that ministers feared Britain's attempt to lead on AI regulation would otherwise be "dead in the water".

Another X user, Seb Johnson, says the change means the AI Security Institute can again reach the latest US models before launch and run its safety tests on them. That is his reading of Diver's report, not a separate confirmation.

No US or UK government statement was seen for this story, and the post does not say when the earlier restriction began or what conditions come with the restored access. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google are not quoted.

The report rests on a single journalist's exclusive. The paper has not seen the linked article, and cannot say which agency made either decision or whether the access covers every lab.

## Moonshot executives in talks to settle shareholder dispute before IPO

**Reuters reports top executives at Kimi maker Moonshot AI are negotiating a settlement with a shareholder demanding compensation for alleged wrongdoing.**

Top executives of the Chinese AI startup Moonshot are in talks to settle a legal dispute with a shareholder who is demanding compensation for their alleged wrongdoing, Reuters reported in an exclusive on Sunday. The talks come ahead of the company's planned Hong Kong listing.

Reuters posted the report on X in two items, one labelled "EXCLUSIVE" and citing "people" as its sources. The posts seen by this paper do not name the shareholder, the executives or the alleged wrongdoing, and give no settlement figure.

A headline from the news aggregator HuggingNews put the figure at $700 million. This paper could not confirm that number from Reuters' posts and does not rely on it.

Reuters reported in September that Moonshot had filed confidentially for a Hong Kong listing, aiming to raise about $3 billion. Moonshot has also faced US allegations about chips and distillation that it denies. Those earlier reports are background, and none of them mention the dispute.

Moonshot has not commented in anything seen for this story. Reuters' full article was not opened, so the details above are limited to what its posts say. No settlement has been announced, and talks may not conclude.
