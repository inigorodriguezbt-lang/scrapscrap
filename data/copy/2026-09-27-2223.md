## Fireworks says Ember-1 cuts reasoning tokens 40%

**Fireworks Research built Ember-1 on Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 to reason in fewer tokens, and the release topped Hacker News with 263 points hours after it shipped.**

A Hacker News post about Fireworks Research's Ember-1 model reached 263 points and 145 comments Sunday, five days after the company released it. Fireworks built Ember-1 by post-training Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 to produce shorter reasoning traces. The company said the model uses roughly 40% fewer tokens while matching K3's quality.

Fireworks said Ember-1 scored 82.0% on Terminal Bench 2.1 and 75.2% on DeepSWE 1.1. Kimi K3 scored 80.9% and 66.4% on the same two benchmarks, the company said. Fireworks said it ran more than 50 training experiments and 200 evaluations, using new training algorithms built on its own serverless training platform.

Ember-1 is available now through Fireworks' serverless API as a free research preview for two weeks, the company said. After that it plans to charge $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. The model carries a 1.04-million-token context window and supports function calling and image input, its model card says.

The token-count and benchmark figures are Fireworks' own, measured on evaluations it selected. They have not been independently verified. Dmytro Dzhulgakov, the company's co-founder and chief technology officer, said on X that the HN reception caught his team off guard, writing that Ember-1 was "hot on HN, our research team cooked."

## Claude Sonnet 5.5 may ship within days, TestingCatalog says

**TestingCatalog cited leaked screenshots and app strings pointing to a Claude Sonnet 5.5 release as soon as next week, with Anthropic yet to confirm the model or its reported specs.**

TestingCatalog reported that Claude Sonnet 5.5 could ship soon, citing leaked screenshots rather than any Anthropic announcement. One leak the outlet cited points to a release "as early as early next week." Anthropic has not confirmed a release date or the model's existence.

TestingCatalog said the leaks include screenshots from two accounts it named, Lyra and LuminaBench, and a listing on the Factory platform that already shows the model marked unavailable. It also cited a video said to compare Sonnet 5.5 against GPT-6 Sol.

The leaked material points to a context window of 872,000 to 1 million tokens and a maximum output of 128,000 tokens, TestingCatalog said. It said thinking would be "adaptive by default," disabled only at low, medium or high settings, and that the model drops forced tool use, a feature in earlier Claude releases.

Alleged pricing in the leaks put input tokens at $2 per million, output at $10 per million and cached reads at $0.20 per million. TestingCatalog said the figures were preliminary and could change. No other outlet had corroborated the report, and Anthropic did not respond to a request for comment.

## Thoughtful Things launches Engram, an AI hallucination sampler

**The startup opened a Kickstarter campaign for Engram, a hardware sampler that uses AI to mangle audio and generate new sounds rather than complete songs, The Verge reported.**

Thoughtful Things opened a Kickstarter campaign Sunday for Engram, its first hardware instrument, The Verge reported. The company describes it as a sampler and groovebox that uses a small onboard AI model to mangle recorded audio and generate new sounds from it, without any internet connection or app.

The company calls the technique "model bending": tuning, and deliberately disrupting, the tiny onboard model to push it toward glitches and hallucinated sounds rather than clean output. Thoughtful Things says the model runs entirely on the device's own hardware, and according to the company was designed and trained in-house, with no cloud service involved.

The Verge said Engram is not meant as a "push-button, get-song" device like AI music generators such as Suno. Thoughtful Things describes the tiny models as a deliberate alternative to large-scale AI systems, and says on its site that they train on standard gaming PCs rather than data centers.

Thoughtful Things' listing describes a limited run of discounted units for early Kickstarter backers. It does not state a price, funding goal or ship date on the page as of Sunday. Neither the company nor The Verge's report specified how many units had been pledged.

## US creative-industry jobs fall 200,000 since 2020, analyst says

**Economist Joey Politano said US creative-industry jobs have fallen by more than 200,000 in four years, the worst stretch outside a recession, and asked how much AI is to blame.**

Economist Joey Politano said Sunday that the US has lost more than 200,000 jobs in creative fields such as publishing, film, TV and graphic design over the past four years. Politano writes the Apricitas Economics newsletter. He called it the worst stretch for arts employment outside a major recession.

https://x.com/JosephPolitano/status/2104282420631777488

Politano posted the figures in a thread asking "how much is AI to blame" for the decline. He did not answer the question himself in the portion of the thread reviewed for this report, and did not cite a specific data source for the job losses in the post.

Stefan Schubert, another newsletter writer, replied to the post. He noted the decline works out to a 20% fall in creative-sector jobs since 2020, "regardless of cause." No news outlet or government agency had independently confirmed Politano's figures as of Sunday evening.

The post did not name which government data series it draws on. Politano said the sector's business model, not only AI, was in question. The claim rests on Politano's own reading of the numbers, not on a published dataset this report could check directly.
