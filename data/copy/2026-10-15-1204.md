## Anthropic tells investors distillation brings in 5 to 7 percent of revenue

**The Decoder, citing The Information and the Financial Times, says Anthropic expects that income to fall, and that some investors now see an IPO valuation under $1.5 trillion.**

Anthropic estimates that 5 to 7 percent of its revenue comes from distillation, where other companies use its models to train their own, according to The Decoder. The outlet cites The Information and the Financial Times, which in turn cite investor meetings in San Francisco. Anthropic has not published these figures, and this paper has not seen the investor materials.

The Decoder reports that Anthropic told investors the distillation income is likely to fall, weighing on the current quarter. The company is said to expect that blocking the practice will help in the long run, because it would make it harder for rivals to match the quality of Claude. How Anthropic would block it was not described in the report.

The same article puts third-quarter revenue at $16.8 billion, up roughly 45 percent from the second quarter, against a rise of more than 140 percent the quarter before. The Decoder attributes part of the slowdown to a summer lull and to OpenAI's September launch of Astra, and says Anthropic answered in late September with Opus 5.5.

On profit, The Decoder says The Information reports an adjusted operating margin of 11 percent for the year through the third quarter, excluding costs such as stock-based compensation. It says the Financial Times calls this the second straight quarter of adjusted profit, and that Anthropic is said to be cash-flow positive. Anthropic earned $1.70 in revenue for each dollar spent on compute.

The listing is planned for November, before Thanksgiving, per The Information. Anthropic's private valuation was $965 billion in May, and some bankers had floated $2 trillion for the IPO. The Decoder says some investors found the numbers disappointing and expect advisers to lower the target, possibly under $1.5 trillion. Anthropic has not commented in anything seen here, and the valuation talk is relayed from others.
