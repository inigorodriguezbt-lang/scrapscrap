Anthropic signs $11.6 billion cloud deal with Akamai

The seven-year contract includes warrants for up to 5% of Akamai's stock and pushes Anthropic's total compute commitments past $500 billion in under a year.

Anthropic agreed to pay Akamai $11.6 billion over seven years for cloud capacity, Reuters reported, pushing the AI lab's total compute commitments to roughly $517 billion in under a year.

The deal, reported by The Decoder, includes warrants letting Anthropic acquire up to 5% of Akamai's stock — 2% tied to the current contract, and another 3% if the agreement grows by as much as $9 billion more. Akamai's shares jumped 22% in after-hours trading on the news.

Akamai expects roughly $5.5 billion in capital costs plus another $1.7 billion in spending before the end of 2026 to build out the capacity, according to the report. Anthropic previously committed $45 billion to cloud provider Nscale for a similar buildout.

On X, an account tracking the deal, mark_k, said the agreement is unusual because it covers CPU capacity rather than GPUs. That is the ordinary compute a frontier lab needs to run the systems around its models, not to train them. Neither company has confirmed the detail.

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei has warned the company faces bankruptcy risk if its revenue projections miss, while OpenAI's Sam Altman has separately cautioned against what he called "unsustainable silliness in compute buildouts."

White House tells AI labs to vet models before UK sharing

Anthropic has restricted its newest model to U.S. organizations after the request, while the UK's safety institute says it still gets early access to frontier models.

The White House has told OpenAI and Anthropic to let U.S. agencies review new AI models before sharing them with the UK's AI Safety Institute, The Decoder reported, citing the administration's Office of the National Cyber Director.

Anthropic has complied, restricting access to its Claude Mythos 5.1 model to U.S. organizations only, according to the outlet. The report did not detail how OpenAI responded to the same request.

UK AISI director Henry de Zoete said the institute still has access to frontier models and had tested OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra before its release, pushing back on suggestions of a rupture. Prime Minister Andy Burnham called instead for "shared global principles and standards" at the UN General Assembly.

The U.S. agency now responsible for the reviews, the Center for AI Standards and Innovation, currently has no permanent director and operates with minimal staff, The Decoder said, raising questions about how quickly it can process new models.

Microsoft merges Copilot apps, exits personal chatbot race

The redesigned app splits into Home, Code and Autopilot tabs as Microsoft leans into enterprise customers rather than chasing OpenAI, Google and Meta for consumer users.

Microsoft is merging the consumer and workplace versions of its Copilot assistant into one product. The redesign, reported by Bloomberg, aims the product at corporate customers and cedes the personal AI chatbot market to OpenAI, Google and Meta.

The redesigned Copilot app organizes its features into three tabs, Engadget reported. Home covers everyday chat, Code generates apps, dashboards and workflows from natural-language requests, and Autopilot handles recurring tasks without supervision.

Jared Spataro, Microsoft's chief marketing officer for AI at work, described what Autopilot can do. It can "set up and manage a full supplier review process: building the schedule and workback plan, then handling prep, meetings, and follow-ups on its own," he said.

The pivot follows Microsoft pulling Copilot back from a broad Windows push. It reflects a bet that the business case for AI is stronger in enterprises processing data and generating reports than among individual consumers.

Musk aims to double Colossus 2's Nvidia chip count

Musk says a further 660,000 Nvidia chips could come online by December, taking the cluster to roughly 1.21 million and serving external customers including Anthropic and Google.

Elon Musk said xAI's Colossus 2 computing cluster could more than double its Nvidia chip count by the end of the year, Bloomberg reported. The cluster currently runs 110,000 GB200s and 440,000 GB300s.

Musk laid out a rollout schedule: another 220,000 GB300 chips due online next week, a further 220,000 in November, and a final 220,000 in late December "if we get lucky," according to the report. Fully deployed, the cluster would approach 1.21 million chips total.

xAI folded into SpaceX in February. Colossus's spare capacity has since gone to outside customers rather than sitting idle between Grok training runs, Bloomberg reported, though the report did not detail what share of total capacity that represents.

Two of those customers are Anthropic and Google, both rivals to Musk's Grok models on the model side. They now rent time on the cluster alongside xAI's own training runs, according to the report.
