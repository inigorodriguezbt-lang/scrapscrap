## Nvidia opens Vera Rubin rack designs to the Open Compute Project

**Nvidia says its Vera Rubin rack and compute-tray designs are now open through the OCP, with racks of up to 250kW and a Cisco-backed optics standard.**

Nvidia said on Monday at the Open Compute Project Global Summit in San Jose that it has contributed Vera Rubin rack and compute-tray designs to the project through its MGX architecture. The company's blog post says MGX 1.2 adds blind-mate liquid cooling, a liquid-cooled 5,000A busbar and native 19-inch equipment support.

By Nvidia's account, MGX 1.2 supports racks of up to 250kW, with fanless liquid-cooled compute trays. The company also described a Vera CPU rack for agentic AI work such as tool calling and orchestration. It said Vera Rubin NVL72 remains its base for large-scale training and inference.

On networking, Nvidia said Meta is adopting its Spectrum-6 switches, rated at 102.4Tb/s. The design could link more than 100,000 GPUs across up to eight network planes, Nvidia said. Omar Baldonado, a senior director at Meta, is quoted saying the two companies are working to make multiplane fabrics operationally scalable. No independent measurements have been published.

Cisco and Nvidia also announced USFP, a pluggable optics form factor that targets 400Gb/s per lane. Nvidia says that is about 1.3 times more power-efficient and four times denser than 200Gb/s-per-lane optics. TE Connectivity and Amphenol will supply the connectors. Tom's Hardware reported that AMD showed a Helion rack design with up to 32,768 Zen 6 cores, shipping next year.

## Katzenberg and ex-Sora lead said to be seeking access to Sora

**The Information reports a filmmaker-focused startup is negotiating deep access to OpenAI's shuttered Sora video model, which it could customise and sell, with OpenAI taking equity.**

A stealthy startup co-founded by the Hollywood executive Jeffrey Katzenberg and Sora's former research lead Bill Peebles is in talks with OpenAI over deep access to the video model, according to The Information. Reporter Stephanie Palazzolo, who wrote the piece with Cory Weinberg, said on X that Sora could be coming back in some form.

In the AI commentator Rohan Paul's summary, the third co-founder is Sujay Jaswa, a former Dropbox chief financial officer. The company aims to build AI video tools for filmmakers. Under terms said to be under discussion, it could heavily customise Sora and sell software built on it. Palazzolo says the buyers would be film studios.

OpenAI would receive an undisclosed equity stake in return, Paul wrote. The Information's article is paywalled and this paper has not read it in full, so these details come from the two posts. Neither OpenAI nor the startup has confirmed anything, and the startup has no public name or product.

Paul describes the model as shuttered, which is the point of the talks. A deal would give a product OpenAI has stopped selling a second life under outside control. Whether it would cover model weights or only API access is not stated in either post. No price or timeline has been reported.

## Ambi raises $26M for an AI app that listens all day

**Ambi, founded by a former Plaud executive, is releasing iPhone, Mac and Apple Watch apps that can record in the background, with ambient capture off by default.**

Ambi has raised $26 million across several rounds for an app that listens in the background and offers suggestions in real time, TechCrunch reported. The company's co-founder and chief executive is Peter Mo, a former Plaud executive. Luminous Ventures, an early backer of Meituan and Unitree Robotics, led the rounds, and the device maker Huaqin also invested.

The apps work like a meeting note-taker, but users can choose to let them listen all day, according to the report. An AI agent draws on the user's calendar, meetings, knowledge base and web data to surface relevant information, and tasks can start on one device and continue on another. No price or release date was given.

Two more products are planned. An upcoming desktop app would observe users' work and store the important parts in memory to personalise suggestions, and a hardware device with a screen, speaker, camera and microphones is also on the way, again without a date. Ambi has more than 50 staff in San Francisco, Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen.

On privacy, TechCrunch said ambient capture is off by default and can be switched off at any time. Ambi says it does not store recordings and encrypts data, though it did not specify the method. The report notes it is unclear whether bystanders get enough warning, and Mo said users must tell others about ambient capture, calling disclosure an industry-wide problem.

## Meta staff tease a model called Watermelon with fruit emoji

**Mark Zuckerberg and Alexandr Wang posted watermelon emoji within minutes of each other on Monday, and TestingCatalog says Meta teased a model called Watermelon.**

Meta's chief executive Mark Zuckerberg posted a photo on X on Monday with the caption "Muse ordered me 🍉 how thoughtful!", which had passed 2,400 likes when this paper read it. Thirteen minutes earlier, Alexandr Wang had posted "vibes kinda good 🍉", also without explanation. Neither post names a product.

https://x.com/finkd/status/2109660829952176518

TestingCatalog, which reports on unreleased AI features, said an upcoming model called "Watermelon" had been teased, and asked whether that meant training had finished. That is a guess, and the outlet's findings stay unconfirmed until the company ships. Meta has not announced a model, a date or a name.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2109658193496551467

The commentator kimmonismus wrote that Meta employees were hinting at a major release and that this week could be exciting. Another commentator, synthwavedd, said Watermelon is Meta's top-tier model, in the works for months and close to release, citing teasers from Meta staff. Both are bystanders relaying impressions rather than documents.

What the posts establish is narrow: two prominent Meta figures are using the same emoji on the same afternoon, which looks coordinated. What the model is, how large it is and whether it will have open weights are all unknown, and nothing here has been tested by anyone outside the company.
