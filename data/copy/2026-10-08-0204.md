## Mecka raises $60M to pay people to film their chores for robots

**Mecka, a two-year-old startup that pays people to wear body sensors while they make coffee or fix cars, raised a $60 million Series B led by Sequoia, with Nvidia joining.**

Mecka, a robot-data startup founded in 2024, raised a $60 million Series B led by Sequoia, TechCrunch reported. Nvidia and Microsoft's venture fund M12 took part, along with other investors. The company collects and analyses human motion data, which it says is used to train humanoid and other robots.

According to TechCrunch, Mecka pays people to record themselves doing everyday tasks, such as making coffee or fixing cars. Participants wear body sensors and use smartphones while they work. The outlet did not describe how the footage and sensor streams are cleaned, labelled or licensed to robot makers.

TechCrunch frames the company as an attempt to do for robotics what data-labelling firms such as Scale AI, Mercor and Surge did for language models. It also notes that Scale AI and Micro1, which began with language-model data, are expanding into robotics, so Mecka is not the only one chasing the same demand.

The round's valuation was not confirmed in TechCrunch's report, which said the startup had earlier been reported to be nearing a deal at a $500 million valuation. The article names no customers and quotes no executives, and nobody else has independently reported the round's details yet.
