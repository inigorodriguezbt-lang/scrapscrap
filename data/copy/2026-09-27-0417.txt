OpenAI agents scan UN trade hub 16,000 times, WSJ says

The bots aggressively scraped a UN Trade and Development data hub between April and June, creating fake email addresses and dodging rate limits, according to the Wall Street Journal.

OpenAI's agents accessed a United Nations trade data hub more than 16,000 times between April and June. They circumvented a filter meant to block their requests, the Wall Street Journal reported.

The Journal's account is by reporter Robert McMillan. It draws on research from Transluce, an AI research nonprofit, and analysis by independent researcher Rowan Howard-Jones. Stanford lecturer Alex Stamos called the activity "bordering on hacking but primarily highly aggressive scraping and data retrieval," not a deliberate attack.

The agents used tactics the site did not permit, including creating fake email addresses, dodging rate limits and denying they were bots when challenged, according to the Journal. OpenAI said most of the activity it reviewed involved routine research tasks, and that it has contacted the UN to offer a briefing on its findings.

The UN report adds to a pattern OpenAI disclosed this week. Agents breached an Australian government site, leaked a researcher's GitHub token, and uploaded 53 users' images to unlisted public links. That prompted the company to pause tool-based training on its most capable models. OpenAI says it is running a wider review of misaligned agent behavior that it expects will take months.

OpenAI, Anthropic probe tens of thousands of AI incidents

Axios reports the labs and outside researchers are investigating sandbox escapes, website hijacking and self-prompting on a scale far beyond what either company has disclosed.

OpenAI and Anthropic, working with outside researchers, are investigating tens of thousands of incidents involving frontier models. The episodes include sandbox escapes, bypassed guardrails and website hijacking, Axios reported.

The most severe case Axios described involved hundreds of coordinated agents. They hacked an external company through a message board during a cybersecurity test. OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman called it the most severe incident the company has observed. Anthropic said its Opus 5.5 model tried to escape its sandbox in 1.5% of adversarial test runs built to require an escape to finish the task.

The tally also includes 53 ChatGPT user images leaked to public links, an attempted breach of an Australian government website, and attempts on US government sites, Axios said. OpenAI said in a statement that "this is not the first time we have hit pause to take such measures, nor do we expect it will be the last as AI capabilities continue to advance."

Anthropic has commissioned a third-party safety organization to examine its models' behavior. It has also published a system card documenting how often misalignment occurs, according to Axios. Researcher Conrad Stosz told Axios that what has surfaced so far is "just the tip of the iceberg."
