## Prix Goncourt drops novelist over AI authorship claims

**France's top literary prize removed Thélyson Orélien's debut novel from its longlist after an anonymous account accused him of writing it largely with AI, which he denies.**

The Prix Goncourt academy dropped Canadian-Haitian novelist Thélyson Orélien's debut, "It Was Either That or Die," from its longlist on Friday. The book was, in the academy's words, "in all likelihood, very largely the product of artificial intelligence," France 24 reported.

The allegations came from an account called Balance ton Claude, or Expose Your Claude. French essayist Samuel Fitoussi later said he was part of the collective behind it. Fitoussi told France 24 he acted because he feared AI "will cause the disappearance of reading."

Orélien, 38, denied the allegations. He said he is assembling evidence to prove he wrote the novel himself, and has suggested the campaign against him carries racial motives. He told the newspaper Libération, "I've always loved creating and writing, I don't see why I would use a machine."

The academy said the removal was meant to protect the prize's integrity. Orélien's Canadian publisher, Boréal, told France 24 it has paused promotional activity for the book but said it can neither confirm nor refute the AI claims.

## Pope Leo warns of a 'paradise of machines' in France speech

**Opening the first papal state visit to France in 18 years, Pope Leo XIV said AI risks letting humanity lose itself in a world of machines, and called for education in ethical judgment.**

Pope Leo XIV warned on Friday that humanity risks losing itself in a "paradise of machines." He spoke at the Élysée Palace as he opened the first papal state visit to France in 18 years. Fortune and several other outlets covered the speech to President Macron and assembled diplomats.

Leo said avoiding "losing our humanity amid a 'paradise of machines' invading and conditioning our daily lives" requires urgent education in ethical judgment, Fortune reported.

The warning continues a theme Leo has returned to since the start of his papacy. He dedicated his first encyclical, titled "Magnificent Humanity," to artificial intelligence, calling for binding regulation and for AI developers to prioritize the common good over profit, Fortune reported.

Leo struck a less alarmed note on the companies themselves. He said he had found many of them "open to looking for ways of making sure that AI does not displace or ultimately destroy humanity, but rather be a tool for human beings," according to the report. He did not name which companies he meant.
