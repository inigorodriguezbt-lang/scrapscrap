Jotbus, a Show HN launch, lets Claude Code, Codex and Cursor share an end-to-end encrypted scratchpad, started with `npx jotbus`; its site says no repo access is needed.

It targets the copy-paste handoff between coding agents on different machines, though it is a proprietary service with a free tier limited to 60-minute workspaces.


TestingCatalog reports Mitra, an AI assistant that works through a user's own phone number, now lets agents take over chats and talk to other people's Mitra assistants via Mitra Link.

Agent-to-agent scheduling is the use case the company pushes first, with real estate showings and consultant kickoffs named as examples.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2107491917692883053


AlphaXiv says its Paperscrolling feed is now on iOS and Android, showing the latest research as key ideas, figures and audio explanations from its Briefs.

It is aimed at people who already read research on X, turning arXiv into a swipeable feed instead of a link list.

https://x.com/askalphaxiv/status/2107486537206419769


Simon Willison says he asked Opus 5.5 to write computer game music and got results much better than he expected, and asked when models learned to compose competently.

Willison is a closely followed independent tester, but this is one informal try and he published no comparison.


Simon Willison posted that Mistral can now draw his pelican test, with an image from Mistral Large 4, the model Mistral launched in preview today.

The pelican prompt is his informal benchmark for how models handle vector drawing, so the post is an early hands-on, not a measured score.


Arena says Mistral Large 4 is now in its Agent Arena and Code Arena, with scores to follow once votes come in.

Arena ranks models on real long-horizon agent tasks with web search, filesystem and terminal tools, so it is the first independent test of the Mistral release.

The comment landed as OpenAI faces an Australian inquiry into an agent breach, and The Verge also reported a publicist tried to steer him off a user's suicide.


Reuters reports that several countries are weighing bans on smart glasses over privacy concerns.

Camera glasses with built-in AI assistants are the product category at issue, so any ban would hit a fast-growing consumer AI device.
