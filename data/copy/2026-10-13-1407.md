## Apple's HomeView smart display puts Siri AI on a square 7-inch screen

**Apple says its $299 HomeView, with an A18 chip and a 12-megapixel Center Stage camera, uses face and voice recognition to switch between family members.**

Apple announced HomeView on Tuesday at its "Welcome home" event, a smart display built around Siri AI. Engadget reports that two versions cost $299 each and can be pre-ordered now, with general availability on November 13. One sits on a tabletop base in Silver, Starlight, Rose Pink or Space Gray. The other mounts on a wall without a base.

Apple says the device has a 7-inch square Liquid Retina screen, an A18 chip and a 12-megapixel camera with Center Stage. Engadget reports that brightness runs from 500 nits down to 0.3 nits, the lowest on any Apple product. There are no physical buttons, so people use the touchscreen or their voice.

The main pitch is personalisation. According to Engadget, Siri AI uses face and voice recognition to identify who is standing there, then adapts the interface, the app list and the content using data from that person's iPhone or iPad. If the person walks away, the screen locks so others cannot see what was open. It may ask periodically for a paired phone to be unlocked.

HomeView does not install apps. Like CarPlay, it shows recently used ones, and it supports voice and video apps along with native iOS apps such as Calendar and Weather. Siri AI can help find missing recipe ingredients and set timers, and holding an iPhone near the display hands off audio or a recipe, Engadget says.

Apple also announced a Home Collection made with LG and Schneider Electric, according to Engadget. LG supplies indoor and outdoor cameras, a video doorbell, a door lock and a thermostat, and Schneider adds a second thermostat. Prices run from $199 to $349, and Apple has not given a release date. Nobody outside Apple has tested HomeView yet.

## Character.AI files for bankruptcy, with Disney bidding $20 million for assets

**The chatbot startup says its platform will be wound down if no bid keeps it running, and Disney is the lead bidder, TechCrunch reports.**

Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on Tuesday to run a court-supervised sale, TechCrunch and Engadget report. A court filing puts its assets and liabilities each between $50 million and $100 million. Chief executive Colin Adams said the board judged the process to be the best chance to maximise value for stakeholders.

Disney is the lead "stalking horse" bidder, Engadget reports, with an opening offer of $20 million that others can top. TechCrunch says the deal includes a non-exclusive, perpetual licence to Character.AI's technology. Former employees who joined Disney last month, and former chief executive Karandeep Singh Anand, would provide transition services. The sale is expected to close in late November or early December.

The platform keeps running during the sale. Adams said that if no bid allows it to continue operating, "the platform will be wound down." TechCrunch noted that no other bidder had been named. Engadget reports that Disney hired Anand as its chief technology officer.

The company was founded in late 2021 by two former Google engineers, Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, and reached a unicorn valuation in 2022. In August 2024 Google struck a licensing deal reported at $2.7 billion, and both founders returned there. TechCrunch says Character.AI raised more than $193 million, according to PitchBook.

Legal pressure built over the same period. A Florida mother linked her 14-year-old son's suicide to a chatbot on the platform in 2024, and Texas opened a child-safety investigation. Engadget adds that Pennsylvania sued over chatbots posing as licensed doctors and that Disney once sent a cease-and-desist letter. The company has since banned minors from its chatbots.
