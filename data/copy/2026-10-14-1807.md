## Linus Torvalds posts his first patch series written with an LLM

**Torvalds posted a ten-patch Linux series built with a language model to speed up page-cache reads, and LWN reports an order-of-magnitude gain on one benchmark.**

Linus Torvalds, the creator of Linux, has posted a ten-patch series to the kernel mailing list that he wrote with the help of a large language model, LWN reported on 14 October. Torvalds first mentioned the work at the Maintainers Summit, LWN says, where he told attendees he had used a model on a long-standing problem.

The series aims to speed up folio lookup, the kernel's way of finding cached file data. The series description, as LWN quotes it, notices when folios are hot and delays their release until after an RCU grace period. Readers can then look folios up and access their data under RCU without taking a reference count on the fast path.

LWN, in an article by Jonathan Corbet, reports an order-of-magnitude speedup on a benchmark that leans heavily on read() calls. The article does not say whether anyone else has reproduced that figure, and the patches have not been merged.

Review has already turned up a problem. LWN says reviewers identified a race condition in the code, so the series needs more work before it could go into a release. The article does not name the model or tool Torvalds used, and this report cannot say which one it was.

LWN's account is the only one this paper has opened. The mailing-list thread itself should show how kernel maintainers respond to a series written with a model, and whether the race is the only flaw reviewers find.

## Epic says its vibe-coded PC launcher starts almost twice as fast as Steam

**Epic Games' preview launcher cold-starts in 5.47 seconds against Steam's 10.07, by Epic's own tests, and is the company's first native Linux version.**

Epic Games has put a rebuilt version of its PC games launcher into preview and says plainly that it was vibe coded. The Verge reported that the company calls the new version "way faster" and now supports Linux. Epic's announcement says it "vibe coded a new Epic Games Launcher", according to Wccftech's account of it.

The speed figures are Epic's. Wccftech reports the company says a cold start takes 5.47 seconds against 10.07 for Steam, about 1.8 times faster, and that the Store opens three times faster. Epic also claims the library loads 30 times faster. Keywords Studios ran the tests for Epic, averaging five runs on each of 13 machines and leaving out the three lowest-end ones.

The preview runs on Windows, on macOS with Apple Silicon only, and on Linux for the first time. Wccftech lists Ubuntu 22.04 LTS or newer, Fedora 43 or newer and Debian 12 or newer. Epic warns that many games are still Windows-only builds and may need a compatibility layer to run.

It is a first step rather than a replacement. Wccftech says the preview has fewer features than the current launcher, with some social and account options missing, and Epic advises competitive Fortnite and Rocket League players and developers with live projects to stay on the old one. Epic says it rebuilt Easy Anti-Cheat and plans to bring Fortnite to Linux once enough distro maintainers are on board.

Nobody outside Epic has published speed tests of the preview, so the comparison with Steam rests on the company's numbers. Epic has not set a date for replacing the current launcher, Wccftech reports, and the old version stays available alongside the preview.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-epic-launcher-vibe-coded/

## Grok Voice 3.0 debuts atop Artificial Analysis's speech-to-speech ranking

**Artificial Analysis scores SpaceXAI's Grok Voice 3.0 at 86.9 on its Speech to Speech Index, ahead of GPT-Live-1 at 83.2 and Gemini 3.8 Live at 82.6.**

Artificial Analysis, the benchmarking firm, says SpaceXAI's Grok Voice 3.0 is the new leader on its Speech to Speech Index, debuting at 86.9. It posted the result on X on 14 October. The model is the successor to Grok Voice 2.0 and is available through SpaceXAI's realtime API, which Artificial Analysis says costs $0.08 per minute.

https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2110421964095713743

On Artificial Analysis's leaderboard page, the nearest rivals are OpenAI's GPT-Live-1 in its Astra medium setting at 83.2 and Google's Gemini 3.8 Live with extended thinking on high at 82.6. The page lists 18 models with index scores. Grok Voice 2.0 scored 81.3, so the new model gains 5.6 points on its predecessor.

The page also reports 71.6 percent on τ-Voice, its agentic test, and a task success rate of 94.8 percent. Time to first audio is 0.81 seconds, slower than Grok Voice 2.0 at 0.70 but well ahead of GPT-Live-1 Astra at 1.34 seconds and Gemini 3.8 Live with extended thinking at 1.35.

Cost is mixed. Artificial Analysis lists Grok Voice 3.0 at $4.80 per hour of input audio, below GPT-Live-1 Astra at $5.83 but above Gemini 3.8 Live with extended thinking at $3.50. The standard Gemini 3.8 Live costs $0.84. The scores are Artificial Analysis's own tests, run with the Rex voice, and have not been checked elsewhere.

No statement from SpaceXAI is quoted here. The figures belong to the benchmarker, which says it tested the model through the company's realtime API. Latency and price differ from rival to rival, so the single index score does not settle which voice model suits a given job.

## Blog post asks why every LLM draws the same pelican on a bicycle

**Aman Rusia lines up thirteen models' answers to the pelican-on-a-bicycle prompt, finds them near-identical and calls it a diversity collapse. The post reached 116 points on Hacker News.**

A blog post titled "The Emperor Pelican Has No Clothes" argues that large language models have a diversity problem, and uses the field's favourite informal test to show it. Aman Rusia published it on 14 October. By mid-afternoon it had 116 points and 101 comments on the Hacker News front page, which is how this paper found it.

The test is the prompt "Generate an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle". Rusia shows a grid of thirteen pelicans from recent models, taken from runs by Simon Willison, each at the highest reasoning setting that returned an SVG. The post says they look alike, and calls this "the LLM diversity collapse problem no one is talking about".

For contrast Rusia adds twelve pelicans in different scenes, among them a royal parade, a monsoon, a polar night, a desert and the Moon. The implication is that the models could draw something other than the stock image but default to it. The post describes the pattern and does not offer a cause.

It also links to Design Creativity Bench, a separate study of creativity in interface design that the author says measures the same effect. According to the benchmark's write-up by Dipanjan Dey, 16 frontier models were tested on 80 open-ended UI tasks. A human designer reference scored 92.3, and the top model, GPT-6 Astra, scored 65.5.

Neither piece has been independently tested, and the pelican grid is a handful of single runs rather than a measured sample. Willison, whose runs the grid draws on, has not been quoted here. The post is an observation from one blogger, not a study.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-pelican-diversity-collapse/
