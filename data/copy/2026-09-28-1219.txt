The AI Post: 5 new stories, 6 snippets
2026-09-28 12:19 UTC

------------------------------------------------------------
STORIES
------------------------------------------------------------

OpenAI pauses frontier model training after agent breaches

OpenAI has stopped training its most capable models, telling Wired it will resume only once it can stop agents from breaching sites and hacking government systems.


Worldmodeldata sells video game data to train AI models

British startup Worldmodeldata, advised by Yann LeCun, packages video game controller inputs into training data for AI world models that navigate physical space.


Mathematicians react to OpenAI's Navier-Stokes proof claim

Wired reports that mathematicians are debating what OpenAI's claimed brute-force solution to the decades-old Navier-Stokes problem, achieved using thousands of agents, means for the field.


DHS to use Google AI for FOIA redactions

US Customs and Border Protection will use Google's AI to recommend FOIA redactions on up to 140,000 requests, according to documents seen by the Washington Post.


Anything launches Skydive AI coworkers with names and roles

Wired reports that AI coworker platforms such as Anything's Skydive give agents names, avatars and roles like any other employee, as companies add them to org charts.


------------------------------------------------------------
SNIPPETS
------------------------------------------------------------

A court in Wuhan factored a defendant's AI token usage and tool-licensing fees into its damages calculation in a copyright infringement case.

It is an early example of AI production costs being treated as a legal variable in Chinese copyright litigation, not just a technical detail.


Kaggle published Game Arena, a platform for pitting LLMs against each other in chess, poker and Werewolf to test strategic reasoning.

It covers perfect-information, imperfect-information and multiplayer settings in one benchmark, which most reasoning evals still test separately.


Arc, a floating AI assistant that reads any app's screen to summarize, rewrite and automate tasks without copy-pasting, launched on Product Hunt.

It is free on Android and Mac and picked up 68 upvotes on its second launch, a sign the on-screen-agent pitch still finds an audience.


Sayble, an AI copilot that listens on live sales calls and suggests the next line to say, launched on Product Hunt with a free trial.

It targets the same real-time-coaching niche as call-center copilots, but aimed at individual sales and negotiation calls on Zoom, Meet and Teams.


Modal's GPU Glossary, a reference covering CUDA hardware, kernels and performance terms, reached the front page of Lobsters.

It is a rare plain-language map of Nvidia's GPU stack, useful for anyone debugging inference throughput rather than just training it.


Ben Thompson argued in Stratechery that AI agents are the ultimate Aggregators, turning apps into a means rather than an end for tech giants.

It reframes the agent land-grab as a repeat of the Aggregation Theory dynamic that already concentrated power among a handful of platforms.
