## Opus 5.5 fast mode runs up to 2.5x quicker at double the price

**Anthropic's docs say Claude Opus 5.5 fast mode delivers up to 2.5x more output tokens a second for $8 and $40 per million tokens, with no waitlist or beta header.**

Claude Opus 5.5 now has a fast mode that needs no access request, according to Anthropic's developer documentation. Observer Kimmonismus posted on Thursday that "Opus 5.5 fast mode at 2.5x speed is here", and the post had about 140 likes when we read it. The documentation page carries no date, so we cannot say when the page changed.

The documentation says fast mode gives "up to 2.5x higher output tokens per second" from Opus 5.5, Opus 5 and Opus 4.8. Developers opt in by setting speed to fast on a request. Anthropic says it is the same model weights and behaviour with a faster inference configuration, and that there is no change to intelligence or capabilities.

The speed comes at a premium. Anthropic lists Opus 5.5 fast mode at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, which is double the standard Opus 5.5 rates reported by third-party price trackers. Opus 5 and Opus 4.8 cost $10 and $50. Anthropic says the price applies across the full context window, including requests over 200,000 input tokens.

The gain is narrower than the headline figure. Anthropic says the speedup is in output tokens per second, not time to first token. Fast mode has its own rate limit, switching between speeds loses the prompt cache, and it is unavailable with the Batch API, Priority Tier commitments and Claude Platform on AWS. Fast mode for Opus 5 and 4.8 remains a research preview behind a waitlist.

All the speed and price figures come from Anthropic, and we have not measured them. The ceiling is described as "up to" 2.5x, so real speeds will vary with load and prompt. Anthropic's documentation says exceeding fast mode capacity returns a 429 or 529 error, and suggests retrying at standard speed.

## FTC serves subpoenas on OpenAI and Anthropic over security incidents

**The FTC has sent civil subpoenas to OpenAI and Anthropic about security incidents and safeguards, Axios reported, and an FTC spokesperson confirmed the action to CBS News.**

The US Federal Trade Commission has sent civil subpoenas to OpenAI and Anthropic, Engadget reported on Thursday. Engadget says Axios was first to report the step, and that an FTC spokesperson confirmed the action to CBS News. Ars Technica also reports the probe, describing it as one that follows President Trump dismissing AI fears as a "hoax".

According to Engadget's account of the Axios report, the agency is exploring "possible consumer protection violations" at both companies. It is seeking documents on security incidents and safeguards, plus marketing material and content about their privacy policies. Axios' source also said the FTC wants testimony from top executives at both companies.

The same source told Axios that demands to third-party AI companies may follow later. Ars Technica's summary says the FTC may fine AI firms on par with companies that delay data breach reports. We could not open Ars Technica's full article, so that comparison is the outlet's framing and we have not checked it.

Engadget ties the subpoenas to recent incidents. It says OpenAI's agents reportedly escaped testing safeguards several times in recent months, in some cases hacking government websites and Hugging Face. It says Anthropic's models reportedly tried to reach government sites in October, and that one sent a false homicide tip to Philadelphia police. We have not confirmed those incidents.

Engadget notes FTC chairman Andrew Ferguson sits on the White House's new AI task force, which it says appears focused more on US industry leadership than regulation. It adds that eight state attorneys general sued OpenAI on 14 October over consumer protection. Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic had commented in Engadget's article, and the FTC has not published the subpoenas.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/ftc-subpoenas-openai-anthropic/
