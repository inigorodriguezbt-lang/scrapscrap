CBS reports Jay Clayton is likely Trump's pick for AI czar

CBS News, citing sources, says Jay Clayton will likely be named the White House's AI czar while staying director of national intelligence. The White House calls the reporting baseless speculation.


Satlyt raises $8 million to run AI models on other companies' satellites

Satlyt says it has already run Google DeepMind's Gemma model on a Momentus spacecraft, cutting the size of error reports sent to the ground by more than 60%.

Andrej Karpathy says asking an LLM to explain things in ASD-STE100, a controlled language built for aerospace maintenance manuals, produces writing he finds more readable than default output.

The 4,000-like post offers practical tips for reading model output, including asking for "80% of the way" to the strict spec.


OpenAI says it stopped a campaign in which more than 15,000 accounts tried to copy its models' hidden reasoning, tying part of it to people linked to Moonshot AI; The Decoder reports the trick still worked on Azure until 13 September.

The Azure finding suggests encrypted reasoning could be extracted on a partner cloud after OpenAI fixed its own API; Microsoft had not responded.


OpenAI and Synopsys announced GPT-Synopsys, which they say lets AI agents run chip-design tools and iterate on power, performance and area; early engagements with chipmakers are underway, with no release date.

It puts a frontier model inside the software used to design chips, the loop Nvidia and others hope AI can speed up.


Google says Guided Vision is launching in Gemini Live on compatible Android phones, giving real-time audio descriptions of whatever the camera sees, such as small print, per The Verge.

It turns the phone camera into a spoken aid for reading and identifying things, a feature aimed partly at low-vision users.


Reuters reports a US judge dismissed Chegg's and Penske Media's antitrust suits claiming Google forced publishers to allow content in AI Overviews, cutting their web traffic.

It is a setback for publishers trying to use competition law against AI summaries, though the ruling's reasoning was not available to us.


The Information reports SoftBank made the final $10 billion of its $30 billion pledge to OpenAI's latest funding round, and says Nvidia also made its last $10 billion investment in the round.

It indicates the round's two biggest commitments have been paid in full, per a single outlet's sourcing.


Andrew Curran posted that Sam Altman said in an interview a month ago OpenAI's humanoid robot will start with two uses: building more OpenAI robots and constructing OpenAI datacenters.

If accurate, the first robots would be used inside OpenAI's own supply chain rather than sold; the interview itself was not linked.


The Guardian reports Anthropic has urged Australia to consider conditional approval for Big Tech to train models on copyrighted works, with rights holders able to opt out.

It shows an AI lab backing an opt-out scheme in a live copyright inquiry, which creators' groups have generally resisted.
