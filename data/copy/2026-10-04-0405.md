## Meta opens Muse Gadgets, a DIY kit for building AI hardware

**Meta says its Apache 2.0 kit pairs ESP32 firmware and a Linux SDK with its Muse agent, and 5,000 USB-C Home Link devices are due within weeks.**

Meta has announced Muse Gadgets, an open-source project that lets hobbyists build their own AI hardware, The Decoder reported on 3 October. It includes firmware for ESP32 boards and a Linux SDK for connecting homemade devices to Muse, Meta's AI agent. The code is Apache 2.0 licensed, and the project has a Discord community.

Meta also built a device of its own. The Muse Home Link is a small USB-C gadget that connects Muse to a home network, letting it control any device with an HTTPS interface, such as a TV or speaker, according to Meta. The company says 5,000 units have been produced and should ship within weeks, free for Muse subscribers while supplies last.

The project is also listed on Product Hunt, which describes it as Meta's open-source kit for building your own AI gadgets. We found no independent builds or tests of the firmware or the SDK, so what they can do rests on Meta's description. The Decoder suggests Meta may use the project to learn which form factors people actually want, a reading the company has not stated.

Muse itself has become a hit with consumers, Wired reported on 3 October, with millions downloading the assistant and connecting it to bank accounts, messages or health data. Researchers have since extracted its internal instructions, which Meta told Wired it meant to make accessible in the interest of transparency. The Gadgets kit would extend that agent from the phone into the home.

## White House names Clayton to lead AI 'Super Intelligence Force'

**Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will lead a White House task force with 120 days to report on AI's risks and opportunities, the Wall Street Journal reported.**

The White House has created a task force to examine AI's risks and opportunities, the Wall Street Journal reported on Saturday. It is to recommend what role the federal government should play in overseeing the technology, with a report due within 120 days. Reuters relayed the account, which cites the task force's head, Jay Clayton.

The group is known as the "Super Intelligence Force", and Clayton, who heads the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, told the Journal he would lead it. He would effectively serve as President Donald Trump's AI czar, according to Reuters. The report also cites a senior White House official. Bloomberg carried the story the same day, attributing it to the Journal.

The appointment follows earlier reporting. CBS News said on Thursday, citing sources, that Clayton was likely to be Trump's pick for AI czar while staying director of national intelligence. The commentator Andrew Curran wrote on X late Saturday that the task force had been renamed the SI Force, citing the Journal.

The Reuters account does not say who else sits on the task force, what budget or legal authority it has, or whether its report would bind agencies. We opened Reuters' version, not the Journal's paywalled article, and found no White House statement confirming the name. The 120-day clock would run into early next year if it started now.
