Mobile MCP server lets AI agents control phone apps

The open-source project, which has drawn 7,700 GitHub stars, lets Claude, Codex, Gemini and other coding agents automate real and simulated iOS and Android devices.

An open-source project called Mobile MCP lets AI coding agents automate iOS and Android apps, according to its GitHub repository. It works with Claude Code, Codex and Gemini on real devices, emulators and simulators. The tool, maintained by Mobile Next, has drawn 7,700 stars and 662 forks on GitHub.

Mobile MCP runs as a Model Context Protocol server. It gives agents tools to read an app's accessibility tree, tap, swipe and take screenshots, the repository says. The project favors that approach over vision models, which it says removes the need for platform-specific testing skills like XCUITest or Espresso.

The tool also connects to cloud-hosted devices through a paid service called Mobile Next Cloud. It works with any MCP-compatible client beyond the named agents, including Cursor, Windsurf and GitHub Copilot, the repository says. It is released under the Apache 2.0 license.

A post from developer midudev highlighting the tool's free tier and open-source license drew more than 380 likes on X on Saturday.

Father, sons build Zelda-inspired game with Claude Opus 5.5

Developer Danny Limanseta said he and his sons spent a day building a paintbrush-combat adventure game by describing what they wanted to Anthropic's Opus 5.5.

Developer Danny Limanseta said he and his two sons spent a day building a video game together, according to a post on X. The boys described what they wanted, and Limanseta prompted Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 to build it. They chose a "Zelda: Breath of the Wild"-inspired open world.

The result, called Wild Brush, gives its hero a paintbrush weapon with four colors tied to different effects, Limanseta said: red for fire, blue for ice, yellow for bounce and green for vines. The finished game includes two biomes, three dungeons with puzzles, an underground cave, more than 30 enemies and a final boss, according to the post.

https://x.com/DannyLimanseta/status/2104215873120764032

Limanseta said the game is playable in a browser, though only on desktop. He said it was not the first game he had vibe-coded, but the most satisfying because his sons were actively co-creating and playing it.

Opus 5.5 launched five days ago. Limanseta's post is among a wave of demos built with the model that have circulated on X in the days since.

China weighs letting ByteDance, Alibaba buy Nvidia chips

China's technology ministry has signaled it will approve purchases of Nvidia's new RTX Pro 5500 chip, with ByteDance said to be seeking 1 million units, The Information reported.

China's technology ministry has told Alibaba, ByteDance and other companies it intends to approve their purchases of Nvidia's new RTX Pro 5500 chip. The Information reported the plan Saturday, citing two people familiar with the matter.

Reuters said it could not independently verify the report. ByteDance is seeking to order 1 million of the chips to power its AI models, according to The Information. The outlet said the ministry had asked companies to disclose their planned purchase volumes and intended use.

Nvidia launched the RTX Pro 5500 on September 14, built on its Blackwell architecture with 84 gigabytes of memory. It is sold for high-end professional workstations rather than as a dedicated AI accelerator. Chinese buyers have faced restrictions on importing Nvidia's most advanced AI chips since Washington tightened export controls.

The potential approval would be a shift for Beijing, which has restricted Chinese companies from buying advanced foreign AI chips as it promotes domestic alternatives from Huawei. Neither China's ministry nor Nvidia had commented publicly on the report as of Saturday.
