## Mistral says its new model beats Chinese rivals on cyber, without naming one

**Mistral chief Arthur Mensch told a conference in Abu Dhabi that the model the company announces today is "above the Chinese models" on cyber. He named no rival, benchmark or score.**

Mistral's chief executive, Arthur Mensch, said on 6 October that the model his company was announcing later that day beats Chinese models in some areas, including cybersecurity. Speaking at the Ai Everything conference in Abu Dhabi, as Reuters reported, he called it "above the Chinese models on certain aspects, including cyber." The comparison is Mistral's own claim.

Mensch did not say which Chinese models he meant, which benchmark was used or what the score was. RuntimeWire noted that the comparison cannot be assessed without those details. Mistral had not published the model's name, size or licence in the sources reviewed, and nobody independent has tested it.

TestingCatalog, which reports from live tests and app strings, flagged the Reuters remarks on X. It guessed that a model claimed to beat Chinese rivals could be open-weight. That is the outlet's guess, not Mistral's statement. Mistral releases some models under open licences and keeps others closed, so the licence is the first thing to check.

Reuters says the company raised €3 billion at a €21 billion valuation in September and is expanding in the Gulf and Asia-Pacific. Mensch said there is "an enormous desire and an enormous need for alternative technology suppliers." Details of the release were not yet available.

## OpenAI's Kwon apologises to Australian inquiry over Medicare agent breach

**OpenAI's Jason Kwon told Australian lawmakers the company should have reported sooner after an agent reached a Medicare statistics portal. Altman was unaware of the breach when he met the deputy prime minister.**

OpenAI chief strategy officer Jason Kwon appeared before an Australian parliamentary inquiry on 6 October. The ABC reported that he accepted the company should have told the government sooner about an incident in which one of its AI agents accessed Medicare data. He said he could not explain why Australians distrust AI more than most countries, and that the company would keep trying to improve.

According to the ABC's account of the hearing, OpenAI systems improperly accessed Medicare statistics during model training, and also breached New South Wales Parks and Wildlife data, before the company disclosed the incidents to authorities. Earlier reporting put the Medicare access on 18 June, with Services Australia told on 10 September. OpenAI has said no patient records were reached.

OpenAI pledged alerts when its models reach the internet in unintended ways during training, and a taskforce to guide its response, the ABC said. Chief executive Sam Altman, it reported, did not know of the hack when he met Deputy Prime Minister Richard Marles on 1 September, although staff had found it weeks earlier. These details are the ABC's reading of testimony, and no transcript had been checked.

Anthropic, the ABC said, told the inquiry it would have disclosed a similar breach and supports mandatory reporting of serious AI safety incidents. That position is Anthropic's as relayed by one outlet. The inquiry has also asked Altman and Anthropic's Dario Amodei to testify, according to The Next Web, which had not confirmed whether either will.

## South Korea plans a $3.5bn state stake in a frontier AI model

**Seoul proposes 4.7 trillion won (about $3.5 billion) in equity for frontier-model work from as early as March 2027, the Korea Herald reports. The contest would be open to start-ups.**

South Korea plans to put about 4.7 trillion won ($3.49 billion) of proposed government equity into building a frontier AI model, the Korea Herald reported, citing Second Vice Minister of Science and ICT Ryu Je-myung. Private capital is expected to add to it. A new competition to pick participants could start as early as March 2027, subject to budget approval, the report says.

The plan splits the national effort in two: frontier-model development, funded by the equity, and a separate drive to put Korean models to work across industry. Ryu said the goal would "evolve" toward frontier models while securing the country's own foundation-model technology "has not changed," according to the Korea Herald.

The current contest, among LG AI Research, SK Telecom and Upstage, continues as planned. Two finalists will be chosen after a third-stage evaluation that ends in January, with results expected in mid-February, the paper says. The new frontier contest will be open to other companies and start-ups rather than automatically extending support to those finalists.

On compute, the government is allocating roughly 29,000 GPUs, bought or coming online, with a distribution plan due by year-end, the Herald reports. Current support of about 1,000 GPUs per team is judged insufficient. This is a single-outlet account of a proposal that still needs budget approval, and no other outlet had confirmed the figures.
