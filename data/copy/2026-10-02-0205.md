## CBS reports Jay Clayton is likely Trump's pick for AI czar

**CBS News, citing sources, says Jay Clayton will likely be named the White House's AI czar while staying director of national intelligence. The White House calls the reporting baseless speculation.**

CBS News reported on Thursday, citing multiple sources briefed on the matter, that Jay Clayton is likely to be President Trump's choice as the White House's AI czar. The sources said Clayton may keep his current job as director of national intelligence while taking the new role, which would put one official over both the intelligence agencies and AI policy.

The report is not confirmed. A White House official told CBS that any personnel announcement would come directly from the President, and that any reporting before then was baseless speculation. CBS described its story as breaking and subject to updates, and we have not seen a second outlet confirm the choice.

The post has been vacant on paper since Trump said last month that he would appoint an AI czar. According to CBS, he described the role then with the line "Only High I.Q. individuals need apply!", and the choice has since been the subject of speculation in Washington.

CBS says the timing follows a White House meeting at which leaders of major AI companies signed voluntary safety standards. After it, the outlet reports, Trump told reporters he planned to name someone within three or four days and called the candidate "extraordinary". That window has now passed without an announcement, as far as CBS reports.

What the job would cover is not defined in the CBS report, including whether it would carry authority over agencies or act as an adviser. Those details, and whether Clayton would hold both posts at once, would only be settled by an official announcement, which the White House has not made.

## Satlyt raises $8 million to run AI models on other companies' satellites

**Satlyt says it has already run Google DeepMind's Gemma model on a Momentus spacecraft, cutting the size of error reports sent to the ground by more than 60%.**

Satlyt, a startup that writes software for running AI models on satellites, has raised an $8 million seed round led by Houston-based Non Sibi Ventures, TechCrunch reported on Thursday. The company does not build satellites. Chief executive Rama Afullo told TechCrunch it aims to be a software layer that works across many operators' spacecraft.

Afullo previously worked in Google's cloud division and briefly at SpaceX's Starlink, and pitched orbital computing inside both before founding Satlyt, with offices in Sunnyvale and Nairobi, according to TechCrunch. He contrasted the company's open approach with what the outlet called SpaceX's closed, all-in-one, iPhone-style model.

The company says it has completed two demonstration missions. In one it deployed Google DeepMind's Gemma model on a Momentus spacecraft, which TechCrunch says cut the size of software error reports sent to the ground by more than 60%. A third mission is due to launch this week on a TakeMe2Space satellite, with NASA and Stellerian among its customers.

Afullo told TechCrunch he wants Satlyt on 20% of satellites by 2030, and expects builders to treat a processor as standard kit. "You're putting up a satellite without putting up a GPU on it, at the very least, you're doing yourself a disservice," he said. The 20% figure is the founder's goal, not a forecast we can check.

Big companies are testing the same idea. International Cyber Digest reported on Thursday that Google launched one of its AI chips into orbit for the first time, on a Planet Labs-built satellite carried by a SpaceX rocket, as the first hardware test for Project Suncatcher. It said Google estimates space data centres need about 1,800 Starship flights over ten years, a figure we have not verified.
