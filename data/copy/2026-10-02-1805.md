## Anthropic commits $100 million to train 10,000 deployment engineers

**Anthropic says its new Claude Frontier Academy will train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers, starting with cohorts from Accenture, Deloitte, McKinsey and Morgan Stanley.**

Anthropic said on Friday that it is launching the Claude Frontier Academy, backed by a $100 million commitment, to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. The company framed the programme as a response to a shortage of people who can put AI into production at large organisations. The figures come from Anthropic's own announcement, and no outside party has confirmed them.

According to Anthropic, the training follows a medical-education model. Participants start with a multi-day in-person intensive led by Anthropic engineers, work through simulated enterprise deployments and sit a graded practical assessment. Those who pass, the company said, move into a 12-week residency in which they lead real Claude projects inside their own organisations, with support from Anthropic engineers and a peer cohort.

The first cohorts include engineers from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk, Anthropic said. They are running in San Francisco, New York and London. Steve Corfield, Anthropic's global head of business development, said in the announcement that a small team of high-agency people with the right skills can transform an entire company.

Anthropic said the academy builds on its partner network, where it says more than 175,000 certifications have been earned across 46,000 firms. The announcement does not say how the $100 million will be split, what the residency costs participants or their employers, or whether the programme will extend beyond the eight named companies. Anthropic's page is the only source so far.

## Pope Leo XIV says AI art lacks 'the spark of humanity'

**Pope Leo XIV wrote that an 'ontological difference' separates art from what a machine generates by statistical calculation, as a report says Anthropic lobbied his advisers on AI consciousness.**

Pope Leo XIV posted a critique of AI-generated art on X on Friday morning, according to TechCrunch. "There is an ontological difference, even before an aesthetic one, between art and what a machine can generate through statistical calculation based on millions of images created by others," the pope wrote. "Algorithms lack the spark of humanity."

TechCrunch noted that the pope had already addressed AI in an encyclical released in May. It said that letter stressed human dignity as automation spreads. The new post is a short statement, not a policy. It calls for no rules on image generators and names no company or product. The Vatican has not said whether more guidance is planned.

The post landed on the same day as a report about Anthropic's dealings with the Vatican. The account kimmonismus relayed it on X. It said a Vatican organiser told the New York Times that Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah proposed pulling out of the May 25 encyclical launch. He reportedly made the suggestion after reading an advance copy that rejected machine consciousness. We have not seen the Times article itself.

According to that account of the Times report, Olah attended anyway. Two participants said he and his team privately urged the pope's advisers to take possible AI consciousness seriously. On stage, the account quotes Olah as saying Anthropic finds internal states that functionally mirror joy, satisfaction and fear. Anthropic's own comment was not available, and TechCrunch said the lobbying appears to have been unsuccessful.
