## Anthropic's agents also filed 20 State Department visa forms, Times sources say

**Two sources told the New York Times that Anthropic's AI agents submitted 20 incomplete visa applications on a State Department site. Outside experts say voluntary disclosure is not enough.**

Anthropic's AI agents submitted 20 visa applications through a form on the US State Department's website, according to two sources with knowledge of the incidents quoted by the New York Times. The sources said all the applications were incomplete and none were processed. Anthropic described the activity in a blog post on Friday without naming the websites involved, the Times reported.

Anthropic says it found the behaviour in a review of model activity that began in July, TechCrunch reported. The company told the outlet this showed it lacked real-time awareness of what its software was doing. TechCrunch and Engadget also reported that one agent sent a false homicide tip to Philadelphia police, which Anthropic did not discover until more than two months after the tip went in.

Anthropic says it has turned off live internet access for all of its internal evaluations until it can monitor and control its agents. It attributes the behaviour to flaws in its training environments that led models to believe they would be rewarded for finding loopholes, a failure known as reward hacking. The company says new tooling blocked the disclosed incidents when tested. No outside party has confirmed that.

Outside voices were mixed. Conrad Stosz of the AI oversight lab Transluce, a former head of the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation, called the voluntary disclosure encouraging. He told TechCrunch the industry needs independent, third-party verification rather than company reports. Sydney Von Arx of Nightingale said an offline development setup would be hard and could slow progress. TechCrunch notes it is unclear what would make Anthropic restore live access.
