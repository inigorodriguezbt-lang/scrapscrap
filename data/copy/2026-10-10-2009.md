## OpenAI lists unannounced gpt-rosalind-discovery model on pricing page

**The unannounced model costs $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output, matching gpt-rosalind-research, and OpenAI has not said what it does.**

OpenAI's API pricing page now lists a model called gpt-rosalind-discovery under life sciences models, beside gpt-rosalind-research. TestingCatalog, which reports from live tests and documentation, spotted the entry after a post by the AI Tracker Bot. OpenAI has published no blog post, help article or system card for it, and the company has not confirmed the listing.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2108804914071658577

The page gives both models the same prices: $5 per million input tokens, $0.50 per million cached input tokens and $25 per million output tokens. For gpt-rosalind-research, the page says billing began on 5 October 2026 and that access is limited to approved internal research through a trusted-access programme. It attaches no such note to the new model.

What the model is for is unclear. TestingCatalog says its purpose is not known and guesses from the name that it could target drug discovery, such as target identification or molecule design. That is the outlet's inference, not OpenAI's statement. It may be a separately trained model or a differently tuned version of the research one.

The page does carry one hint of a family. It says all eligible organisations will continue to get access to the latest GPT-Rosalind models as they are released. OpenAI introduced GPT-Rosalind in April as a reasoning model for biology, and TestingCatalog reports it opened to eligible organisations worldwide through trusted access in September. No one outside OpenAI has tested the new entry.

## Medicare agency's 1,700-member Slack gives AI firms a say on health apps

**KFF Health News reports AI firms such as Palantir sit in a CMS-run Slack where vendors press for easier access to patient records.**

A Slack workspace run by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services has grown to about 1,700 members. KFF Health News reported on 9 October, in a story carried by CBS News, that it gives technology companies a direct line to officials shaping health app rules. Amy Gleason, now a CMS product chief, started it in August 2025.

Members include digital health firms, AI companies such as Palantir, and investors, according to KFF, while only a handful of patient advocates, doctors, plus hospital representatives belong. The group's code of conduct says it is not a federal advisory committee and will not be used to obtain advice for HHS or CMS. Former FDA lawyer Joseph Daval told KFF it resembles one.

KFF describes vendors using the channel to press for a frictionless route to patient records through the national TEFCA exchange after one-time consent. It reports that an FDA listening session on conversational AI, with invitations sent through Slack, drew at least 35 industry groups including Microsoft, Anthropic, OpenAI, Apple and Google, and was not on the public calendar.

CMS declined to answer several questions, including about the workspace's legality, KFF reports. The FDA did not respond. Gleason said in an emailed statement that the Health Technology Ecosystem is an open, voluntary technical collaboration. AMA chief executive John Whyte told KFF that some AI tools are not ready for widespread use. We have not verified the Slack messages KFF cites.
