## Veria says its AI agent found a 2015 XRP Ledger bug worth 18 trillion XRP

**Veria Labs says its AI agent found and exploited a decade-old XRP Ledger overflow that could have created about 18 trillion XRP in one transaction. Ripple paid the top $250,000 bounty.**

Veria Labs, an AI security startup, says its AI agent found a critical flaw in the XRP Ledger's payment engine. The bug could have let an attacker create about 18 trillion XRP in one transaction, The Block reported on Sunday. Co-founder Cayden Liao told the outlet the agent also built a working exploit.

https://x.com/IntCyberDigest/status/2109471868704993685

RippleX, the ledger's developer, shipped a fix in xrpld version 3.4.1 on 25 September, three days after Veria reported the bug. The disclosure report states there is "no evidence that this issue was exploited on any public network," The Block reported. Liao said Ripple paid the top bounty of $250,000, which he called the largest ever paid for a bug found entirely by an AI agent.

The flaw dates to 2015 and chains two bugs, The Block said. The payment engine summed the cost of many order-book offers filled at once with no overflow check, so a large enough total wrapped around to a small number. A related check on newly created XRP had its own addition issue, so it would not have stopped the minting. Liao said each bug alone would be low severity.

An attacker would have needed a few hundred accounts listing tiny token fractions for large amounts of XRP, then one payment closing all the sales, per the report. The sellers would be paid in full while the buyer paid a few hundred drops plus the fee. Liao put the potential creation at roughly 184 times the 100 billion XRP cap. Those figures are Veria's scenario, not a loss.

The patch skipped the usual amendment vote, which needs more than 80 percent of trusted validators over two weeks, The Block reported, calling that a first in more than ten years of amendments. More than 80 percent of validators on the default list ran the fix that day. The 3.4.1 source code followed about two weeks after the binaries, which drew criticism from Cyber Capital founder Justin Bons.

RippleX engineer Mayukha Vadari said "You can't sneak a critical bug patch into a routine public release process." Head of engineering J. Ayo Akinyele said "AI is fundamentally changing how quickly vulnerabilities can be discovered," and that RippleX will expand AI-assisted bug hunting. We could not open the disclosure report itself, and Veria's claims about the exploit have not been independently verified.
