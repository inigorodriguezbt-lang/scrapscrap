SNIPPETS

Alibaba's Qwen team congratulated Lightseek on TokenSpeed results showing Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at 685 output tokens per second per user on Nvidia GPUs.

A per-user speed figure that high would make a reasoning-class model feel instant in chat, though the number comes from Qwen and its partner.


Google DeepMind says it is working with Singapore's GovTech to build on-device scam detection on its open Gemma model, fine-tuned on local scam tactics so it can flag threats without sending data off the phone.

It is a concrete public-sector use of an open-weights model, and the privacy claim rests on the processing staying on the device.


SemiAnalysis says Nvidia's Rubin runs the SGLang inference engine at 4.23 times the performance per dollar of GB300 NVL72, in a thread it posted overnight.

SGLang is widely used in production, but the figure depends on the serving point chosen, and SemiAnalysis has published much larger gains against other baselines.


MaxForAI says Espressif shares rose 8.27% to 116.30 yuan on Wednesday morning after reports that the OpenAI recorder used a Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 board built on Espressif's chip.

Espressif had also issued a strong third-quarter profit forecast the night before, so the poster says the move cannot all be put down to the bugging story.


GroundAnything, a 4B vision grounding model on Hugging Face papers, says it scores 72.42% across 30 benchmarks and runs 4.51 times faster in self-speculative mode with a 0.74-point drop on COCO.

The authors only promise to release code and weights later, so the results are self-reported and nothing can be downloaded yet.


Chinese manufacturers took 97% of global humanoid robot shipments in the first half of 2026, Bloomberg reports, citing government support and deep supply chains.

The share, if accurate, means almost every humanoid shipped this year came from China, which frames the race Western robot startups are chasing.


Peter Wildeford relayed that the suspect in the OpenAI recorder case had asked ChatGPT to list meeting rooms and to "build a continuous audio recorder", then a second and third unit.

The prompts, as relayed from OpenAI's newsroom account, are the first detail on how the device was made, and they remain unverified by court records.
