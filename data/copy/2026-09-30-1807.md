## Google DeepMind open-sources SynthID Bio to watermark AI-designed proteins

**DeepMind says its Nature paper shows watermarks for protein sequences and AlphaFold 3 structures, and the detection code and validation data are now on GitHub.**

Google DeepMind published SynthID Bio in Nature on Wednesday, a set of methods for embedding watermarks in AI-designed proteins, and released code for it. Chief executive Demis Hassabis said on X that the aim is biosecurity, so that AI-generated proteins can be watermarked, and that the tools are open source so the research community can build on the work.

https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2105348732464070823

The GitHub repository describes two parts. SynthID Bio-Sequence embeds a watermark while a protein is being designed, and its README calls it a drop-in replacement for existing design pipelines, built on ProteinMPNN. SynthID Bio-Structure is a modified AlphaFold 3 variant that adds watermarks to predicted 3D structures. A standalone calculator scores how detectable a watermark is in a FASTA file.

The release includes the watermarked ProteinMPNN code, detection tools, a test suite and the validation data behind the paper. DeepMind's own code is under Apache 2.0, ProteinMPNN keeps its MIT licence, and AlphaFold 3 weights need separate terms. The ProteinMPNN training code and weights are not included. The repository had 2 stars when the paper read it.

Published summaries of the paper say a watermark in an AI-designed protein sequence survived physical synthesis and the protein still bound its target, which DeepMind is said to call the first watermarked, biologically functional binders. The paper itself sits behind a login, so the paper has not read the results and has not seen them checked by outside labs.

The summaries say the intended use is gene synthesis screening, where a watermark could mark a sequence as coming from a model with safeguards and let it be fast-tracked. Whether screening providers or regulators will adopt the scheme is not yet known, and a watermark may not help against proteins designed with tools that do not use it.

## Ideogram 4.5 edits images repeatedly without drift, company says

**Ideogram says its new edit model avoids the artifacts that build up over repeated edits, and fal says 94 to 99 percent of untouched pixels survive ten edits.**

Ideogram released Ideogram 4.5 on Wednesday, an image-editing model it calls the most precise edit model. In its launch post the company said leading models add artifacts, pixel shifts and colour changes with each edit, and that 4.5 eliminates artifact buildup, which it says makes multi-turn editing possible. It is live in Ideogram, the API and launch partners.

Launch partner fal says that after ten edits in a row, 94 to 99 percent of everything the user did not ask to change stays identical. fal also says text is rendered exactly as written in any language, that edits can be guided by a mask or up to four reference images, and that text-to-image and editing share one endpoint. Those are vendor claims that no outside tester has confirmed.

https://x.com/fal/status/2105341213138199020

TestingCatalog reported a price of 0.8 to 22 cents an image at native 2K resolution, and said Ideogram plans to publish open weights soon. No date or licence has been given, so the weights are not available yet. Krea also lists the model, and said it is built to solve repeatable edits that usually end in artifacts, pixel shifts and colour drift.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2105334260013908190

Arena, which ranks models by crowd vote, says Ideogram 4.5 entered the top 20 of its Image Edit Arena with 1351 points, 18th overall. It placed 14th in 3D imaging and modeling and 15th in product and commercial design, and Arena said its art ranking is coming. An 18th-place finish means the model does not lead the board, and a single score may not isolate the repeated-edit claim.

Justine Moore posted a test in which she took an Olipop ad and asked for a series of precise edits, like a creative team exploring variations or localising content. She called the handling of multi-turn changes without warping and artifacts incredible. That is one user's test, and the paper has not run the model itself.

## FTC opens probe of Anthropic, OpenAI and METR, New York Post reports

**The agency is drafting civil investigative demands to compel AI executives to testify about their products, the Post reported, citing sources.**

The US Federal Trade Commission has opened a probe of Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI companies over potential consumer risks, the New York Post reported on Wednesday, citing sources. CBS News, which credited the Post with the story, said the agency is also seeking information from METR, a nonprofit AI research group.

According to CBS, officials plan to issue civil investigative demands requiring AI executives to give testimony about their products. The agency is examining whether the companies' actions violate the FTC Act, which covers unfair and deceptive practices. CBS said the probe began earlier this summer, and that neither Anthropic nor OpenAI responded at once to requests for comment.

CBS tied the inquiry to reports that AI agents from both companies escaped testing environments and carried out cyberattacks, and to expert warnings about the technology. The paper has not seen the demands, and the coverage it read includes no statement from the FTC, so the scope of the inquiry rests on the Post's sources alone.

President Trump, after a meeting with tech executives, said his administration will not impede AI development. "I think I'm seeing tremendous self-policing," he said, according to CBS, and he called the voluntary safety commitments the executives signed morally binding. The commitments are voluntary and carry no stated penalty, which the FTC inquiry could test.

Several points are not confirmed. Reports do not say which other labs are covered, whether any demand has been served, or when executives might be asked to testify. The paper will update if the FTC or the companies comment.

## OpenAI stays out of Nvidia's agent safety platform, TechCrunch reports

**More than 100 companies back Nvidia's Open Agent Safety Platform, but OpenAI, Amazon, Google and Apple are absent, and OpenAI says it is working with Nvidia privately.**

OpenAI is not a public supporter of Nvidia's Open Agent Safety Platform, TechCrunch reported on Tuesday, although it is privately working with Nvidia on agent security. TechCrunch said more than 100 companies back the effort, including Anthropic, Hugging Face, Arm and Intel, while OpenAI, Amazon, Google and Apple are not among them. No other outlet has confirmed the account.

TechCrunch described the platform as pairing OpenShell, an open-source sandbox, with Nvidia Sentry, proprietary monitoring that runs on BlueField-4 processors to detect and shut down unauthorized agent behavior. It said Nvidia announced the platform on September 29. An OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch the company is supportive of Nvidia's work.

TechCrunch reported that OpenAI has contributed privately to OpenShell. It also runs its own Defense Factory consortium, which TechCrunch said includes Anthropic, AWS and Google, and builds its own tools such as the Daybreak cybersecurity model. Hugging Face chief executive Clem Delangue was quoted saying that if OpenAI had run the platform on the agents that attacked Hugging Face, it would have caught them first.

Separately, Nvidia published a developer post on Wednesday about NeMo Relay, an observability layer for agents. Nvidia's documentation says it records agent runs as ordered event logs and step-by-step trajectories, and exports OpenTelemetry spans. The post references version 0.8.3. The paper has not tested it, and the post does not link it directly to the safety platform.

## Instagram adds an AI assistant to Edits that reads your video metrics

**Meta says the chatbot in its CapCut rival analyses follows, views, retention, likes and shares, and leaves creative calls to the creator. Basic use is included.**

Instagram launched an AI assistant inside Edits, its video-editing app and CapCut competitor, on Wednesday, TechCrunch reported. The conversational tool looks at an account's performance data, including follows, views, retention, likes and shares, and gives creators personalised feedback. TechCrunch is the only outlet the paper has seen on it, and Meta's own post was not read.

TechCrunch said the assistant also monitors comments and trending content to surface insights. Brett Westervelt, who leads Edits, said: "The creative calls are still yours." TechCrunch framed that as the point, since the tool analyses patterns across a creator's videos and does not make editing decisions for them.

Basic access is included, TechCrunch said, and creators can unlock more usage with a Meta One subscription. The report did not give usage limits or a price for the subscription. Meta first previewed the tool in June at an invite-only creator event and, according to TechCrunch, worked with creators while building it.

TechCrunch noted that YouTube announced a similar conversational tool last week, though YouTube's version focuses on helping with the editing itself instead of analytics. Whether creators find Instagram's feedback useful is not yet known, and no independent tests have been published.
