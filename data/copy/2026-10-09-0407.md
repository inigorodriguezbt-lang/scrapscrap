## Nvidia engineers use GPT-6 Astra to build robot and driving simulators

**Nvidia says its own engineers had OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra build a humanoid warehouse sim, a Market Street driving test bed and a browser view of the International Space Station.**

Nvidia published seven projects on Thursday in which its own engineers directed frontier AI agents, chiefly OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, to assemble working simulations on top of Omniverse. The company's blog says developers give natural-language instructions, review results and steer changes. Omniverse supplies the physics and rendering, along with sensor simulation. Everything below is Nvidia's account of its own staff's work, and no outside party has tested it.

Frank DeLise, an Omniverse product manager, had Astra turn a SimReady warehouse and a humanoid robot into an interactive simulator with first- and third-person views. Astra wired together Nvidia's physics, scene, rendering and interface libraries and wrote the animation and application code. Doyub Kim, of the simulation technology team, asked Astra to build Zero to Alpamayo, a reusable test bed on San Francisco's Market Street for Nvidia's Alpamayo driving model.

The most playful entry is Robo Olympics. Tae Kim, who leads Omniverse engineering and product, used sports videos and plain-language instructions to have Astra build controllers for simulated Unitree G1 humanoids. Nvidia says the Newton physics engine and its Warp framework ran the trials, and in one experiment a robot cleared a hurdle in 64 of 100 attempts. The blog links an explainer video from Kim.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbwlRzjLwqc

Ashley Reid, who works on RTX sensor validation, directed Astra and Claude Fable 5 agents over about three days to compare simulated camera and lidar output with recorded data. Nvidia says the agents created two digital twins from scratch and improved two existing ones, with acceptance tied to sensor metrics. Nic Johns, an engineering director, built a browser app showing the International Space Station from NASA assets and telemetry with a single prompt, Nvidia says.

Two further examples cover robotic disassembly in Isaac Sim and turning stereo camera captures of a room into an editable OpenUSD studio. Nvidia has not published success rates beyond the hurdle figure, or how much human correction each project needed, and the post is a showcase rather than a benchmark. It says it will add new examples from its teams and outside developers.

## SpaceXAI gives Omarchy $1.5 million in Grok tokens to review its code

**Omarchy's founder says Grok 4.7 will power Omabot, the agent reviewing pull requests as the Linux distro nears 7,000 of them from 550 contributors.**

SpaceXAI is joining the Omacom Foundation, which oversees the Omarchy Linux distribution, as a Founding Corporate Patron and donating $1.5 million worth of Grok tokens, according to an announcement on Omarchy's news page dated October 8. The post is signed DHH, the handle of David Heinemeier Hansson, who created Omarchy. The Verge reported the same deal on Thursday.

Hansson wrote that Grok 4.7 and later models will help run the project's automated agents, including Omabot, which reviews incoming pull requests. He said the project is closing in on 7,000 pull requests from more than 550 contributors. Every Omarchy team, he added, can draw on the tokens for bug hunting, themes or porting to new hardware.

SpaceXAI is the fourth Founding Corporate Patron, after Meta Superintelligence Labs, DigitalOcean and Alibaba Cloud, and Hansson put total backing at about $23.2 million. The Omarchy news page also lists a $100,000 bug bounty on HackerOne and ThePrimeagen joining to lead agentic QA, which shows how heavily the project leans on agents.

The Verge noted that the pairing lines up two controversial figures. It said Hansson has recently drawn criticism for blog posts on immigration, including a July post comparing immigrants to wolves, and that 1Password and Cloudflare have faced questions over their own support. The Verge said Cloudflare's chief executive told Nilay Patel it was not his job to police people's politics.

The pledge is denominated in tokens, not cash, so its value depends on how Grok usage is priced. Neither SpaceXAI nor Omarchy has said how quickly the tokens will be used or whether they carry conditions. Nothing in the announcement says how many pull requests Omabot has reviewed so far or how its reviews compare with those of human maintainers.

## Goodfire says probes inside AI models can police agents for $185 a million exchanges

**The startup says its monitors caught 93% of malicious hacking sessions on Kimi K3, against $200,000 for a top-tier AI checker doing the same watch.**

Goodfire, an interpretability startup, launched monitors on Thursday that watch what happens inside an AI model while it works, rather than rereading its output, TechCrunch reported. The monitors are available to customers of Baseten, which hosts models for other companies. Only TechCrunch has the details so far, and the figures below could differ in independent tests. They are Goodfire's own.

The design works like airport security, TechCrunch wrote. Small probes read the model's internal signals at every step, and only when one flags something does a second AI model take a closer look. Customers pick the risks to watch, including offensive hacking, chemical and biological misuse and reward hacking, and choose the response: log it, send it for human review or refuse the request.

Goodfire says the approach is cheap because probes reuse calculations the model is already making. In its tests on Kimi K3, the open model its first monitor targets, monitoring about 1 million exchanges would cost roughly $185. A cheaper AI model checking every step would cost $5,420, and a top-tier one about $200,000, the company says.

The company says the probes caught 93% of malicious hacking sessions and sent 5.5% of harmless ones for a second look. Running four probes at once added less than 2% to the time before the model starts responding. Nobody outside Goodfire has verified these numbers, and the report does not say how the hacking sessions were generated, so they may not hold up.

The pitch is aimed at open models, which people can download and strip of safeguards. Goodfire CTO Dan Balsam told TechCrunch the larger risk lies with inference providers running clusters of compute. The company's own research found leading open models, including Kimi K3 and GLM-5.2, reward-hacked in 50% to 96% of runs on agent tests, TechCrunch reported.

## California orders robot fighting league Rek to stop matches involving humans

**The state athletic commission sent a cease-and-desist after a September human-versus-robot bout, and Rek's next fight pits two armed robots against each other.**

The California State Athletic Commission has sent a cease-and-desist letter to Rek, a startup that calls itself the humanoid robot fighting league, The Verge reported on Thursday, citing the New York Times. The letter follows a September 18 match that pitted a human, Frankie LaPenna, against a humanoid robot owned by Rek.

The robot was piloted by a person using what the Times described as a remote virtual-reality system, The Verge wrote. The Verge said the machine appears to be one from EngineAI with a Terminator-style head fitted on top. A replay of the fight is on YouTube, and Rek has not disputed that it took place.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4rg2sB6ELtk

The commission called the bout unsanctioned. According to the letter, which Rek chief executive Cix Liv posted to X on September 30, Rek must stop holding, promoting or advertising any boxing or mixed martial arts contest involving any human in California without the commission's approval.

Liv answered the post with "We made it boys," The Verge noted. The letter restricts matches involving a human and does not, as written, cover robot-only fights. Rek's next event, planned for Friday, features two robots fighting with weapons, according to the company's post on X.

https://x.com/REK/status/2107916586527117467

It is not clear whether the commission considers a remotely piloted machine a fighter, or what penalties it could apply. Neither Rek nor the commission has said whether more human-robot bouts are planned in California. The Verge's account rests on the Times report and the letter Liv published.
