OpenAI withdraws three of its AI-written maths papers over a sign error

A sign error in one manuscript invalidated an argument two other papers rely on, OpenAI's maths repository says, a day after mathematicians urged colleagues to quit its work.


AI took 75 per cent of Europe's $25 billion venture quarter, Crunchbase says

Crunchbase's Q3 tally puts European funding up 77 per cent on last year, led by Mistral's $3.5 billion round, which it calls the biggest ever for a European company.

OpenAI says it disrupted a Russian influence operation tied to the Politology network that used ChatGPT, calling it the most far-reaching it has disrupted, NBC News reported.

The operation reportedly reached schools in Latin America, a politician and media outlets, and stoked Ukraine-Poland tension.


CrowdStrike says a likely Chinese-speaking, financially motivated actor used open-source agentic pentest tool ARTEX with DeepSeek, GLM and Grok models against South Korean financial firms from late September.

CrowdStrike gives moderate confidence on attribution and says the number of victims is unconfirmed; it does not claim the AI ran the intrusions alone.


Google Cloud announced a universal Gemini agent that can have its own Workspace account, email and calendar, run multi-day tasks, and work in Microsoft 365 and Slack, VentureBeat reported.

Google gave no price or general availability date, and has published no independent reliability benchmarks for long cross-app tasks.


Theo says T3 Code now supports many agent harnesses through ACP, plus a new beta Muse provider, available on its nightly build; the post had 470 likes.

T3 Code is a third-party coding tool; the post does not say which company makes Muse, and the paper has not tested the provider.


Three AI safety researchers fired by OpenAI wrote to its board urging it to preserve chain-of-thought monitoring and cooperate with outside auditors, mark_k reported on X.

OpenAI denies firing them for raising safety concerns; the researchers dispute that they leaked anything, and the letter itself has not been seen by the paper.


Hugging Face chief Clément Delangue called for public traces of AI agents attacking and defending systems, saying defenders cannot learn from what they cannot see.

The appeal comes as labs keep such traces private; he offered open DMs to anyone being pressured to withhold them.
