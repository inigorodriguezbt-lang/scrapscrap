webAI releases a 3.66B formal-logic model that runs in 2 GiB

webAI says its TwIL-LM3-Pro, a 3.66-billion-parameter model that checks whether conclusions follow from premises, roughly matches Qwen3-8B on formal logic and fits in a 2.09 GiB file.

Andrej Karpathy shared an eval that asks an LLM "Land or Water?" for a latitude and longitude given as text, repeated 16,200 times and plotted as an image. He says the models know.

The map comes out recognisable from text alone, which Karpathy takes as evidence of how much world structure models absorb from compressing the internet.


Korean local media say Samsung is quoting mid-to-high $4 per gigabit for HBM4 in talks with major customers, per analyst Jukan, over three times the $1.50 per gigabit of HBM3E.

HBM is the scarce part of AI accelerators, so a tripled price per bit would feed straight into the cost of future GPUs.


Theo says T3 Code, his open-source coding-agent app, now has over 400,000 users, a figure he posted without a breakdown of how active they are.

It puts a solo-led agent front end among the larger independent tools next to the coding tools the labs ship themselves.


Codync, an MIT-licensed Rust tool, runs Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and 40-plus other agents as named bots on your own machine, with approvals from iPhone, Mac, Linux or terminal; its repo has 54 stars.

It pitches itself as an open alternative to Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots, keeping transcripts and code local, though the project is early.


Elon Musk says Tesla decided to nudge its AI5 chip up to 96GB of memory, so it would not be the only company using the minimum-RAM version of LP5.

It revises his earlier statement that AI5 would carry 72GB, a change of a third in memory for Tesla's in-car and robot silicon.
