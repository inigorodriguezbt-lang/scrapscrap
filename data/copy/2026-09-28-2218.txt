Gemini fully replaces Google Assistant on Android

9to5Google reported the assistant has been removed from phones, tablets and paired devices across the US and Europe, with no way to switch back.


GitHub's AI agent finds 24 Android app flaws

GitHub Security Lab says its open-source taskflow agent uncovered 24 vulnerabilities in Android apps, including a location-tracking bug in OsmAnd and an account-takeover flaw in Wikipedia's app.


Solo developer ships Jeff, an open decision-model family

Jeff mimics the request format of the proprietary Jev API but runs locally in about 22 to 28 milliseconds, trained on synthetic data with no closed-model outputs, its GitHub README says.


Browser tool lets users run seven tiny LLMs

MicroLLM Lab runs 25-million to 360-million-parameter models entirely on-device via WebGPU, compressing them to 4 bits per parameter so they fit in under 85 megabytes of memory.


Shopify lets AI agents complete purchases at checkout

The company added three new WebMCP tools that let browser-based agents read, update and complete a Shopify checkout without scraping the page, TechCrunch reported.


Tender Claws builds AI game for Meta glasses

Body Proxy has players complete about 100 odd tasks while an AI watches through Ray-Ban Meta glasses and judges their performance in real time, UploadVR reported.

SNIPPETS
------------------------------------------------------------

OpenAI says its GPT-6 Astra model completed a 50-tab tax workbook twice as fast as GPT-5.6 Sol for accounting firm Basis, while Agent Arena ranks the cheaper GPT-6 Luna 23rd on its real-world agent leaderboard.

Arena's own numbers put GPT-6 Luna's median cost per task at $0.05, 98% below GPT-6 Astra's $2.59, showing OpenAI is spreading one model family across widely different price points.


Google is discontinuing Gemini's task-specific "Gems" feature in favor of a new "skills" system, TechCrunch reported, as all-in-one agents like Meta's Muse and Instinct gain ground.

It shows Google folding a standalone feature into a broader agent platform rather than competing feature-for-feature with rivals building single assistants that do everything.


Inference provider Modal Labs is closing in on a $750 million round that would value it at $15.75 billion, more than triple its valuation four months ago, a source told TechCrunch.

The jump shows how fast demand for AI compute infrastructure is repricing even mid-sized providers, not just the frontier labs themselves.


Mistral is opening a Munich hub for "Physics AI and Industrial AI" research with BMW, Siemens Energy and the Technical University of Munich as partners, the company said.

Mistral is betting that simulating physical processes like crash tests, which currently take days to run, is a market it can own in Europe.


Benchmark firm ValsAI says reasoning longer on its new MysteryMechanism test raises GPT-6 Astra's and Claude Opus 5.5's accuracy but costs up to 20 times more depending on the effort level chosen.

It is a concrete look at where extra "thinking" stops paying for itself, a question every buyer of reasoning-model APIs is currently pricing in by hand.


Developer theo said a $200-a-month Claude Code subscription is netting him about $9,000 worth of Opus usage a month, based on three accounts he ran down to zero since Opus 5.5 shipped.

It is a real usage data point on how generous Anthropic's flat-rate coding plans have become relative to metered API pricing.


Liminal launched a walking-meditation mode that uses Meta AI glasses to narrate a spoken meditation based on whatever the wearer is looking at outdoors, UploadVR reported.

It is another sign wellness apps are moving off the headset and onto glasses light enough to wear on an actual walk.


Computer science professor Cal Newport's essay calling for a congressional investigation into OpenAI and Anthropic's safety practices topped Hacker News with 103 points and 24 comments.

Newport argues labs' own talk of extinction risk is being used to justify moving faster rather than slower, a framing that clearly struck a nerve with HN's readers.
