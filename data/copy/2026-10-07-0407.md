## Arizona court throws out AI video of a murder victim forgiving his killer

**An appeals court says an AI avatar of road-rage victim Christopher Pelkey carried 'undue emotional weight' in sentencing, and ordered a resentencing without it, 404 Media reports.**

An Arizona appellate court has ruled that an AI-generated video of a dead man, shown at his killer's sentencing, must be excluded when the case is heard again. 404 Media reported the decision on 6 October, and no other outlet has been found carrying it yet. The court said the video carried undue emotional weight, and ordered resentencing.

The case involves Gabriel Horcasitas, convicted of manslaughter for a 2021 road-rage shooting that killed Christopher Pelkey. At sentencing, Pelkey's sister Stacey Wales played an AI avatar of her brother delivering words she had written herself. In the video the avatar forgives the killer and says they might have been friends in other circumstances, according to 404 Media.

The judge reacted warmly. 404 Media quotes him saying 'I loved that video' and describes him as finding it genuine; Horcasitas received the maximum sentence of 10.5 years. The conviction itself stands, and only the sentence goes back to the trial court, which must now decide the penalty without the avatar.

According to the report, the appeals court found that a lifelike recreation conveys false authority and prejudiced the judge unfairly. It distinguished the video from photographs of victims, which courts have long allowed in comparable cases. The reasoning suggests judges may treat generated likenesses differently from recorded ones, though one appellate ruling does not set a national rule.

Wales defended her choice. 404 Media quotes her asking: 'Isn't that what you want human connection to be? Human connection is powerful.' She compared courtroom acceptance of AI to the way photography won legal acceptance over roughly 15 years. For now, the ruling puts that argument on hold in Arizona.

## Google's Foresight for Mac turns scribbled bullets into full notes, offline

**Google AI Edge Foresight records a meeting, takes your shorthand and expands it from the transcript, and Google says audio and transcripts never leave the Mac. It is downloadable now.**

Google has released AI Edge Foresight, a notetaking app for Mac, according to 9to5Google, which published the details on 6 October. The idea is simple: jot bullet points during a meeting while the app records audio, and it expands your shorthand into polished notes using the transcript. The app can be downloaded from the Google AI Edge Foresight website.

Google's pitch is privacy. 9to5Google reports that transcripts and audio are processed entirely on the device rather than in the cloud, so the app works offline. That is the company's description of its own product, and nobody outside Google has yet published an independent check of what leaves the machine, and 9to5Google does not claim to have run one.

The app does more than meeting notes. Users can point it at project folders and reference material, including Google Drive files, and ask questions about their work without organising anything first. It also has a Live Assistance mode that listens during a conversation and answers questions automatically, according to the report.

Underneath sits EmbeddingGemma 2, which 9to5Google describes as a 740-million-parameter model built on the Gemma 4 architecture and licensed Apache 2.0. The report puts its memory needs at about 191MB of RAM for text only and 567MB for multimodal use. Those figures come from Google's model description rather than from outside testing.

Foresight arrives alongside the EmbeddingGemma 2 release, and it works as a demonstration of the model running on a laptop. Whether the notes it writes are accurate enough to trust is untested in the sources available, since the report is based on Google's description and does not include hands-on results. Apple-silicon requirements and pricing were not stated.
