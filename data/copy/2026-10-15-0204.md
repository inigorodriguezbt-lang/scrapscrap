## OpenAI agrees to stop using alleged Apple trade secrets in lawsuit

**At a hearing, OpenAI agreed not to use material Apple says was stolen and to image the devices of 14 employees, and the judge called an injunction for Apple likely.**

OpenAI agreed on Wednesday not to use trade secrets that Apple claims were stolen by employees who moved to OpenAI, while the companies' legal fight plays out. Bloomberg reported the commitment from a hearing, and 9to5Mac, citing Bloomberg, gave the details. The agreement is voluntary, and it follows Apple's request for a preliminary injunction.

OpenAI also committed to collect forensic images of devices belonging to 14 employees who may hold Apple's confidential information, 9to5Mac reported. Judge Edward Davila can enforce the agreement and could impose sanctions if OpenAI does not comply. He is also reported to have said he was likely to grant Apple's injunction request.

Davila called Apple's request to inspect every device and repository that might hold its secrets a huge ask. Apple's lawyer, John Desmarais, said Apple would confine inspections to a small group of former staff, saying OpenAI has a few bad apples rather than 400. Apple's suit names two former employees, Chang Liu and Tang Tan, as well as OpenAI and io Products.

OpenAI went on the offensive the same day. In a filing, it alleged that a former employee who left to return to Apple took more than 100 confidential files about unreleased hardware to personal accounts. International Cyber Digest relayed the claim, and Apple denies the allegations. The filing is OpenAI's account and has not been tested in court.

The case sits on the hardware side of the AI race. According to 9to5Mac, the court granted many of Apple's requests for expedited discovery on Tuesday, covering internal documents, forensic evidence and employee testimony. Neither article names the next hearing date, and 9to5Mac's report carries no on-the-record statement from OpenAI.
