## Gemini 4 Argon reaches Google AI Pro users in Antigravity

**TestingCatalog reports Google AI Pro subscribers can now use Gemini 4 Argon in Antigravity under a dedicated weekly quota, while Google cites 97 of 134 bugs closed in under three hours.**

TestingCatalog reported on Thursday night that Gemini 4 Argon is rolling out to Google AI Pro subscribers in Antigravity, Google's coding tool. The outlet said the model comes with a new weekly quota set aside for Argon alone, and added that the rollout moved quickly. Google had not published the quota figure in anything the paper could open.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2110872881261601280

Google's own Antigravity blog, dated October 13, lists the desktop app and command-line tool as available to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, and to businesses through the Gemini API and eligible Gemini Enterprise plans. The page gives no quotas or pricing. An independent guide said the tiers listed there do not all line up with what users see.

The blog leans on Alphabet's own use. Google said more than 100,000 people use an internal version with Argon each month, that agentic code rose 35% over four weeks and that post-submit rollbacks fell 30%. It said over half of Cloud sales staff and nearly half of finance staff now use Antigravity.

Google also listed case studies, all self-reported. It said 97 of 134 bugs were closed in under three hours, a Fortran-to-Rust migration ran 1.8 times faster, and a security audit found four critical bugs where two earlier frontier models had found none. The page cites no standard benchmark scores for Argon.

The company did not say how the new weekly quota compares with limits on other models, or whether heavy users will hit it. TestingCatalog's report rests on screenshots of the subscriber view, and Google has not commented on the Pro rollout in the material the paper has seen.

## Apple's Joswiak 'not threatened' by OpenAI hardware

**Apple marketing chief Greg Joswiak told TIME "We don't feel threatened" by OpenAI's devices, as Apple said it found no evidence for OpenAI's claim that an ex-OpenAI hire took 100 files.**

Greg Joswiak, Apple's senior vice president of worldwide marketing, told TIME that Apple does not worry about OpenAI's hardware plans. "We don't feel threatened," he said, according to 9to5Mac's account of the interview. He argued that Apple's tight integration lets it "create just a better experience than anybody else."

Joswiak also addressed enthusiasts of the technology. "We love them, and they can use it too, but that's not our target," he said, adding that Apple wants "to make it better for everyday people." TIME's Andrew Chow described the group as Silicon Valley's "lunatic fringe". 9to5Mac noted Joswiak did not define the term.

The remarks landed a day after a legal exchange between the companies. 9to5Mac reported that OpenAI filed allegations that a former employee who left for Apple sent more than 100 confidential files about at least one unreleased OpenAI device to his personal accounts. Apple is already suing OpenAI, io Products and two former employees over trade secrets.

In a court filing, Apple said it has policies to keep new hires from using a former employer's confidential information. It said OpenAI filed "without any notice to Apple." Apple began investigating on October 9 and told OpenAI on October 12 that it could not find any OpenAI confidential information. Reuters reported Apple found no evidence for the claim.

Apple said OpenAI did not answer its request for more specific details and instead filed Docket No. 176. It also said the employee left Apple for OpenAI, then returned, and works on iPhone manufacturing design. OpenAI's allegations are its own and untested in court, and OpenAI has not published a reply to Apple's account.
