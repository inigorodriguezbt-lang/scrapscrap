Microsoft benchmark finds agents often claim success but leave databases wrong

Microsoft's ThinkingBox checks what an AI agent actually changed in a database, not what it said. Across 121,680 trials, the authors say 67% of failures were clean tool calls with wrong values.


Willison says coding agents mean pay-per-use services need hard spending caps

Simon Willison argues cloud services should cut off at a set bill by default, since agents make surprise bills of thousands of dollars easier. AWS and Google Cloud only just added caps.

Tom's Hardware reports Google has frozen product-vulnerability submissions to its open-source bug bounty program until 2027 because maintainers are drowning in invalid, AI-generated reports.

The freeze shows low-quality AI output is now degrading the security reporting channels that open-source projects rely on.


9to5Google reports that from October 9 the Gemini app's free tier loses Flash and Pro and keeps only Flash-Lite, while $4.99 AI Plus loses Pro and the $19.99 AI Pro tier gains Deep Think.

Google is moving its strongest models up the price ladder, and Deep Think reaching the mid tier is the one gain.


Tom's Hardware reports a Futurum chief says AI agents use five times as many tokens as humans, heading for ten times, mostly because cached prompts are re-read repeatedly rather than new work.

If agents mostly reread context, inference demand grows faster than the useful work done, which bears on both costs and capacity.


Security researcher Paulos Yibelo says Vercel confirmed a KVM zero-day allowing full VM escape to host root; Vercel reportedly paid its $50,000 maximum bounty, according to a screenshot he posted.

The bug sits in hypervisor infrastructure that hosts AI-agent sandboxes, though the details and Vercel's own statement are not yet public.


Andrew Curran says the White House's AI task force has been renamed the SI Force and will be led by Jay Clayton, citing the WSJ, with no official announcement yet.

It would put the task force's 120-day report on AI risks under a single named chair, though nothing is confirmed by the White House.
