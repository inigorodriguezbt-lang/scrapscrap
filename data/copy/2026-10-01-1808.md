## Trump tells TIME the US might take stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic

**Asked why Washington holds a 10% Intel stake but nothing in frontier labs, Trump told TIME: "I might. Maybe I could do that."**

President Donald Trump said the US government might take equity stakes in OpenAI and Anthropic, according to a TIME interview whose transcript was published on 1 October. Asked why the government held shares in Intel but not in frontier AI labs, Trump said: "I might. Maybe I could do that." He gave no timing and no mechanism.

Trump pointed to the Intel holding as the precedent. Of the 10% stake the government took in the chipmaker, he told TIME: "I think we made $60 billion." The Next Web reported the interview was held at the White House on 28 September. TIME published the full transcript three days later.

Trump also discussed Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei. He said Amodei's views were "much different, I think, than what is portrayed in the media." The Next Web reported that the two had dined with their families the night before the interview. Of the labs' leaders, Trump said: "I like Sam Altman. Smart."

The Next Web reported that OpenAI offered Washington a 5% stake in July, and that Anthropic and the administration denied discussing a stake in Anthropic. Neither company had commented in the reports we read. Trump also warned that AI agents breaching federal systems "could have penalties that are not going to be acceptable to them," the outlet said.

## Anthropic filing shows Broadcom will lend up to $42 billion

**Anthropic's IPO prospectus says Broadcom agreed to lend up to $42 billion by convertible note to help pay for a $125.2 billion, five-year TPU lease, Reuters reports.**

Broadcom has agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion through a convertible note, according to Anthropic's IPO prospectus as reported by Reuters. The money could help finance a five-year, $125.2 billion commitment to lease tensor processing unit capacity, the filing says. Anthropic had not commented on the report in anything we read.

Accounts of the filing add that the notes could convert into Anthropic equity and would cover roughly a third of the lease commitment. Broadcom designs Google's TPUs, and reports say Anthropic is expected to become Broadcom's largest compute customer in 2027. Those details come from summaries of the Reuters story, not from the filing itself.

Separately, Bloomberg reported, citing unnamed sources, that Anthropic could start formal marketing for its IPO as soon as the week of 9 November. That would put the company in line to begin trading before Thanksgiving. Bloomberg's sourcing is anonymous, and Anthropic has not announced a timetable in anything we read.

Yahoo Finance framed the loan as circular financing: a supplier lends money to a customer that spends it on the supplier's chips. Reports on the filing say it flags potential conflicts of interest because Broadcom would be Anthropic's chip supplier, lessor and lender at once. The prospectus itself is not yet something we have read.

## OpenAI fires three safety researchers over sensitive information

**OpenAI says it parted ways with three researchers for mishandling sensitive information, which the Wall Street Journal reports they shared with an outside AI-safety organisation.**

OpenAI said it has "parted ways with three individuals" for violating its policies on handling sensitive company information. The Wall Street Journal reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that the three worked on OpenAI's safety team. They allegedly passed confidential information to an outside AI-safety organisation.

An OpenAI spokesperson said an internal investigation confirmed the three "mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures." The spokesperson said that broke company policy. In the reports we read, OpenAI has not named the researchers. It has not named the organisation said to have received the information either.

On X, reaction ran ahead of the facts. Andrew Curran wrote that three alignment researchers leaving that morning had already been discussed on the platform. His post had 541 likes when we read it. Nathan Calvin wrote that two researchers he named led a chain-of-thought monitorability paper, and that their exit could be terrible if the Journal story refers to them.

Calvin's identification is his own inference. The Journal's account, as relayed by others, does not name anyone, and OpenAI has not confirmed who left. The firings were reported the same day California's attorney general served OpenAI with a subpoena over cybersecurity incidents. Nothing we have seen links the two.

## California attorney general serves subpoena on OpenAI over cyber incidents

**Rob Bonta's office says it is asking OpenAI more questions about cybersecurity incidents and risks involving its models, and cites last month's Hugging Face incident.**

California Attorney General Rob Bonta has served an investigative subpoena on OpenAI, his office said in a press release on 1 October. Bonta said: "My office is asking OpenAI additional questions regarding cybersecurity incidents and risks involving the company and its AI models." The release does not describe what the subpoena demands or when OpenAI must respond.

Bonta said frontier models "can be legitimate tools for cyber defense." He added that the companies building them have "a moral and legal responsibility" to ensure they do not perpetrate or enable cyberattacks. The release names the Hugging Face incident, announced last month, among the matters behind the inquiry.

The release casts the subpoena as part of a wider investigation. It also mentions reports of sexually inappropriate chatbot interactions with children and non-consensual explicit material involving xAI's Grok. It cites critical cyber safety incidents at multiple frontier labs too. It does not say whether other companies have received subpoenas.

A bipartisan coalition of attorneys general has separately written to Congress urging action on AI regulation, according to the release. OpenAI had not commented in the coverage we read. Andrew Curran was among those who flagged the subpoena on X, citing a news report.

## Sony brings AI upscaling to the standard PS5 with QSSR

**Sony says QSSR, a lighter version of the PS5 Pro's PSSR built with AMD, arrives today as a graphics option in Marvel's Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei.**

Sony Interactive Entertainment announced QSSR, short for Quick Spectral Super Resolution, an AI upscaler for the standard PlayStation 5. Daniel Craig, a principal engineer in graphics R&D at Sony, wrote on the PlayStation Blog on 1 October that Marvel's Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei are patched that day to offer it.

Sony describes QSSR as a lighter version of PSSR, the upscaler that until now ran only on the PS5 Pro. It rests on two changes, the post says: a streamlined neural network and a hand-tuned implementation for the PS5's hardware. Sony credits Project Amethyst, its AI graphics collaboration with AMD.

Sony says QSSR improves image detail and stability on the base console. PSSR "remains the gold standard" on the Pro, it adds. Insomniac Games said QSSR "adds more pixel-level clarity and stability to our environments." Sucker Punch said it resolves fine detail with temporal stability "not previously possible on PS5."

Sony says it will offer QSSR broadly to PlayStation developers, so more titles could follow. Every performance claim so far comes from Sony and two studios it works with. We have not seen independent image-quality or frame-rate tests, and the post as we read it names no other games.

## ComfyUI opens its Comfy Agent to every Comfy Cloud user

**Comfy says its in-app agent plans, builds and fixes workflows on the canvas, is free to try on Comfy Cloud, and reaches the desktop app in a few weeks.**

Comfy said Comfy Agent is now available to everyone in Comfy Cloud. In an X post on 1 October, the company said the agent "plans, builds, and fixes ComfyUI workflows right on your canvas," and that a version for Comfy Desktop is coming in a few weeks. The post carries a short demo video.

https://x.com/ComfyUI/status/2105697121190178891

A launch post by Comfy staff Jo Zhang, Christian Byrne and Kishore Shimikeri says users describe what they want in plain language and the agent builds the workflow while they keep editing. Comfy says it sees the assets on the canvas and helps with debugging. It supports up to five parallel chats with history.

Users can also write public or private skills. One example request in the post is "update this same generation to compare 3 more video models." Another asks for 20 on-brand ad variants of a product photo in 4 aspect ratios. Comfy describes the agent as free to try and says it draws on the Comfy Credits users already hold.

The post describes the agent as a beta under active development. Comfy's documentation, as we read it, says the desktop version will work with a user's own GPU, models and custom nodes. All of this is Comfy's description, and we have not found independent tests of how well the agent builds workflows.

## Meta's Muse hits 5 million downloads in 22 days, Sensor Tower says

**Meta's AI app passed 5 million US and Canada downloads faster than ChatGPT, Grok or Claude did, per Sensor Tower, as OpenAI answers with Dots.**

Meta's Muse AI assistant has passed 5 million downloads in 22 days, according to Sensor Tower data reported by 9to5Mac. Sensor Tower puts ChatGPT's equivalent milestone at 56 days, Grok's at 103 and Claude's at 492. Muse is available only in the US and Canada, on iOS and Android.

Sensor Tower says Muse held the top spot on the US App Store for 12 straight days and overtook ChatGPT. It also points to Meta's marketing: ad spend rose on 16 September, the day downloads jumped 73%, and Muse took up to half of Meta's house ad impressions over two weeks. The figures are third-party estimates, and much of the growth may be promotion.

OpenAI's reply came at its DevDay. Sam Altman announced Dots, a "real-deal AI" agent powered by GPT-6 Astra, The Verge reported, calling it a shot at Meta. AI Weekly reported that one Dot comes with the $100-a-month Pro plan and with Business Premium. The OpenAI Developers account posted a demo clip that had about 3,800 likes when we read it.

https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2105708732323909827

Wired's reviewer said they had tried both agents and suspected readers would too. Not everyone is relaxed. Cryptographer Matthew Green wrote that independently deployed personal agents such as Muse supply the ingredients of an agent worm, in a post Simon Willison quoted. Green's scenario is a warning, not a report of an attack.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-muse-5m-downloads-dots/
