## Pulse Local is a Mac budgeting app with its AI models on-device

**The free Apple Silicon beta downloads about 2.9 GB of small open models and, its maker says, keeps the ledger and chat questions on the machine.**

Pulse Local, a budgeting app for Apple Silicon Macs, is in beta and runs its chat assistant on small AI models downloaded to the user's own machine. The developer showed it on Hacker News, where the Show HN post had 54 points and 29 comments at the time of reading. Nobody else has reported on it or tested it yet, so what follows is the maker's description from its website.

The page says the app is free with every feature and needs no account. Users can pay a one-time $20, $40 or any amount they choose, and US bank sync through Plaid costs $29 a year for three institutions, with extra institutions at $8 each. Elsewhere, transactions come in by CSV import. A Windows version is planned next, and the chat answers in English only.

The models download once on first start, about 2.9 GB in total. The largest, at 2.5 GB, checks answers about money and can be turned off in settings. The page calls the models small and open and links one tuned model on Hugging Face, but does not name the others. It says the chat answers from the user's own transactions, labels what it included and checks each figure before showing it.

On privacy, the developer says the ledger, the models and the questions never leave the machine. The only regular network request, it says, checks for app updates and carries nothing about the user, and analytics and crash logs are never sent. For bank sync, it says transactions pass through the server's memory without being stored. These are the maker's claims and have not been independently checked.

The page is open about the limits. Small models are weaker than frontier ones on open-ended questions, and a money answer with the checker on takes 12 to 14 seconds. When the two readings disagree, Pulse says it is not sure, according to the page. It also says nothing in the app is financial advice, and Intel Macs are not supported.
