## Anthropic replaces git with its own system as merges pass 30,000 a day

**Anthropic says its monorepo went from 2,500 merges a day in May to over 30,000 in September, and that seven engineers and Claude built a git replacement called Scion in five months.**

Anthropic said on Tuesday that it has moved its monorepo off git onto an in-house version control system called Scion. In a post on its engineering blog, the company says merges rose from about 2,500 a day in May to more than 30,000 a day in September, and that source control had become its biggest development bottleneck.

https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2110047081414987957

The company argues that git's offline-first design works against agent-scale traffic. Anthropic says nearly all its clients are always-online agents and jobs. It says the main-branch history is almost 100 GB and that it ran roughly 25 million CI checkouts a day. Several hundred internal git mirrors were needed at peak.

Scion keeps authoritative state on the server and treats the working copy as optional and sparse. Anthropic says it checks out code about two orders of magnitude faster, from minutes to seconds, uses roughly 200 times less CPU and memory than the old mirrors, and gives CI a 25 percent capacity gain. These figures are Anthropic's own and have not been independently checked.

Seven people built Scion and Antique, a code review tool, plus about half a dozen related systems, the post says. It says Claude wrote most of the code. The engineers' role was often "to tell Claude 'no'," the post adds. It also says an early internal snapshot of Claude Mythos Preview rewrote a handwritten ingestion prototype. The rewrite used 20 times less memory and ran four times faster.

Scion is not available to outside users. The post says it is "by no means a suitable replacement for git for general use." Anthropic says it wants to share its thinking so others can borrow parts of the approach for git. It says it is interested in working with GitHub, GitLab and open-source maintainers. No outside party has yet tested the performance claims.

## Google Play lets US adults install their own AI Studio apps without a computer

**Google says anyone 18 or older in the US can now build an Android app in AI Studio and launch it privately from Play, with sharing among friends promised in the coming months.**

Google announced on Tuesday that people in the US can build a personal Android app in Google AI Studio and install it through Google Play. A standard Google account is enough. According to TechCrunch and The Verge, the change removes the earlier step of connecting a phone to a computer.

Personal apps do not appear in Play search or listings and are visible only to their creator, TechCrunch reports. They must still follow Play's existing policies. The feature is open to users 18 and older in the US at launch, and the report does not mention any fee for building or installing an app.

Google says that in the coming months users will be able to share their apps with a network of trusted friends, with examples such as a family project or a child's sports league. If an app becomes popular, it could be moved to the public store after trust and safety reviews, TechCrunch reports.

Sam Bright, Google's VP and GM for Play and developer ecosystem, said in a press briefing that "the future of apps on Google Play is personal, secure, and built by everyone." The announcement came with other Play changes. They include a Game Finder recommendation tool and deeper Gemini integration. Google has not said how many people have built apps in AI Studio.

## Liquid AI releases LFM2.5-24B-A2B, a 24B model that activates 2.45B parameters

**Liquid AI says its largest release yet runs at 184 tokens a second on an Apple M5 Max and beats Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on agent tasks, but trails it on GPQA.**

Liquid AI released LFM2.5-24B-A2B, a mixture-of-experts reasoning model with 24 billion parameters in total and 2.45 billion active per token, according to its Hugging Face model card. Liquid announced it on X on Tuesday. The model always thinks before answering, handles 131,072 tokens of context and supports 16 languages.

The card describes 40 layers: 30 double-gated short-convolution blocks and 10 grouped-query attention layers. Each block has 64 experts with top-4 routing. Liquid says it trained on about 36 trillion tokens. It then used supervised fine-tuning, on-policy distillation and agentic reinforcement learning. The licence is Liquid's own LFM 1.0, not a standard open-source one.

On benchmarks, Liquid reports 48.09 on LiveClaw against 38.19 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, and 96.75 on IFEval. The same card concedes the Qwen model is ahead on GPQA Diamond, 83.69 to 74.42, and on SWE-Bench Verified, 66.20 to 51.00. These are the company's numbers and have not been independently reproduced.

Liquid says the model runs at 184 tokens a second on an Apple M5 Max and 114 on an AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395, using llama.cpp at Q4_K_M. It claims up to 21,000 output tokens a second on one Nvidia B300 at high concurrency. Weights are on Hugging Face in BF16, FP8, GGUF, ONNX and MLX 4-bit, and work with vLLM, SGLang and LM Studio.
