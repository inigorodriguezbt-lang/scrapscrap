## Reflection's Beam, a 501B open model, ships under Apache 2.0

**Reflection's first model, Beam, has 501 billion parameters, a 1 million-token window and an Apache 2.0 licence. Artificial Analysis ranks it second among US open-weights models, behind only Meta's Muse Spark Open.**

Reflection, a US lab, published Beam on Hugging Face on Thursday, its first model. According to the model announcement, Beam is a text-only mixture-of-experts model with 501 billion parameters in total and 23 billion active per token. It has a 1 million-token context window and an Apache 2.0 licence, with BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 checkpoints.

Reflection says Beam is built for coding and agent work. The company reports 81.6 percent on SWE-Bench Verified, 80.1 percent on Terminal Bench v2.1, 97.7 percent on AIME 2026 and 90.6 percent on GPQA Diamond for the BF16 version. On Humanity's Last Exam it reports 36.0 percent without tools and 51.1 percent with them. Those figures are Reflection's own, and the announcement says nothing about independent verification.

Two outside leaderboards have posted early results. Artificial Analysis says Beam scores 30 on its Intelligence Index, second among US open-weights models behind Meta's Muse Spark Open at 39. Arena says Beam placed 12th among open models in its Agent Arena and 22nd in Code Arena: WebDev, and cautions that the Agent Arena margin of error is wide, at plus or minus 3.84 percent.

Running it is a large job. According to Reflection, the BF16 weights come to about 1 TB, while the NVFP4 version is about 280 GB, and it suggests four B200 GPUs for that version. The model has day-zero support in vLLM, SGLang and Transformers, and Hugging Face lists it on Inference Providers through Baseten, Fireworks and Together.

For people without that hardware, Ollama says Beam is free on its cloud for a limited time, hosted in the US with zero data retention, and can be wired into Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode. Reflection says it will give $10,000 in Hugging Face credits to the best projects built with Beam. Few people have tested it beyond the two leaderboards, so real-world coding results are not yet known.

## OpenAI offers US university labs 30 free GPT-Rosalind seats

**OpenAI's new Rosalind Academic Plan gives approved US university labs up to 30 free seats on its life-sciences model for up to 12 months, with a Workbench that links to chemistry and genomics tools.**

OpenAI has launched the Rosalind Academic Plan, a free programme that gives research labs access to GPT-Rosalind, its model for life sciences. According to The Decoder, it is open for now only to labs at US universities that have no enterprise contract with OpenAI. OpenAI described it as sponsored access to the model and to tools for life sciences research.

The Decoder reports that each approved lab can get up to 30 seats on the Business Standard plan for up to twelve months. API access is not part of the plan. OpenAI vets the institution, the lead researcher and the planned use before approving a lab, so the seats are not open to anyone who applies.

The plan centres on what OpenAI calls the Rosalind Workbench. Through it, GPT-Rosalind connects to specialised tools for medicinal chemistry, genomics and lab experiments, according to The Decoder's account. OpenAI says the model supports deeper reasoning on complex biological questions, a claim about its own product that this paper has not tested.

Approved labs work under safeguards, The Decoder says. For sensitive uses, OpenAI's checks take the approved research context into account, and the company follows up with researchers when something is unclear. After approval, OpenAI monitors how the model is used. It says it does not train on research data by default.
