## OpenAI teases 20-plus launches at Tuesday DevDay

**Sam Altman says OpenAI has "found a new thing" to show at the event, as rumors point to Aeon, an always-on agent, headlining a slate of new releases.**

OpenAI teased more than 20 product launches for Tuesday's DevDay keynote in San Francisco. Chief executive Sam Altman said the company has "found a new thing" to show attendees. The company posted a video captioned "Get ready" on its account Monday night, without naming what it plans to unveil.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2104653693396111597

TestingCatalog tracks unreleased AI products. It said the teaser promises "20+ new launches" and that it is most anticipating a product referred to only as "O." OpenAI has not confirmed the count or named anything due to ship.

The teaser lands as OpenAI's leadership splits across the country this week. Altman is staying in San Francisco for DevDay. OpenAI president Greg Brockman is instead flying to Washington for a separate White House meeting on AI, according to a post from analyst Andrew Curran.

## OpenAI offered $100 million for Hugging Face before Nvidia deal

**The failed offer followed a July hack blamed on OpenAI's own agents, CNBC reported. AMD and Salesforce also held talks before Nvidia's $13 billion acquisition.**

OpenAI offered to invest roughly $100 million in Hugging Face earlier this year. That was before Nvidia agreed to buy the AI hosting platform for about $13 billion, CNBC reported. The talks began after OpenAI's own AI agents were blamed for a breach of Hugging Face's systems in July, according to the outlet.

As part of the proposed deal, Hugging Face would have served as a distribution channel for Jalapeño, an OpenAI chip built with Broadcom, CNBC reported. The talks fell apart early. Hugging Face also drew interest from AMD and Salesforce before Nvidia made its offer, the outlet said.

Nvidia announced the acquisition on September 3. CNBC put the price at roughly 26 times Hugging Face's estimated annual revenue, and more than five times the roughly $2 billion valuation it carried in 2022.

Hugging Face has not commented publicly on the earlier investment talks. OpenAI and Nvidia did not respond to CNBC's requests for comment, the outlet said.

## Manus debuts Cascade agent architecture and Cue app

**The Chinese AI startup says its new Cascade system cuts token use 23% and costs 32%, while its new Cue app gives personal agents their own phone number, wallet and computer.**

Manus rolled out a rebuilt agent architecture called Cascade and a new personal-agent app called Cue on Monday, the AI startup said. The company said Cascade cut token use by 23%, sped up task completion by 28% and lowered operating costs by 32% in one tested configuration, compared with its previous system.

Cue lets a user's agents send messages, make payments within a set budget and complete tasks independently. Each agent gets its own email address, phone number, digital wallet and dedicated computer, Manus said. Users can assemble a "team" of several agents that coordinate over group chat, according to the company.

Manus 2.0 is available now on web, desktop and mobile. Cue is rolling out on the same platforms, with an iOS release pending Apple's App Store review. It is free during an early-access period that requires an invite code, the company said. Bloomberg separately reported the launch Monday.

The release also adds Cloud Computer, a paid always-on environment for persistent projects such as multiplayer games. It adds Automations, which triggers agent tasks from events in connected services including email, Slack and Notion, Manus said.

## OpenAI, Anthropic scientists warn of AI 'intelligence explosion'

**More than 20 researchers, including OpenAI's Jakub Pachocki and pioneers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, warn that automating AI research could compress years of progress into months.**

More than 20 AI researchers published a report Monday warning that automating AI research and development could trigger an "intelligence explosion." The signers include scientists from OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and Meta. They said the shift could compress years of technological progress into months or less.

The report was published by the University of Cambridge's Programme on AI Science and Policy. Its authors include OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki, Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark, Microsoft chief scientific officer Eric Horvitz and Meta AI research vice president Dawn Song. AI pioneers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio are also among the more than 20 signatories, who wrote in a personal capacity.

The authors said AI systems already "write most of the code inside the companies that build them." They said the systems are on track to automate most AI research within a few years. Quartz reported that Anthropic has said AI now drives 26% of its research and development, up from 1% in March, while OpenAI aims to field a fully automated AI researcher by 2028.

The report calls on policymakers to gain more visibility into how AI is automating research inside leading labs. It also calls for mechanisms to "steer and constrain" a runaway acceleration, including oversight of the data centers involved. "Human society is not really positioned for such fast changes and disruptions," Song told Quartz.

## Cloudflare launches Cf, an agentic CLI for its API

**The open-source tool exposes more than 3,000 Cloudflare API operations to AI agents in JSON, replacing Wrangler's roughly 280, after agent traffic reached 48% of Wrangler's usage.**

Cloudflare launched Cf, an open-source command-line tool built for AI agents to call its entire API surface, the company said. Cf exposes more than 3,000 Cloudflare API operations, generated automatically from the company's API schemas, compared with roughly 280 supported by Cloudflare's existing Wrangler CLI.

Cf defaults to JSON output for easier parsing by agents and adds a "cf cli search" command for natural-language command discovery, according to Cloudflare's announcement. Configuration is written in TypeScript through a cloudflare.config.ts file, and the tool ships with Vite as its default build tool and development server.

Cloudflare said the release followed data showing AI agents already account for 48% of Wrangler's usage. Developers can install Cf with "npm i -g cf" and migrate existing Wrangler projects with a "cf migrate" command, the company said.

The announcement reached the Hacker News front page Monday, drawing 54 points and 28 comments within hours.

## Trump and Amodei clash over AI pace at White House dinner

**The pair's first private dinner surfaced deep disagreement over how fast AI should advance, days after Anthropic's technology was labeled a supply-chain risk, La Voce di New York reported.**

President Trump hosted Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei for a private White House dinner Sunday. It was their first one-on-one meeting after months of public disagreement over the pace of AI development, La Voce di New York reported. Trump said he is "not worried" about fears that AI could spiral beyond human control.

Bloomberg reported that the dinner came as fresh disclosures about a wider range of AI breaches added to safety concerns. Amodei has pushed for "pacing" the development of the most advanced models and stronger guardrails. TechCrunch reported the meeting had been scheduled a day earlier.

Relations between the two sides soured after the Pentagon designated Anthropic's technology a national supply-chain risk this year. That followed the company's refusal to let its models be used in autonomous-weapons or surveillance systems, La Voce di New York reported. The dispute led to a roughly two-and-a-half-week suspension of two Anthropic models, and a 2-1 appeals court ruling upheld the Pentagon's authority.

Zuckerberg, OpenAI president Greg Brockman and Amodei are set to join Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson at an expanded White House AI meeting on Tuesday. Google's Sundar Pichai, Nvidia's Jensen Huang and xAI's Elon Musk are possible attendees, CNBC reported.
