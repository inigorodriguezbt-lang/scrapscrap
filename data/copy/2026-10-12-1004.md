## Researchers teach a Unitree G1 humanoid to catch thrown boxes from camera alone

**A University of Washington-led paper says its Reflex controller let a Unitree G1 catch human-thrown boxes 65 percent of the time at 2.5 metres, using only an onboard depth camera.**

A team led by researchers at the University of Washington has published Reflex, a framework that teaches a humanoid robot to catch boxes thrown at it using only a camera on its own torso. The paper appeared on Hugging Face's daily papers on Monday with 55 upvotes. The authors, including Ranjay Krishna, list affiliations at Stanford, USC, the National University of Singapore and the Allen Institute for AI.

According to the paper, earlier systems often leaned on motion capture, marked objects or a hand-only controller. Reflex instead trains in three stages. A reinforcement-learning policy first learns whole-body motion from human references. A second stage compresses delayed, partial observations of the box into an eight-number description of its motion, with latency randomised between 20 and 60 milliseconds. A vision network then learns to predict that description from depth images.

On a real Unitree G1 with a torso-mounted RGB-D camera, the authors report catching human-thrown boxes 65 percent of the time at 2.5 metres, 40 percent at 5 metres and 15 percent at 7 metres, with 20 trials per distance. Three human catchers managed 85.0, 68.3 and 28.3 percent at the same ranges, so the robot reached 76 and 59 percent of human success at the first two distances, the paper says.

In simulation the visual controller caught 85.7 percent of throws, against 87.9 percent for a version given the box's true state. The authors also ran a 20-trial task chain in which success fell from 65 percent after the catch to 25 percent after carrying the box, handing it over and returning it. Their listed limits: one task, wide error bars of up to about 20 points, and a controller that is effectively blind to box size.

The paper links no code or project page, and no outside group has reproduced the results. All figures here are the authors' own, from a preprint that has not been peer reviewed, so they may change.

## Kling team says new diffusion training method cuts GPU hours 12-fold

**KlingAI researchers say DiffusionART matched rival reinforcement-learning methods on a 13B video model with about 12 times fewer GPU hours, and removes the need to tune EMA.**

Researchers from KlingAI have posted DiffusionART, a method for fine-tuning image and video generators with reinforcement learning from black-box rewards. The paper, dated Monday, drew 67 upvotes on Hugging Face's daily papers. It targets a trade-off. Reverse-process methods such as Flow-GRPO assign credit poorly and run slowly, the authors write. Forward-process methods such as DiffusionNFT are quick but unstable unless an exponential moving average is carefully tuned.

The authors argue the instability comes from a force in NFT-style objectives that pulls the forward and reverse processes apart. Positive and negative training only partly cancels it. DiffusionART instead pools each prompt's rewards into one shared improving direction. It adds a second, trust-region-masked update, which the paper claims removes the EMA tuning. It needs no differentiable reward model and could work with fewer samples per prompt, the authors suggest.

On text-to-video with HunyuanVideo-13B, the authors report reaching the late-stage mean reward of DiffusionNFT and Flow-GRPO-Fast with 12.02 and 11.72 times fewer GPU hours, with mean training rewards 28.8 and 53.0 percent higher. On text-to-image with SD3.5-M, they say it reached Flow-GRPO's step-500 reward 10.0 times faster on GenEval, 5.2 on OCR and 9.6 on PickScore.

For image-to-video on Wan2.1-I2V-14B, the paper says a motion-quality score of 6 took 45.8 GPU hours against 220.3 for Flow-GRPO-Fast. A pairwise-feedback variant came close to scalar rewards, and, the authors say, using two samples rather than eight cut compute by 3.4 times on video, the authors say.

The page we read did not mention released code, and the numbers come from the authors' own experiments on a preprint. No independent group has reported reproducing them, so the claimed speedups may not hold elsewhere.

## Palantir CTO says Pentagon already replaced Anthropic

**Palantir's Shyam Sankar told CBS the Pentagon's drop of Anthropic had no operational impact because it "has already been replaced", and that AI makers own the liability for defective models.**

Palantir chief technology officer Shyam Sankar said the Pentagon's decision to stop using Anthropic products has had no operational impact, in an interview CBS News published on Sunday from Face the Nation. Asked about the move, he said the Defense Department "has a wide range of model providers" and that Anthropic's tools were "already replaced". Control over models, he argued, is institutions' "alpha", and none should depend on one provider.

On the Iran campaign, Sankar said "targeting is a process" and that AI does not pick targets: "That's not what's happening." He argued speed is the benefit. Executing many strikes in a day with one person working in hours, he said, "provides you a significant battlefield advantage", according to the CBS transcript.

Asked about the strike on a school in Minab that reportedly killed at least 123 children, Sankar said "we should wait for the final report". He rejected Bloomberg's reporting that personnel over-relied on AI, saying "The Bloomberg reporting is wrong", and attributed the cause to "human error". He said Palantir then built extra AI agents to double-check work. He offered no evidence for the cause in the interview.

On liability, Sankar said faulty AI is "a defective product" and that responsibility lies with "the AI companies of course". He used a car analogy: a defective car is the maker's problem, but driving it into a neighbour's house is the driver's. He added that existing law gives the field "a massive running head start".

These are Sankar's statements as published by CBS. Palantir is a Pentagon contractor, and neither the Defense Department nor Anthropic is quoted in the transcript. The paper has not independently confirmed the Minab findings or the replacement claim, which remain his account.
