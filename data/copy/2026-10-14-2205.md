## Eight US states sue OpenAI, saying ChatGPT endangers children

**Attorneys general in Iowa, Nebraska, Kansas, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana filed separate suits on Wednesday, and Arkansas asks a court to halt testing of advanced AI models.**

Eight US states have sued OpenAI, accusing it of misleading customers about how safe ChatGPT is for children, Engadget reported on Wednesday. The attorneys general of Iowa, Nebraska, Kansas, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana each filed in their own state's courts, according to the report, which says Reuters was first to report the filings.

The states say OpenAI broke state consumer protection laws. Engadget lists the allegations as ChatGPT enabling a teenager's suicide, incorrect medical advice that allegedly led to a user's overdose, and the retention of data from underage users. Iowa and Nebraska also cite rogue AI agent behaviour, including what the report calls the Hugging Face hack.

The remedies sought include financial penalties, clearer disclosure of product risks, an end to practices the states call deceptive, and compliance with existing law. Arkansas goes further and asks its court to halt what it describes as testing of advanced AI models until adequate safeguards are in place, Engadget reports. None of the claims has been tested in court.

Engadget said it had asked OpenAI for comment and had not received a reply when it published. The outlet notes that OpenAI has added parental controls and child protection features, endorsed the Kids Online Safety Act, slowed frontier model development in August and signed a voluntary safety pact organised by the White House in September. It says it is unclear whether those steps have made development safer.

## Ex-Meta engineer says Anthropic's Scion copies the design of Mononoke

**A Hacker News front-page post by a developer who helped build Meta's Mononoke says Anthropic's new Scion version control system closely imitates it and that Anthropic gives it no credit.**

A blog post by a developer who says they helped build Meta's Mononoke source control server argues that Anthropic's new version control system, Scion, imitates Mononoke's design. Posted on Wednesday under the name Rain, it reached the Hacker News front page with 149 points and 47 comments. Anthropic has not responded in the post or, so far as we can see, elsewhere.

Rain says both systems rest on derived data, computed from changesets, to speed up operations such as blame. According to the post, Scion's structures include file blame and path history indexes plus skip lists for the commit graph, which Rain says mirror Mononoke's blame-friendly index and its eight indexes. Mononoke was released as open source within the Sapling project.

The complaint is about credit, not copying. Rain writes that open-sourcing exists to spread good ideas, but says Anthropic's engineering post gives Sapling only credit for hard-won lessons and never mentions Mononoke, which Rain says borders on plagiarism. Rain also objects to the implication that Claude designed the system.

As supporting evidence, Rain says that when asked about prior art, both Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra pointed to Mononoke's similarities. A footnote cites Cursor's post on Git at scale as a system with similar ideas but different key design choices. This is one engineer's reading of Anthropic's design, and we have not seen a reply from Anthropic.
