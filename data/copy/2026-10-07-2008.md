## ChatGPT's GPT-6 update answers with charts, buttons and playable tools

**OpenAI says GPT-6 with Intelligent UI reaches paid ChatGPT users today and Free and Go users tomorrow, with calculators, forms and games built into the chat.**

OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 in ChatGPT on Wednesday with a feature it calls Intelligent UI. Answers can now include graphics, charts, tappable buttons and forms rather than text alone, according to the company's announcement on X. OpenAI says paid users get it today and Free and Go users starting tomorrow.

https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2107894997538525580

Paid tiers run on GPT-6 Sol and Free and Go tiers on GPT-6 Luna, both tuned for everyday conversation, OpenAI said. The update applies only to the Chat tab. The models behind Work and Codex are not changing. OpenAI's demo posts show interactive tools, such as a budget planning calculator and a game, built inside the conversation.

https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2107895002022187411

The Decoder reports that GPT-6 can also start answering while it is still thinking, which OpenAI says cuts wait times by 44 percent. According to the Decoder, OpenAI's internal tests found GPT-6 beat its predecessor, GPT-5.6, on difficult web searches with this method. The article does not say how large the tests were or how the 44 percent was measured.

The commentator mark_k describes the launch as day three of OpenAI's "28 Days of Improvements". A post from the thsottiaux account, which has been announcing OpenAI releases, puts the ChatGPT audience at 1.2 billion users. That figure comes from OpenAI-linked posts, and none of them gives a breakdown by tier.

No one outside OpenAI has yet published tests of how well Intelligent UI works in practice. The Decoder does not describe how Sol and Luna differ in capability, or whether free users get the same interactive features as paying ones. It adds that Google shipped a similar feature in Gemini in May under the name Neural Expressive.

## Microsoft and Nvidia pitch Windows laptops built to run big models locally

**Nvidia says its RTX Spark chip offers up to 128GB of unified memory and 1 petaflop of FP4 compute, in Surface and partner PCs with preorders open today.**

At a Microsoft event in San Francisco on Wednesday, Nvidia chief Jensen Huang and Microsoft chief Satya Nadella set out plans for AI agents to run on Windows PCs, Nvidia said in a blog post. The headline hardware is the Surface Laptop Ultra, which The Verge says starts at $2,599 and is powered by Nvidia's Arm-based RTX Spark chip.

Nvidia lists RTX Spark as a Blackwell GPU with up to 6,144 cores, a Grace CPU with up to 20 cores, up to 128GB of unified memory and up to 1 petaflop of FP4 AI performance. Nvidia says the chip could run large models locally without sending data to the cloud. Those figures are Nvidia's own and have not been independently tested.

Nvidia says laptop preorders opened Wednesday, with availability on October 16. Compact desktops follow in November. It names eight system makers, among them Dell, HP, Lenovo and Microsoft. Nvidia also announced a preview of DGX Station for Windows, built on the GB300 chip with 748GB of memory.

On the software side, Nadella posted that MAI-Code-1.1 Flash, a 137-billion-parameter coding model with a 256K context window, is now optimised to run on a PC. He added that GitHub Copilot can hand work to local models. Microsoft says Hybrid Intelligence lets Copilot use local models "when it makes sense".

https://x.com/satyanadella/status/2107919668514295908

Microsoft also announced general availability of Microsoft Execution Containers, which Nvidia says let agents run securely under operating-system control. Georgi Gerganov, creator of llama.cpp, wrote that it was "very cool" to see the project on the big stage. No independent benchmarks of local coding models on RTX Spark have been published.

https://x.com/ggerganov/status/2107908265032229261

## Anthropic halves Sonnet 5.5 cache prices and adds API credits for Max

**Anthropic says cache reads fall from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens, about 20% off typical agent work, and Max 20x subscribers get $200 a month in API credit.**

Anthropic cut the price of Claude Sonnet 5.5 cache reads from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens on Wednesday, alongside the launch of Claude Haiku 5.5. The company says the change makes Sonnet 5.5 about 20 percent cheaper on most agentic work. VentureBeat notes that actual savings depend on how much stored context an application reuses.

The cut also reaches some existing Azure and Google Cloud customers, which VentureBeat says will receive it over the following days. Sonnet 5.5's other prices, listed on Anthropic's page, stay at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Anthropic's page lists Haiku 5.5 cache reads at $0.01 per million tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens.

Anthropic is also adding monthly API credits for subscribers. Max 5x gets $100, Max 20x gets $200, and Team plans get up to $500 shared among users, according to VentureBeat. The credits roll out this week and can be spent on any model through Anthropic's platform. The article does not explain how Team credits are split or whether unused credit rolls over.

Cursor said it passed the lower price on, writing that Sonnet 5.5 cache reads are now $0.10 per million tokens in its product. The move comes as OpenAI offers GPT-6 Luna at $0.10 and $0.50 per million input and output tokens, the same base rates as Haiku 5.5, VentureBeat reports. It describes the figures as price comparisons, not capability ones.

Anthropic has not said how many developers use prompt caching or how much revenue the cut gives up. The 20 percent figure is the company's own estimate, and no independent measurement of agent costs after the change has been published yet.

## Healthleap raises $38 million for AI that flags undiagnosed inpatients

**The South African-founded startup says its software runs in more than 50 hospitals, up from three a year ago, and reads every adult inpatient's record each night.**

Healthleap, a startup that makes AI screening software for hospitals, has raised about $38 million, TechCrunch reports. The money is an $8 million seed round co-led by Sequoia Capital and First Round Capital, and a $30 million Series A led by Hummingbird Ventures. The valuation was not disclosed.

According to TechCrunch, the software connects to a hospital's electronic health record system and uses language models to pull signals from clinicians' notes. It combines them with lab results and vitals in risk models, scores every adult inpatient each night, and gives care teams a ranking each morning in their existing workflow.

The company says the tool does not diagnose and only highlights patients for additional review. Malnutrition and delirium are live, while aspiration pneumonia, pressure ulcers and heart-failure readmission risk are in clinical validation. Healthleap plans to cover more than 40 conditions and move into outpatient and home care.

Healthleap says it is deployed in more than 50 hospitals, up from three about a year ago, with named customers including Penn Medicine, Cedars-Sinai, Intermountain, Houston Methodist and Emory Healthcare. It says revenue grew more than tenfold in a year, without giving figures. Contracts run three years, priced by licensed beds, with a guaranteed-return component.

Healthleap says Penn's malnutrition programme produced $23.8 million in annualised impact, $6.3 million from added reimbursement and $17.5 million from shorter stays. Those numbers come from the company, and TechCrunch says it has not independently verified them. Founded in 2022 by siblings Jemima and Josiah Meyer, it began as a nutrition tool for dietitians.

## Haiku 5.5 is cheap per token but talkative, Artificial Analysis finds

**The benchmarking firm scored Claude Haiku 5.5 at 43 on its Intelligence Index and says it spends about 162,000 output tokens per task, roughly three times GPT-6 Luna.**

Artificial Analysis, which runs an independent AI index, scored Anthropic's new Claude Haiku 5.5 at 43 on its Intelligence Index, up 26 points from the previous Haiku a year ago. It says Haiku 5.5 is the first Haiku with Anthropic's effort settings and adaptive thinking, and that Anthropic has introduced tiered pricing for it.

The headline price is $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, the same as GPT-6 Luna and a tenth of the previous Haiku, Artificial Analysis says. Above 100,000 tokens the price rises fivefold to $0.50 and $2.50. The firm says its site does not yet reflect tiered pricing, so its cost figures are provisional.

The catch, according to Artificial Analysis, is verbosity. At maximum effort Haiku 5.5 uses about 162,000 output tokens per Intelligence Index task, more than Claude Opus 5.5 at maximum and about three times GPT-6 Luna. Across effort settings it says Haiku 5.5 uses more output tokens than Luna to reach similar scores, which narrows the price advantage per finished task.

Anthropic's own chart tells a different story. Its release page reports Haiku 5.5 at 72.4 percent on OSWorld 2.1 against 48.9 percent for Luna, and 39.2 percent on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against 16.4 percent. Those are Anthropic's own figures, and the company concedes Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 remain better for complex agentic coding.

Neither side has published the cost of a complete task for both models at matched quality, so it may be unclear which is cheaper in practice. Artificial Analysis says its effort-by-effort comparison is on its site, and Arena says it has put Haiku 5.5 into its Agent Arena, with scores to come.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/haiku-55-artificial-analysis-token-use/
