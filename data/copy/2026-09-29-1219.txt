McDonald's uses AI to set menu prices at 14,000 US restaurants, Reuters says

PostHog released Jeeves, an MIT-licensed 9-billion-parameter decision model built on Qwen3.5 that reasons before answering yes/no and rating questions, and says it scores 0.889 on unseen data against 0.857 for Jev.

A follow-up to the Jev decision-model family the paper covered earlier today; the benchmark numbers are PostHog's own, and the repo had 29 stars when read.


EliseAI, which sells AI tools to housing and health-care firms, raised $350 million at a $4 billion valuation, up from $2.2 billion after its $250 million round in August 2025, Fortune reported.

The valuation has nearly doubled in a year for an applied-AI company selling to landlords and clinics rather than building frontier models.


TestingCatalog's daily brief says Claude Haiku 5.5 is still weeks away after Sonnet 5.5, and that Cursor already lists the new Sonnet's slug; it also reports a possible $500 OpenAI plan.

TestingCatalog reports from app strings and tests, so the Haiku timing and the $500 plan are unconfirmed until Anthropic and OpenAI say so.


The Financial Times reports that UK AI companies that have raised a combined $5 billion are pressing the prime minister to end non-compete rules so they can hire on terms closer to those in the US.

It shows the talent race for AI researchers reaching into UK employment law, with the firms framing it as a competitiveness issue.
