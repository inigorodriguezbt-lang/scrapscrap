## Meta launches Muse Wonder, claiming a lead over Opus 5.5 on long coding

**Meta says Muse Wonder Full scores 57.5% on SWE-Marathon against 46.3% for Claude Opus 5.5, and Artificial Analysis ranks it second overall, two points behind.**

Meta released two models on Monday, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, which it calls its most capable yet and built for "personal and professional agentic tasks", according to its research blog. Mark Zuckerberg wrote that it has been just over a year since Meta Superintelligence Labs began, and that Muse Wonder "brings us to the frontier". TechCrunch calls it Meta's first attempt at a frontier model.

https://x.com/AIatMeta/status/2109691148943999167

https://x.com/finkd/status/2109689307288240154

Muse Wonder is available now through the Meta Model API and Muse Code, Meta's coding product. Muse Wonder Full, the higher-effort version, is available only to Muse Code subscribers and is not served through the API, TechCrunch reports. Meta says Muse Wonder will soon power its Muse agent, starting with subscribers. TechCrunch gives the API price as $4.00 per million input tokens and $20.00 per million output tokens.

The headline numbers are Meta's own. Rohan Paul, an AI commentator, relayed Meta's chart: on SWE-Marathon, an ultra-long engineering test, Full scores 57.5 against 46.3 for Claude Opus 5.5 and 42.5 for GPT-6 Astra. On DeepSWE 1.1 it scores 79.0 against 74.2 for Opus 5.5, which Meta says came from independent third-party runs. Meta also names Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark and a memory test, but its blog text gives no scores.

The one outside measurement so far is Artificial Analysis, which says Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full score 52 and 56 on its Intelligence Index v4.3 at maximum reasoning effort, up from 48 for Muse Spark 1.3, released six weeks earlier. The firm says Full ranks in the top three on six of ten component evaluations, first on CritPt and GDP.pdf with narrow leads, and lifts Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 33% to 57%. Commentators put Full two points behind Opus 5.5 overall.

Meta pitches two features for agents. Active Memory keeps user context across conversations and retrieves it when relevant; Active Focus keeps a model working through blockers and asks for clarification before important actions, the blog says. Meta says the models have its strongest safeguards yet against chemical, biological, nuclear and cyber misuse, and that a full safety report will follow. The page gives no context length.

Treat the comparisons with caution. The benchmark and "state-of-the-art" claims come from Meta, TechCrunch has not verified them, and the Artificial Analysis result covers only part of the picture: commentators noted that Opus still leads on ProgramBench and on the overall index. Hands-on tests from people outside Meta have only just started to appear.

## Subquadratic launches SubQ Snap, claiming 100,000 tokens read in a second

**Subquadratic says SubQ Snap handles 384,000 tokens of context, costs $0.05 per million input tokens and beats its own Jev model on 13 of 16 enterprise tests.**

Subquadratic launched SubQ Snap on Monday, which the San Francisco company calls a multimodal "decision model" for fast, long-context choices inside agent workflows. A decision model reads supplied context against a defined question and returns a structured answer, such as a choice, score or label, instead of open-ended text, according to the company's page. It is a separate product from SubQ, the long-context language model it announced earlier this year.

Subquadratic says Snap takes up to 384,000 tokens of context, against the 32,000 it says current decision models see. The company says it can process 100,000 tokens and return a decision in about one second, though its page gives no latency table. It suggests uses such as curating context for coding agents, recommendation systems and support-queue triage. The company credits its Subquadratic Sparse Attention method for the efficiency.

Pricing is listed at $0.05 per million input tokens, with output free, and the company offers 100 million free tokens to the first 10,000 users. Access runs through console.subq.ai, with enterprise requests handled by sales. Reports of the launch describe access as limited.

The benchmarks are the company's own, and compare Snap with Jev, a model the page never explains. Subquadratic says Snap scores 61.7 on its Decision Index against 57.9 for Jev, 74.0 against 48.0 on a multi-document test, and leads on 13 of 16 enterprise benchmarks, averaging 67.5 to 61.6. It says Snap trails on three, by 7.6 points on When2Call.

No independent results have been published for Snap. The page cites no outside evaluator for it, and the "multimodal" claim does not say which modalities are supported. Subquadratic's earlier 12-million-token SubQ drew scepticism from commentators who said its numbers were not reproducible, and one report says its chief technology officer confirmed that model builds on someone else's open-source weights.
