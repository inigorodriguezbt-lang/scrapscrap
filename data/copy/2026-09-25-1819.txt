OpenAI says Proaction's fleet agents boost sales 60%

Proaction built voice and computer-use agents on Codex, GPT-Live-1 and GPT-6 Astra to call drivers and repair shops, OpenAI said in a customer case study.

OpenAI published a case study saying Proaction, a fleet-management startup, built agents on Codex, GPT-Live-1 and GPT-6 Astra. The company said the agents raised Proaction's sales 60% and saved more than 75 hours of staff time, figures that are OpenAI's own.

GPT-Live-1, OpenAI's voice model, lets the agents call drivers and repair shops and help arrange vehicle maintenance. A person steps in when needed, OpenAI's developer account said separately on X. GPT-6 Astra, the company's computer-use model, is credited with shortening the automated runs needed to finish the same tasks.

https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2103528579212669374

OpenAI did not say how it measured the sales and hours figures, over what period, or how large Proaction is. Case studies like this one are marketing material commissioned by OpenAI, and the results may not be typical of other customers.

GPT-6 Astra is part of OpenAI's GPT-6 line of models built for agentic, computer-using tasks, distinct from GPT-Live-1's voice-focused design. Proaction is the latest customer OpenAI has featured pairing the two models with Codex, its coding tool, in one workflow.

Meta's Muse gives every user a persistent cloud computer

The AI agent, now Meta's most-promoted app, runs each user's own Ubuntu Linux VM, and reporters say prodding it also exposes files the company did not mean to show.

Meta's Muse agent gives every user a free, persistent cloud computer running Ubuntu Linux. Users can install software and browse the web inside it. They can also write and compile code, Meta's David Singleton, the company's vice president of engineering, said.

Muse has drawn more than 500,000 users in its first week. It reached number one on the US App Store on September 18 and the Google Play Store the next day, The Decoder and TechCrunch reported. Download estimates vary by tracker. Sensor Tower put the total at 3.4 million and Apptopia at 4.3 million. Appfigures's estimate was lower, near 2.3 million.

Meta has pushed the app hard internally, TechCrunch reported. House ads for Muse began a day after launch, and by September 22 the app ranked among Meta's top 10 by ad spend. Promotion also expanded to outside platforms such as Reddit and TikTok, the outlet said. Daily active users rose 27% in a single day after Meta's Connect announcements.

Each user's session runs in an isolated "Runtime Cell" with its own file system. An external process Meta calls Sentinel watches for risky actions from outside that cell. A built-in file explorer also lets users inspect every file, including Muse's own binaries. The Verge reported that prodding the system exposed details it "wasn't supposed to reveal," without saying what those details were.

The writer John Gruber, quoted by developer Simon Willison, called Muse "the first consumer-accessible agentic AI system." He said its friendly packaging undersells the risk. "I don't think people realize how powerful — and thus dangerous — Muse is, especially if it's running on your Mac," Gruber wrote.

Perceptron releases Mk1.5 model for embodied AI agents

The startup says one model flies drones and controls quadrupeds without retraining, and claims its egocentric-vision score beats rivals that cost 25 times as much to run.

Perceptron AI released Mk1.5, a single intelligence layer for embodied agents, the company said. It can fly drones, control quadruped robots, power smart glasses, track objects and reason visually, across devices, without platform-specific retraining, according to Perceptron.

https://x.com/perceptroninc/status/2103508527813669193

A Perceptron researcher posting as AkshatS07 said Mk1.5 is the startup's "first step towards agentic physical world foundation models." It draws on new work in reasoning and reinforcement learning, the researcher said. The model responds 2 to 5 times faster than the previous Mk1 while matching or beating its accuracy, according to the same post.

A second researcher, posting as ArmenAgha, said Mk1.5 posts the company's best score yet on egocentric understanding, the task of reading video from a first-person camera. The model beats rival systems that cost 25 times as much to run, the researcher said. The same account said Mk1.5 combines audio and video training, demonstrated by predicting when a passing truck is closest to the camera using sound alone.

The company said Mk1.5 can also call external tools, such as web and reverse-image search. It can split tasks across sub-agents it dispatches itself, illustrated in a demo identifying allergens in a basket of snacks. None of the performance claims has been independently verified.

Appeals court upholds Pentagon's risk designation for Anthropic

A divided panel sided with the Trump administration, letting the Pentagon keep treating Anthropic's Claude models as a supply-chain risk barred from military contracts.

A federal appeals court upheld the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk. A divided panel sided with the Trump administration over the company's objection, Wired reported. CNBC separately reported that the panel found Claude's integration with Department of Defense systems to be a national-security risk under federal statute.

Anthropic had argued the designation violated multiple of its rights, Wired reported. Neither outlet detailed which specific claims the panel rejected, or the judges' vote breakdown.

Anthropic said in a statement, quoted by the X account AndrewCurran_, that it "remains confident in its position and is considering its options." Neither Anthropic nor the Pentagon has said whether Anthropic plans a further appeal.

The ruling drew wide attention beyond the outlets that first reported it, reaching 196 points and 256 comments on Hacker News' front page within hours of publication.

SpaceX details $90 billion AI hub buildout in Memphis

A new project website lays out Colossus 2's grid battery, water plant and substations, including what SpaceX calls the largest grid-connected battery pack in the US.

SpaceX's AI division published a new website detailing its Colossus 2 training cluster in Tennessee and Mississippi. The site says the company has invested more than $90 billion in the region since 2024, according to the X account SawyerMerritt, which linked to it. The company said the buildout supports 7,500 local jobs.

https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2103516363470032947

The site says Tesla Megapacks will supply Colossus 2 with 3.3 gigawatt-hours of storage, enough to power Memphis for two hours. The company calls it the largest grid-connected battery pack in the US. SpaceX also said it is spending $360 million on a water-recycling plant meant to process up to 10 million gallons of wastewater a day.

The company said it is paying for two new substations, worth $35 million and $20 million, at no cost to the local utility or homeowners. It is also funding sound walls and quieter turbines around the site. Under an order with Mississippi's environmental regulator, it must remove all temporary generators by July 2027, and says it is already ahead of that schedule.

None of the figures have been independently verified. SpaceX did not publish them through its own account. They were relayed secondhand by a single X account that linked to the new site.

Anthropic says Claude cracked a nine-loop physics problem

Claude spent about a week computing a particle-physics calculation past the previous eight-loop record, and Stanford physicist Lance Dixon, who set that record, verified the result.

Anthropic said its Claude Science system computed a nine-loop scattering-amplitude calculation in theoretical particle physics. That is one loop past the previous record of eight, set by Stanford's Lance Dixon and collaborators. Dixon independently verified Claude's result, Anthropic said, after physicist and science writer Matthew von Hippel challenged AI companies last month to attempt the problem.

https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2103541577083719888

Anthropic researchers Liam Fitzpatrick and Siddharth Mishra-Sharma gave Claude a single prompt describing the six-particle amplitude. They told it to keep working with updates every four to six hours, the company said. Claude ran largely unsupervised for about a week on 96 parallel CPUs. It solved the problem with two methods: a bootstrap approach and a separate form-factor technique.

The full computation cost a few thousand dollars, Anthropic said, with the bootstrap method alone costing about $100. Within days of Anthropic's result, a group led by Song He at the Chinese Academy of Sciences reported similar progress. It said it had computed parts of the same nine-loop result using GPT-6 alongside human researchers.

Von Hippel wrote that Claude relied on established methods rather than inventing new ones. That, he said, suggests "there is more low-hanging fruit out there than you'd expect." Claude also reached the correct answer in a single attempt, he noted, where such calculations typically demand several rounds of debugging.
