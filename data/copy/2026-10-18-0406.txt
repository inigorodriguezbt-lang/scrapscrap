Meta removes terms that let it train AI on its advertisers' websites

Meta deleted a clause saying it could use any content from a business account's domains to train its products, after legal experts called it a remarkable provision.


Altman says OpenAI will build a humanoid robot, with a demo hoped for 2027

Asked on a podcast whether OpenAI would make a humanoid, Sam Altman said "We will." He told Fortune last month to expect "a cool demo in 2027", with robots on streets years away.

Andrew Curran posted that three unannounced models, aurora-red-alpha, aurora-green-alpha and aurora-blue-alpha, have appeared in the OpenAI API. OpenAI has not commented, and nothing is known about what they are.

Unlisted alpha names turning up in an API often precede a launch, but this rests on one account's post and no one has confirmed it.


Bloomberg reported, citing a filing, that Disney agreed to hire more than 60 Character.AI staff and pay them about $180 million over three years, plus $30 million in retention awards, if a bankruptcy judge approves.

It shows Disney buying a failed chatbot company's people rather than its product, a month after the startup's Chapter 11 filing.


Tom's Hardware reports that HUMXN is offering free plumbing, HVAC and electrical repairs in Minneapolis, Chicago and Miami, while wearable sensors record the technicians' work as training data for robots.

Home repair is hard to simulate, so the company is paying for real footage; Tom's Hardware's report is the only detailed account found so far.


decomp.dev, the public index of game decompilation projects, has frozen its leaderboard. Its creator told Tom's Hardware that AI-driven forks, which finish other teams' work, have turned progress into a hollow numbers game.

It is a small, concrete case of AI-generated output undermining a hobbyist community's way of measuring its own work.
