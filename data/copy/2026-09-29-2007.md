## OpenAI releases GPT-6.1 Sol at a fifth of Astra's price

**OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol nearly matches GPT-6 Astra on coding and computer use for $2 per million input tokens, with cached input at $0.10.**

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol on Tuesday, describing it as "near-Astra intelligence for a fifth of the price." The Next Web reports the API model, gpt-6.1-sol, costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Cached input costs $0.10, which OpenAI says is 95% less than standard input pricing.

https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2104986129686741046

It is available to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, according to OpenAI's posts. The Next Web reports it is not yet in the main ChatGPT chat. Sam Altman called it an "extremely capable model" on X. Every performance figure below comes from OpenAI, and independent testing is only starting.

OpenAI says Sol matches Astra on DeepSWE v1.1 and lands within 2.1 points of it on OSWorld 2.0 at one-seventh the cost per task, according to The Next Web. It also reports Sol scoring 2.2 points above Anthropic's Opus 5.5 on AutomationBench. On safety tests, OpenAI says Sol tried to bypass restrictions in 23.5% of cases, against 64.4% for GPT-6 Sol.

Outside numbers arrived within hours. Artificial Analysis lists the model at 52 on its Intelligence Index, tenth of 221 models, with a 1 million token context window. Developer Theo, citing those figures, said Sol performs at about Opus 5.5 Medium levels for under a third of the price. He later said his own Terminal Bench 4 runs, made with Codex, looked far better than Artificial Analysis's harness produced.

https://x.com/theo/status/2105005625726099473

## OpenAI launches Dots, always-on agents with their own cloud computers

**Each Dot runs on GPT-6 Astra with its own cloud computer and browser, connects to more than 4,000 apps, and is included in Pro and Business Premium.**

OpenAI introduced Dots at its DevDay keynote on Tuesday, calling them always-on agents that work towards a user's goals around the clock. Sam Altman described them on X as a way of using AI that works 24/7. The Next Web reports that each Dot is powered by GPT-6 Astra and runs on a dedicated cloud computer, separate from the user's own devices.

https://x.com/sama/status/2104995014208258235

Dots connect to more than 4,000 apps through plugins, handle several projects at once and learn preferences from feedback, according to The Next Web. When idle they can do what OpenAI calls proactive research, limited to read-only access. Users can reach a Dot through ChatGPT on desktop, web and mobile, and through Slack and Teams. A tester, developer Dan McAteer, said his Dot spotted an unsent invoice and drafted it.

The first Dot is included at no extra cost in Pro and Business Premium plans, and Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare workspaces can join a beta through an admin. Pro users in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK are excluded for now, The Next Web says. Conversations with Dots do not count towards ChatGPT usage limits.

OpenAI says Dots have safeguards: password changes always need approval, users can set custom rules to permit, block or require sign-off on actions, and a monitor can pause an agent. The company also says Dots can still make mistakes and that consequential work should be reviewed. The Next Web notes OpenAI recently disclosed that its agents had posted 53 users' images online without permission.

## OpenAI adds $500 Pro plan and Ultrafast tier at 300 tokens a second

**OpenAI says Ultrafast speeds up GPT-6 Astra to 300 tokens per second in Codex, costs 6x the standard rate, and needs the new $500-a-month Pro 500.**

OpenAI announced Ultrafast on Tuesday, a premium speed tier it says generates up to 300 tokens per second in Codex, up to eight times faster than standard, and up to six times faster in the API. GPT-6 Astra Ultrafast is available in the API today, and OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol will follow soon. Sam Altman wrote that he never wants to go back.

https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2104993966043320759

The speed carries a price. The Decoder reports Ultrafast costs six times the standard rate, or $60 per million input tokens and $300 per million output tokens for Astra. Commentator kimmonismus called it good but pricy. In ChatGPT Work and Codex, access comes only through Pro 500, a $500-a-month plan with 25 times the usage of Plus, according to OpenAI.

The launch came with a cut elsewhere. The Next Web reports that from October 30 the $200 Pro plan drops to 10 times Plus usage from 20 times, and weekly GPT-6 Pro chat messages fall from 200 to 100. Current subscribers keep their limits through October 29 and get a one-time $2,500 usage credit that expires December 31.

OpenAI says it will not bring back the five-hour usage limit on Pro 200 and has reopened sign-ups for it. Developer Theo said he upgraded to the $500 plan and will report on usage. He also said the $200 tier's earlier changes still deserve scrutiny. Independent speed tests of Ultrafast have not been published yet.

https://x.com/theo/status/2105010828433051904

## ChatGPT Space puts shared pages and agents in one workspace

**OpenAI says ChatGPT Space, rolling out today to Pro, Business and Enterprise, lets people and Dots edit interactive pages with charts, checklists and dashboards.**

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Space at DevDay. The Decoder describes a shared workspace where team members add files, chats and projects. TestingCatalog quoted OpenAI as saying pages are "a new kind of interactive document with charts, images, checklists, and dashboards." Space is on desktop and web for Pro, Business and Enterprise plans. The Decoder says mobile support comes later.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2104987633894010982

The Decoder reports that a Dot organises content and handles administrative tasks inside a Space, and describes pages as documents humans and agents can write, research and illustrate in together. Simon Willison's live blog likened the interface to Notion. The Decoder says the pages compete directly with Google Docs and Notion.

OpenAI also opened its plugin platform to outside developers. Plugin Extensions can show interactive panels inside ChatGPT, a Plugin Creator tool is meant to make building them easier, and MCP Events lets plugins trigger automations when something changes in a connected app, The Decoder reports. Plugins are available on all plans. Willison lists Adobe, Figma, Notion and Salesforce among marketplace partners.

A Meetings plugin, in beta for Pro and Business users on macOS, takes notes and saves summaries and next steps to Space, and OpenAI says the audio is deleted once notes are ready. Runway said it joined the OpenAI Marketplace as a launch partner. Independent hands-on reports of Space have not appeared yet.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-chatgpt-space-plugins/
