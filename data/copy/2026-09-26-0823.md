## Thieves steal Nvidia-branded trailers, find 20 tons of sand

**The trailers advertised PlusAI's self-driving truck work, so thieves broke them open expecting valuable hardware. Police found them nearby with the sand still inside.**

Thieves stole two trailers bearing Nvidia and PlusAI branding from PlusAI's Fremont, California warehouse late Wednesday, the SF Standard reported. Warehouse workers discovered the theft Thursday morning. The trailers had held about 20 tons of sand, which the self-driving truck company uses to simulate real freight during testing.

One trailer carried the slogan "Autonomy built for the long haul," the SF Standard reported. Security footage showed two unidentified tractors pulling the trailers away. "The very apparent branding probably made them think there was very valuable equipment back there," PlusAI marketing vice president Lauren Kwan said.

A friend of PlusAI's vice president of legal spotted the trailers outside a Newark office building, minutes from the warehouse, and sent a photo, the SF Standard reported. Newark police recovered both trailers. Hoodline reported that their locks appeared forced open, with the sand cargo left untouched inside.

PlusAI's SuperDrive autonomous driving system has been trained on more than seven million miles of real-world driving, according to Hoodline. No arrests have been made, and Fremont police are still investigating.

## macOS 27 adds AI photo editing, smarter Safari tabs

**Apple's new operating system lets Photos remove people from images and Safari group tabs by topic, after months of beta testing, 9to5Mac reported.**

Apple released macOS 27, code-named Golden Gate, to the public this week after several months of beta testing, 9to5Mac reported. The update adds a set of Apple Intelligence features across Photos, Safari, Shortcuts and Finder on Apple Silicon Macs.

In Photos, a new Spatial Reframing tool lets users adjust an image's perspective after it is taken, and an Extend feature expands a photo beyond its original edges. Apple's Clean Up tool, which removes objects and people from photos, gained more capability in the update, according to 9to5Mac.

Safari now groups open tabs by topic automatically and can send a push notification when a tracked website changes, useful for watching prices or restocks. A new Describe an Extension option lets users build custom Safari extensions by typing a natural-language prompt instead of writing code.

Shortcuts gained the ability to build automations from natural-language descriptions, with iterative refinement, and Finder now suggests file and folder names based on their content, 9to5Mac reported. Apple has not published usage data for any of the new features.

## OpenAI challenges Apple's new evidence in trade secrets suit

**OpenAI and Jony Ive's io Products say Apple's reply brief smuggled in 126 pages of new expert testimony they had no chance to answer, 9to5Mac reported.**

OpenAI and co-defendants asked a court to strike evidence Apple introduced in its reply brief in a trade secrets lawsuit, arguing the filing went far beyond what Apple's original complaint covered, 9to5Mac reported.

Apple sued OpenAI, Jony Ive's io Products, and former Apple employees Chang Liu and Tang Tan, alleging the defendants unlawfully obtained and used confidential Apple hardware information for OpenAI's consumer hardware work. Apple is seeking a preliminary injunction to block further use of the material during litigation.

OpenAI's motion objects to five expert declarations totaling 126 pages and 13 exhibits, including an entirely new expert witness, Dr. Jude Runge, whom Apple had not previously disclosed. "These declarations go far outside the scope of Apple's opening brief and Defendants' opposition," OpenAI said in the filing, according to 9to5Mac.

OpenAI also said some of Apple's evidence came from investigations conducted only after its initial filing, and that some communications were presented out of context. The defendants asked the court to disregard the material rather than let Apple respond to it in a later filing.

## Meta adds opt-out for smart glasses' visual data storage

**A new toggle stops Meta from storing images its AI features capture on smart glasses, though the company says human reviewers may still see that data unless it is off.**

Meta added a setting that lets smart glasses owners stop the company from storing visual data from AI features, Engadget reported. The feature covers data captured during tasks like Live AI and object identification. It is separate from photos and videos taken with the camera's recording light on.

Meta said the stored visual data may go through human review, according to Engadget. That review happens as part of training and improving its AI models. Without the opt-out, footage from tasks like translating a menu is not reviewed by automated systems alone.

To opt out, users open the Meta AI app and go to their glasses' settings. They select Meta AI, then toggle off "store visual data from AI experiences." The steps repeat for each pair of glasses a person owns. The glasses must be out of their case and connected to a phone to change the setting.

Turning the setting off can disable features that rely on stored visual data, Engadget reported. That includes visual reminders that reference what a user was looking at earlier.
