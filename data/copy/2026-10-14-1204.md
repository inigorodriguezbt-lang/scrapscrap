## DeepMind details Singapore's on-device Gemma scam filter in two stages

**Google DeepMind says a two-stage Gemma pipeline for Singapore scans private chats for scams on the phone, with no conversation data sent to the cloud.**

Google DeepMind and Singapore's Government Technology Agency, GovTech, have described an on-device scam detector built on Google's lightweight Gemma models, according to a DeepMind blog post dated 14 October. DeepMind says the model runs entirely on the phone, so conversations are analysed locally rather than scanned in the cloud. No independent test of its accuracy has been published.

The post describes two stages. DeepMind says a lightweight classifier screens incoming messages first, using minimal power. A fine-tuned Gemma model then reads a larger snapshot of the conversation to confirm a threat or dismiss a false alarm. When it spots manipulative patterns, it warns the user to pause before sharing information or money.

DeepMind says the Gemma model was fine-tuned on local scam conversations, covering impersonations of public bodies and banks, and trending regional schemes. The post says fine-tuning took place in GovTech's managed Google Cloud environment, used anonymised scam reports and intelligence feeds, and excluded private or personally identifiable information. It was stress-tested on synthetic dialogues and local corpora, the post adds.

DeepMind frames the work against Singapore's scam problem. It says scams make up over half of the country's reported crimes, with losses above S$4 billion since 2019 and social engineering accounting for up to 80% of losses. It says the model was presented at Singapore International Cyber Week 2026, with a phased rollout inside applications still to be developed with the government. DeepMind gives no launch date, and does not name the apps.
