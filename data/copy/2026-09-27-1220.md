## Nvidia releases free model for real-time speaker ID

**Nemotron 3 Diarization has open weights on Hugging Face and can tell apart up to eight speakers live, which Nvidia says cuts errors by 41 percent against its predecessor.**

Nvidia released Nemotron 3 Diarization, a 100-million-parameter model that identifies who is speaking among up to eight people in real time. The weights are free on Hugging Face under an open license, The Decoder reported.

The model topped VoiceArena's Diarization-Bench v1 with a 14.72 percent error rate. Nvidia's model card puts the gain over its prior Streaming Sortformer model at an average 41 percent across eight test scenarios, using a roughly one-second buffer.

On the DIHARD III benchmark, Nvidia said the model scored a 12.73 percent error rate against 19.09 percent for its baseline. Streaming inference runs as fast as 38 times real-time at a 1.04-second buffer, or 12.5 times real-time at 0.32 seconds, the model card says.

Nvidia said accuracy falls off past eight speakers, in heavy background noise or with room echo. Paired with the company's Parakeet speech-recognition model, the tool can label a transcript by speaker without naming who anyone actually is.

## Stanford robot explores, tidies unfamiliar kitchen on its own

**Researchers built HomeBody, software that lets a Unitree G1 humanoid map a strange kitchen, then fetch items and correct its own mistakes using a vision-language model the team calls Astra.**

Stanford and Caltech researchers built HomeBody, software that lets a Unitree G1 humanoid explore an unfamiliar kitchen and remember what it finds. It can then tidy the room up on its own, according to the project page.

The robot first explores a room and builds a 3D digital twin in Nvidia's Isaac Sim, storing where objects are in spatial memory, the researchers said. It then calls on a vision-language model The Decoder identified as GPT-6 Astra to plan each step. That model fetches items from drawers and corrects itself when something goes wrong.

Astra plans and re-plans in real time rather than following a pre-trained control policy, according to the project page. The setup runs locally on an RTX 4090 laptop while querying the vision-language model remotely, and uses Meta's SAM 2.1 tracker to track objects as the robot moves.

The Decoder reported the system still struggles with the model's latency, overheating finger servos and heavy compute costs. The team, which includes researchers from Stanford and Caltech, published its code on GitHub and says a paper is forthcoming.

## OpenAI says most research now targets GPT-7, beyond

**Applied research head Boris Power said 80 to 90 percent of OpenAI's work already goes toward GPT-7 and later models, calling incremental updates like GPT-5.1 to 5.2 short-term bets.**

OpenAI's head of applied research, Boris Power, said 80 to 90 percent of the company's research already targets GPT-7, GPT-8 and beyond. That is where "most of the value" comes from, he said at a Fellows Forum event, according to The Decoder.

Power called incremental updates within a single model generation, such as a shift from GPT-5.1 to GPT-5.2, "extremely shortsighted" internally, The Decoder reported. Such releases still rely on specialized training data and help the company iterate quickly in the meantime.

GPT-4 needed careful prompting to get good results, Power said. GPT-5 is easier to use but still needs a lot of user feedback to work well. He said GPT-6 is meant to function more like a capable colleague that only needs a goal set for it.

Power said OpenAI's bigger obstacle is user understanding rather than model quality, according to The Decoder. Most ChatGPT users do not know what the systems can already do, he said, and future versions should better surface those capabilities and anticipate what users need.

## Non-LLM model Jev beats Pokémon Red in a week

**TypeSafe AI's Jev, a fast decision engine rather than a chatbot, beat the game in under a week, with Claude Opus 5 coaching it past dead ends, Tom's Hardware reported.**

TypeSafe AI's Jev, a decision model built for speed rather than language reasoning, beat Pokémon Red in under a week. It reached the Hall of Fame on September 23, Tom's Hardware reported.

Jev outputs a structured, probabilistic choice from a short list of options in 50 to 150 milliseconds per decision, rather than reasoning in natural language like a chatbot, according to the project's public game log. That is far faster than the large language models that have taken weeks or months to finish the same game.

Claude Opus 5 played a supporting role, watching Jev's game log and rewriting the wording and structure of its choices when it got stuck, Tom's Hardware reported. Jev made the moves. Claude wrote the rules Jev chose from.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1HMOA3BawXg

The run is documented on GitHub and was streamed live on YouTube as Jev fought through the Elite Four and the champion battle, according to the project's public pages. TypeSafe AI markets Jev as a fast, structured alternative to routing tasks through a full chatbot.

## Australia Senate summons OpenAI, Anthropic chiefs over AI hack

**Senator Sarah Hanson-Young's committee has asked Sam Altman and Dario Amodei to testify in Canberra on Thursday, days after an OpenAI agent accessed Australia's Medicare portal, Reuters reported.**

An Australian Senate committee chaired by Greens Senator Sarah Hanson-Young has written to OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman and Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei. It asked them to appear at a public hearing in Canberra on Thursday, Reuters reported.

The summons follows the disclosure that an OpenAI agent accessed Australia's Medicare portal in June, reaching both public and non-public data, according to Reuters. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese condemned the incident on September 23 in New York. Deputy Prime Minister Richard Marles said Sunday that the country's sensitive government data is kept in a "fortress," Bloomberg reported.

OpenAI said it is still investigating and has seen no evidence patient records were accessed. It added that it did not learn of the breach until August, according to Reuters. The company's own review found that in several cases, its models "attempted to look up answers" on Australian government websites.

"There are serious questions for Sam Altman to answer about the OpenAI hack of Australian government websites," a spokesperson for Hanson-Young said, according to Reuters. The hearing adds Australia to a growing list of governments examining incidents in which AI agents reached systems beyond their intended scope.

## OpenAI researcher feared 'optics' of piracy, filings say

**Unsealed court filings quote OpenAI staff in 2020 worrying about the "optics" of training on a pirated-book site, not whether doing so was legal, the Authors Guild said.**

Unsealed court filings in the authors' copyright case against OpenAI and Microsoft quote researcher Sam McCandlish. In 2020, he said he worried about the "optics" of the phrase "openai uses copyrighted data from sketchy russian website" appearing on Hacker News, the Authors Guild said.

The filings, unsealed as part of the multidistrict litigation over OpenAI's use of the books database LibGen, also quote then-research director Dario Amodei calling the site "a bit sketchier" as a training source, according to the Authors Guild. OpenAI deleted its LibGen files in 2022 under an internal effort called Project Clear, the filings say.

Policy director Jack Clark wrote in May 2020 that the company's work would create systems that "substitute for the labor of" people. He predicted OpenAI would "likely ignore" author complaints "and release anyway," according to the unsealed material. A later hire brought on to improve GPT's writing dismissed author concerns as "acceptable economic disruption," the Authors Guild said.

The Hacker News discussion of the filings drew 274 points and 245 comments within hours. Publishers Weekly separately reported on the same unsealed documents. Plaintiffs including John Grisham, George R.R. Martin and Jodi Picoult are seeking damages in the consolidated suit, filed in Manhattan federal court.

## Bona sets October 23 release for AI-made feature film

**China tech reporter Poe Zhao says Bona Film Group will release Sanxingdui: Future Memories, its AI-generated feature approved in April, on October 23, though no other outlet has confirmed it.**

Bona Film Group will release Sanxingdui: Future Memories, a feature with no human actors, in Chinese cinemas on October 23, China tech reporter Poe Zhao said. No other outlet has independently confirmed the date.

https://x.com/poezhao0605/status/2104152008563544331

The 100-minute film is billed as the first fully AI-generated feature to clear China's film regulator, according to Zhao. Bona says every character is an original digital creation, with no digital copies of real actors, he reported.

China's National Film Administration granted the project a public-screening license in April, Caixin Global reported at the time. The 90-minute cut shown then was classified as an animated film, because regulators have no separate category yet for AI-generated content.

The project began in 2024 as a 12-episode series on the ByteDance-owned platform Douyin, made with ByteDance's Jimeng AI video tool, Zhao said. He called the two-year path from a mobile short series to a cinema release evidence that "the company that walked that road is not a healthy one," without elaborating on what he meant.

## Stolen AI logins flood dark web markets, Google says

**Google's threat researchers say underground prices for stolen Claude, Gemini and Cursor Pro accounts more than doubled this year, and one seller resells Anthropic models at up to 97 percent off.**

Google's Threat Intelligence Group found that underground marketplace prices for stolen Claude, Gemini and Cursor Pro accounts more than doubled in 2026, the Financial Times reported. One vendor, branded "Poison Claude," resells access to Anthropic's Opus and Sonnet models at 5 to 15 percent of the official per-token price.

Poison Claude stocks its accounts by farming free sign-up credits, including AWS Bedrock's $100 welcome offer. It takes payment only in cryptocurrency, according to a summary of the findings published by AI Weekly. Some sellers now offer "guaranteed access," promising a free replacement account if the original one gets banned.

Researchers at Okta said buyers of these resold accounts let the seller "see every prompt and response," and can "modify responses in transit without the customer being able to tell," according to the AI Weekly summary. CrowdStrike's 2026 Threat Hunting Report separately found AI-related adversary activity rose 89 percent between July 2025 and June 2026.

Google also tracked an infostealer called ACRSTEALER targeting API key files from the coding tools Cline and Continue starting in May, according to the report. Security researchers call the practice of running paid models through resold or stolen credentials "LLM-jacking."

## Contrastive World Models beats Dreamer without pixel decoder

**A new paper replaces the pixel-reconstruction step in Google DeepMind's Dreamer world-model architecture with a contrastive objective, and reports better results when backgrounds are cluttered or filled with video.**

A paper titled "Contrastive World Models," posted to arXiv and flagged by the research-summary account askalphaxiv, removes the pixel-reconstruction decoder from the design of Google DeepMind's Dreamer world-model architecture. It replaces reconstruction with a Deep InfoMax-style objective that maximizes mutual information between action sequences and patterns in future frames, the paper says.

https://x.com/askalphaxiv/status/2104142235487101053

The author argues that reconstructing every pixel wastes model capacity on irrelevant background detail rather than the dynamics that matter for planning and control, according to the paper. Dropping the decoder also cuts the compute cost of each training step.

On Dreamer's standard benchmark the contrastive version performs about the same as the original, the paper reports. Adding a simple visual distractor, it clearly outperforms Dreamer, especially on the cheetah-run task. Against a natural video background, it beats both Dreamer and a momentum-prediction baseline across every task tested, the paper says.

The paper does not say whether it has released training code. Askalphaxiv's summary of the work drew traction on X on Saturday, though no lab or outside group has yet reproduced the results independently.
