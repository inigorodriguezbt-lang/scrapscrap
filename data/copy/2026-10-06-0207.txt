Aleph Alpha says its Kolibri-1, a German-English mixture-of-experts model with 78 billion parameters and about 3 billion active per token, is out under Apache 2.0, with a context window up to one million tokens.

It is a European-built open-weights model trained on 768 B200 GPUs, pitched at public administration and industry as a sovereignty play; the model card dates the release to 3 October.


Artificial Analysis says ElevenLabs' Eleven v4 Turbo now ranks first on its Provider Voice text-to-speech arena with an Elo of 1,334, ahead of Eleven v4 at 1,321, at half the price.

The ranking rests on 1,645 appearances, and it puts a cheaper model ahead of ElevenLabs' own flagship sibling.


Z.ai says GLM-5.3 is now available on Amazon Bedrock, pitching it to enterprises for coding and agentic work.

Bedrock availability puts a Chinese open-weights lab's model on a major Western cloud, where many enterprise buyers already hold accounts and procurement approval.


A Quinnipiac University poll finds 77 percent of Americans want AI development slowed or stopped until its safety is verified, and 74 percent have little or no trust in AI company leaders, The Decoder reports.

The poll also finds 86 percent back independent safety standards, a sign of public sentiment as AI companies talk up IPOs and funding rounds.


404 Media reports arXiv is rate-limiting submissions because it cannot keep up with AI-generated slop; the preprint server says submissions have doubled in two years and moderators are struggling.

arXiv is where most AI research first appears, so tighter limits could change how quickly new papers reach readers.


Tom's Hardware reports Tencent is renting 100,000 AI chips from Oracle data centers in Southeast Asia for about $7 billion over five years, according to reports it cites.

The deal would give a Chinese tech giant offshore access to accelerators, even as per-hour rental prices climb.


Tom's Hardware reports a solo developer has got Sony's Wolverine running on PC using AI, working from source code taken in the 2023 ransomware attack; the port is described as buggy.

It is an early example of AI tools helping one person port a console exclusive, though the source was stolen, which raises legal questions.
