## OpenAI's annualized revenue nears $70 billion, Axios reports

**Axios, citing unnamed sources, says OpenAI's run rate has grown more than 70% since July, with business revenue up more than 100%.**

OpenAI's annualized revenue is nearing $70 billion, Axios reported on Tuesday, citing unnamed people familiar with the matter. Bloomberg, which relayed the report, said the figure is based on the company's current performance. OpenAI has not published the number, and neither outlet says the company confirmed it.

Axios says the run rate has grown by more than 70% since the start of the third quarter. Business-to-business revenue has grown by more than 100% over the same period, according to the report. On the consumer side, OpenAI added more revenue this quarter than it added during all of 2025, Axios says.

The jump is steep against earlier reporting. Bloomberg News reported in August that OpenAI was on pace for more than $40 billion in annualized revenue, according to the Axios coverage. If the new figure holds, the run rate would have climbed by roughly $30 billion in about a month.

A run rate takes a recent pace of sales and multiplies it out over a year, so it is not revenue collected. Commentator kimmonismus, writing on X, compared the figure with Anthropic's reported expectation of more than $100 billion in annualized revenue this year, though the two figures cover different dates.

Nothing in the reporting describes OpenAI's costs, so it is not known how much of the growth survives compute and cloud bills. The timing is notable, since OpenAI's annual DevDay keynote was due to start in San Francisco later on Tuesday and Anthropic has an IPO filing under way.

## Trump launches America.gov, an AI front door to federal services

**The site lets users ask questions in plain language instead of navigating agency websites, and an executive order tells agencies to plug their services into it.**

President Donald Trump launched America.gov on Tuesday at an event in Washington, describing it as a single entrance to federal services. Users can ask questions in plain language rather than working through separate agency sites, according to coverage syndicated by local stations. The first version is available now, Trump said, with more features planned in coming weeks and months.

The project is led by Joe Gebbia, the Airbnb co-founder who serves as U.S. Chief Design Officer. Trump said it would spare people an "endless maze" of government websites, rules and more than 10,000 forms. Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio attended the launch, the reports say.

Trump compared the interface to commercial chatbots. "Anyone who's used Grok or Gemini or ChatGPT will understand instantly how to use America.gov," he said. The coverage does not say which AI models power the site, and the administration has not published that detail in the reports available so far.

The administration plans to add tasks including passport applications, name changes after marriage, campsite reservations, Medicare plan selection and veteran benefits, according to the reports. An executive order directs agencies to integrate their services with the platform, so what the assistant can do will depend on how quickly each agency connects.

No independent test of the site has been published, and the coverage reviewed carries no outside criticism. How the assistant handles wrong answers on benefits or immigration questions, and what happens to the data users type in, is not addressed in the reports.

## Anthropic's IPO filing lists up to $84.5 billion in SpaceX compute deals

**The Information reports most of the commitment can be cancelled on 90 days' notice, one of the more flexible items in Anthropic's $518 billion compute tally.**

Anthropic has agreements to pay SpaceX up to $84.5 billion for Nvidia-based computing capacity through 2029, The Information reported on Tuesday, citing Anthropic's IPO prospectus. The Information says most of the amount can be cancelled with 90 days' notice. The article is behind a paywall, so this account rests on its summary and on other coverage of the filing.

InvestmentNews, summarising the filing, lists the counterparty as xAI and describes the commitment as up to $84.5 billion, mostly cancellable with 90 days' notice. That matches The Information's figure. Neither outlet has published the prospectus itself, and Anthropic has not commented in the coverage reviewed.

The xAI deal sits inside a much larger list. InvestmentNews reports Anthropic has committed to spend at least $518 billion on computing over the next decade, with about 80% non-cancellable. It lists Google at $111.1 billion, Amazon at $110 billion, Microsoft at $31.4 billion, Broadcom at $161.2 billion in equipment leases and AMD at up to $20 billion.

Set against those, the xAI line is the most flexible, which could matter if demand or pricing shifts. The same coverage says Anthropic's 2025 revenue rose about twelvefold to nearly $4.6 billion, with a net loss of about $42 billion, and that Reuters expects the offering after the November midterms.

## Wajo launches Fo, an AI agent that phones, emails and pays for errands

**Wajo says Fo finished 71% of 104 real-world tasks unaided, against 42% for OpenClaw, and hands the rest to human assistants.**

Wajo, a startup founded by former Google DeepMind engineering lead Shivani Poddar, has launched Fo, an agent that calls, emails and coordinates with businesses to finish everyday tasks, according to the company's site. TestingCatalog posted a launch video on X on Tuesday. Wajo describes Fo as proactive and self-learning rather than a chatbot that waits for prompts.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2104947696909844740

Fo sends email from its own address rather than as the user, and pays with a single-use card for each purchase, Wajo says. The company says the merchant never sees the user's real card details and Fo does not see them either. Wajo also says Fo signs into websites without storing passwords.

In Wajo's own tests on 104 real-world tasks, Fo completed 71% without help, compared with 42% for OpenClaw, according to the company. It says Fo passed 94% of safety checks, including checks for unauthorised messages or data leaks. No outside group has repeated the tests, and Wajo has not said how it picked the tasks.

When a task needs a person, such as dealing with a barbershop that has no online booking, Fo passes it to one of Wajo's trained human assistants, the company says. Those assistants receive only task-specific information, not the user's full chat history, according to Wajo.

Wajo's site lists a free Fo Mini tier capped at 1,000 tasks a day, an invite-only OG Fo tier with unlimited tasks and call time, and a Pro tier priced through sales. Fo works through email, calendar, Slack, phone and web, the company says.

## InstaCloud launches a serverless cloud that coding agents operate themselves

**The Y Combinator-backed service lets Claude Code or Cursor deploy apps, branch whole environments including data, and pause for human approval on risky changes.**

InstaCloud, built by Y Combinator-backed InsForge, is pitched as an "agent-native cloud" where a coding agent provisions and runs the apps it builds, according to the company's site. TestingCatalog posted a demo video on X on Tuesday. InstaCloud says it works with Claude Code, Gemini, Cursor, Kiro and OpenCode.

https://x.com/testingcatalog/status/2104958023554724254

Services scale with traffic and drop to zero when idle, the company says. Agents can also branch an entire environment, data included, to test a change without touching production. Users approve critical changes, according to InstaCloud, whose docs describe a daemon that holds all credentials and gates every sensitive action.

The company also publishes an open-source runtime under the Apache-2.0 licence. Its README calls it a self-hostable platform on a single machine, with a Postgres database, S3-compatible storage and containers per project. It installs with a one-line shell script, and the repository showed only 6 stars when read on Tuesday.

The hosted service has a free tier and a freemium model, though the landing page does not list prices. No independent review has been published, so claims about scale-to-zero restart times of about two seconds and safe branching are the maker's own and have not been tested by anyone else.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-instacloud-agent-cloud/
