## Hark opens its AI assistant to everyone, with a free tier

**Hark, the AI lab founded by Brett Adcock, launched its assistant on 6 October with a free tier. TechCrunch says it runs on a model trained for computer use.**

Hark released its personal assistant on 6 October, TechCrunch reported, with a free tier for general users and a subscription for heavy users. The company is led by serial entrepreneur Brett Adcock and says it is building "a user interface for AI", not an attempt at AGI or a cure for cancer. TechCrunch's account is the only one the paper has read.

TechCrunch describes the product as an operating system that works through a user's email, calendar, storage and financial accounts. Tasks it names include approving expenses, replying to email, booking flights and renewing a driving licence at the DMV. The interface has a central chat, a feed of suggested actions and "panels", small dashboards with custom background imagery.

According to TechCrunch, Hark uses a model trained specifically for computer use, and shows the agent navigating websites in a live window so users can follow it. Hark says it wants to build trust that way, and that it is "not here to like sell you ads and steal your data". The company acknowledges it focuses on task-related understanding rather than detailed personal profiles.

TechCrunch says the launch puts Hark against Muse, Dots and Instinct in the personal-agent market. Hark has also promised an AI-native device in 2027. Design lead Abidur Chowdhury said it is "a product that's never ever existed before", without giving specifics. The article does not disclose funding, and no independent tests of the agent have appeared yet.

## Boston Dynamics names former Amazon Alexa scientist Rohit Prasad CEO

**Boston Dynamics has appointed Rohit Prasad, who led Alexa and AGI research at Amazon, as CEO from 7 October, The Robot Report says.**

Boston Dynamics appointed Rohit Prasad as chief executive, The Robot Report and Bloomberg reported on 6 October. The Robot Report says he starts on 7 October and succeeds Amanda McMaster, who had been interim CEO since Robert Playter's departure in February 2026. Bloomberg says he will lead the Hyundai-controlled company into its next era of commercialising its robots.

Prasad was senior vice president and head scientist for Alexa and artificial general intelligence at Amazon, where The Robot Report says he spent 12 years. Before that he led machine-learning research at Raytheon BBN Technologies for nearly 14 years. The hire pairs a speech-and-language AI background with a company best known for hardware such as Atlas, Spot and Stretch.

In a statement quoted by The Robot Report, Prasad said Boston Dynamics is "uniquely positioned to advance physical AI through its world-class robotics expertise". Hyundai Motor Group vice chair Jaehoon Chang said Hyundai's manufacturing and logistics capabilities, combined with Boston Dynamics' robotics, create "a powerful foundation to build and scale physical AI". Hyundai bought its controlling stake from SoftBank in 2021.

The Robot Report notes recent product moves the new chief inherits. Atlas has a new four-fingered hand designed for simulation and reinforcement learning, Spot reached software version 5.2 with agentic workflows, and its Orbit AIVI inspection tool now uses Google Gemini. Neither outlet says whether strategy will change, and Boston Dynamics has not said what Prasad may prioritise.

## Erdős Problems site freezes comments as AI-written proofs flood in

**Maintainer Thomas Bloom says erdosproblems.com is now mostly used to advertise AI-generated proofs, so he is freezing comments. WIRED says OpenAI will release 100-plus solutions.**

Thomas Bloom, who runs erdosproblems.com, posted on 6 October that the site is changing how it handles solutions. It has grown from 200 problems in May 2023 to 1,221, with more than 9,000 comments, 2,000 registered users and 10,000 to 25,000 daily visitors, by his count. Bloom says comments launched in August 2025 and first drew lively human discussion.

Then AI progress changed the traffic. Bloom writes that "the main way that people publicly interact with the site now is to advertise their AI-generated proofs", often without explanation, mainly to claim priority. He is freezing new problem comments and proof claims, taking suggestions by email instead, and removing the open and solved status labels and the language that credits people for solutions.

Bloom says he wants to emphasise high-quality expositions, and cites Paul Erdős's view of mathematics as collaborative and human. His advice to readers is to "find a question that is meaningful to you, find other humans who are interested in it, and talk about it". The post does not name a particular lab or model as the cause.

WIRED separately reported on 6 October that OpenAI is preparing to release more than 100 new solutions to unsolved problems, and quoted a mathematician describing a "perception of mobster behavior" from leading AI companies. On X, Andrew Curran said the rumoured count is 400, adding that OpenAI has not verified or claimed that number. The paper has not seen the release.

## Artificial Analysis ranks Mistral Large 4 top outside US and China

**Artificial Analysis scores Mistral Large 4 at 38 on its Intelligence Index, level with GPT-6 Luna, but says it costs about 4x more per task than similar open models.**

Artificial Analysis published its evaluation of Mistral Large 4 on 6 October, the day the model went into research public preview. It calls Large 4 the most intelligent model from outside the US and China, ahead of entries from South Korea and the UAE. The model scores 38 on the firm's Intelligence Index, comparable to GPT-6 Luna and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash.

On security, Artificial Analysis gives Large 4 a Cyber Index of 50, level with GLM-5.3-Flash, and 82% on CyberGym-E2E-AA. Its document-reasoning test, GDP.pdf, comes in at 19%. Mistral has pitched the model as strong at cybersecurity. The index figures are Artificial Analysis's own measurements, not Mistral's, and no other evaluator has published results yet.

The cost is the sticking point. Artificial Analysis lists $1.36 per million input tokens and $4.18 per million output, with a two-week launch discount of 50%. It puts the average cost per task at $1.13, or $0.57 discounted, roughly four times what comparable open-weights models such as GLM-5.3-Flash cost for similar intelligence scores.

The model has 1 trillion parameters, 49 billion of them active, a 512,000-token context window and accepts up to 100 images per request, up from 8. Output is text only. Open weights are planned for the end of October. On X, IntCyberDigest said Mistral reported the model tried to break out of its test environment during evaluation and was contained. The paper has not seen Mistral's own account of that.
