## Pluto Security leaves stealth with $42 million to watch AI agents

**Pluto Security says about 60% of the AI risks it detects come from vibe-coded tools built by non-developers, with finance staff the biggest source, TechCrunch reported.**

Pluto Security, a startup that monitors the AI tools, agents and apps employees use at work, has come out of stealth with $42 million in funding, TechCrunch reported on 13 October. The round combines an $8.5 million seed led by TLV Partners and a $34 million Series A led by Jump Capital. No other outlet has reported the figures yet.

The company's own numbers are the most striking part, and they are Pluto's claims rather than independent findings. Chief executive and co-founder Shahar Bahat told TechCrunch that around 60% of the risks Pluto detects across its customers involve tools built by non-developers through vibe coding. Finance departments are the largest source, followed by marketing and legal. Developers rank fourth.

Bahat gave one example: a vibe-coded app built by a finance employee at a Fortune 500 company to manage customer information and churn, which was exposed to the internet. She also said a newly announced AI tool can appear inside a company's environment within two hours of release, and that Pluto found malicious activity in every proof-of-concept deployment, some of it live attacks, according to TechCrunch.

The product watches AI tools, skills, extensions, MCP servers and the apps and agents staff build. TechCrunch says it hooks into endpoint security and device management systems, or runs its own sensors, and collects prompts, commands, running processes and permissions. Pluto says lightweight models on the device judge agent behaviour and can step in mid-task, for instance by blocking an agent that asks for permissions beyond what the user intended.

Pluto began selling in April and says it has passed $2 million in annual recurring revenue with more than 30 enterprise customers, including Oscar Health, Workato, Sixt and AlphaSense. It has 40 employees across the US and Israel. In April it also launched Plutonium, a free scanner for public AI skills, connectors and add-ons, which Bahat says thousands of security professionals use. TechCrunch notes it joins a crowded field that includes Zenity, AIR, InsightFinder and LayerX.
