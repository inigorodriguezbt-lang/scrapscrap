## Replit's CEO says it has dropped Anthropic; a source says no

**Replit chief Amjad Masad said onstage that "ChatGPT is better now" and the company no longer works with Anthropic, but a person familiar with the relationship told TechCrunch the partnership stands.**

Amjad Masad, Replit's CEO and co-founder, said Replit no longer works with Anthropic, TechCrunch reported. Speaking onstage at TechCrunch Disrupt on Thursday, he said the decision weighed "cost, partnership, and performance." He added: "I think ChatGPT is better now." TechCrunch is the only outlet that has the account so far.

On performance, Masad said Replit tests models on an internal benchmark and a public one, and that OpenAI's models, which he called "Sol and Astra," do better at building vibe-coded applications from scratch. He called OpenAI "a better partner for now." These are Masad's own claims about his company's tests, and Replit has not published the results.

The account is disputed. TechCrunch said a person familiar with the Anthropic and Replit relationship, who asked not to be named, rejected Masad's version and said the partnership remains in place. The outlet noted that Replit announced a partnership with OpenAI in August, and that nothing had signalled Replit leaving Anthropic until Masad spoke.

TechCrunch pointed to recent evidence of continued use. A Replit blog post from late September listed Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 among models running in Replit Agent's production environment, and around then a Replit product employee posted on X about using Anthropic's Opus 5.5. TechCrunch said it had asked Replit for clarification and would update if it replied.

Masad also gave a few business figures at the event, according to TechCrunch. Enterprise made up about 2 percent of Replit's revenue last year and about 15 percent this year, and more than half of revenue is now business-to-business. He did not share annual recurring revenue. It is unclear whether Replit has switched its production models or is describing a change of preference.

## Apple bans hidden app features and names AI nudify apps in new rules

**Apple's October 16 App Review update adds a rule against hidden, dormant or undocumented features, and lists nonconsensual intimate imagery as a content risk, after months of pressure over AI nudify apps.**

Apple updated its App Review Guidelines on October 16, according to 9to5Mac, with changes aimed at AI nudification apps and at apps that behave differently after approval. The update adds a new Section 2.5.19, which says apps may not contain hidden, dormant or undocumented features, and specifically calls out code obfuscation. Apple says violations are grounds for removal and termination of a developer account.

Apple already barred hidden features under Section 2.3.1, 9to5Mac noted, but the new rule makes the requirement explicit. Apple also strengthened its language on apps that "misrepresent their purpose during review and only reveal harmful capabilities" afterwards, giving nudification as an example of what it means.

In Section 1.2, on user-generated content, Apple added "nonconsensual intimate imagery" to the risks it lists. Intellectual property infringement and anonymous bullying were already there. Developers who "fail to implement appropriate safeguards" may be treated as breaching the Developer Code of Conduct, as may those who do not fix violations after notice, according to 9to5Mac's quotes.

9to5Mac traced the pressure over the past year. The Tech Transparency Project found dozens of nudify apps in January. A later report said App Store search suggestions and ads steered users to them. Apple confirmed in April that it had privately warned xAI that Grok could be removed. San Francisco officials ordered eight apps pulled in July.

Section 5 also changes. Apps that facilitate human trafficking or child exploitation will be removed and blocked from alternative distribution, per 9to5Mac's reading. How strictly Apple will enforce the hidden-feature rule is not yet known. The reporting rests on Apple's published guidelines as summarised by 9to5Mac.
