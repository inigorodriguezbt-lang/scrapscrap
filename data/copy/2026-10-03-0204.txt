Cloudflare and Perplexity release open 27B decision models built on Qwen

Cloudflare's Clef and Perplexity's pplx-decider both turn a 27-billion-parameter Qwen model into a classifier for routing tickets and labelling bug reports, and Ollama now lists Clef to pull in one command.


Microsoft launches MAI-Transcribe-2-Streaming at $0.54 an audio hour

Microsoft says its new real-time speech-to-text model returns partial transcripts about 100 milliseconds after audio arrives, covers 60 languages and ranks first for accuracy on Artificial Analysis.


Supabase raises $150 million and buys Turso for lightweight agent databases

Supabase co-founder Paul Copplestone says agents are spinning up millions of databases, and the Postgres company is buying SQLite-based Turso after a $150 million round led by Singapore's GIC.

The independent ranking puts Ideogram's precision-edit pitch next to HiDream and HunyuanImage, which sit just above it.


Theo says he will put up $10,000 to benchmark whether models are being nerfed, matching BridgeMind's stake, and donate another $10,000 to charity if his tests show the viral nerf claim holds.

The wager turns a viral argument over daily model degradation into a test with a stated payout and a proposed third-party audit.


Theo says BridgeMind's nerf bench never discloses its harnesses, tasks, run counts or how its plus-or-minus 10 per cent variance band was chosen, and asks why Opus runs moved from weekly to daily.

Methodology is the crux: without task and harness details, a daily benchmark dip cannot be told apart from run-to-run noise.


Ollama says Cloudflare's decision models are on its library, Clef at 27 billion parameters and Clef Flash at 9 billion, pulled with one command.

They are built to classify images, label bug reports and route support tickets rather than chat.


Microsoft's MAI-Voice-2.1 text-to-speech is priced at $22 per million characters across 23 languages, with a Flash version at $15 and 55 per cent faster inference, TestingCatalog reports.

It launched alongside MAI-Transcribe-2-Streaming, giving Microsoft a speech stack in its own Foundry catalogue.


Tavus says its new Griffin model passed a "video Turing test", with 48 per cent of users in live chats believing they were talking to a human, Techmeme's summary of the announcement says.

It is the company's own test and figure; no outside evaluation has been published, so the result is unconfirmed.


Shrivu Shankar argues every SaaS business will become a harness around a model, with agents handling routine work and humans reviewing the high-value decisions. The post has 96 points on Hacker News.
