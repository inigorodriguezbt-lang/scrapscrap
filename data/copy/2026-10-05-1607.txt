Nolla Health says Utah has cleared its AI to issue first prescriptions for acne

Nolla Health says Utah regulators approved an AI that writes first acne prescriptions, with a physician checking each of the first 100. The company says it costs $4.99 a month.


Eigen Labs and Zcash group open HashSmash, a contest for AI to attack SHA-256

Anyone can now point an AI agent at SHA-256, SHA-3, BLAKE3 and Poseidon and submit attacks. Eigen Labs and Shielded Labs say people were already probing SHA-3 and BLAKE3 within 23 minutes.


Figure is about to launch Hark, an AI assistant with memory, TestingCatalog reports

TestingCatalog says code for Figure's Hark assistant points to Claude Opus 5.5 as its main model. Founder Brett Adcock says it launches this week, and the first 100,000 users reportedly get paid access free.

Hugging Face's Clement Delangue says Cloudflare's Clef, an Apache 2.0 27B decision model that returns probabilities for typed answer options, is the number one trending model on the Hub.

It is Cloudflare's first in-house open model family, and it can be downloaded and run on your own hardware.


A paper called SelfSearch says a coding agent rewrote its own harness to solve 82.0% of Terminal-Bench 2.1 with DeepSeek V4 Flash, at a search cost of $4.03, matching Codex.

If it holds up, cheap self-improvement of the scaffold around a model could rival hand-built coding harnesses.


Hugging Face's Clement Delangue says open tooling now trains models inside real coding harnesses such as Claude Code and Codex through a capture proxy; one model scored 62% under Mini-SWE-Agent and 33% under Claude Code.

Training in the scaffold people actually ship, rather than a re-implementation, is a long-standing gap for open coding models.


TestingCatalog reports ContextQA has launched Ship, an AI quality engineer that reproduces bugs reported in Slack or Linear, records evidence and can pass findings to Claude or Codex for a fix.

It is one more attempt to put an autonomous agent between a bug report and a developer's pull request.

TestingCatalog says Gemini Notebook is moving to usage limits that refresh every 5 hours instead of 24, with one shared limit and a background queue for generations once quota runs out.

The change, spotted in app code and unconfirmed by Google, would make heavy Notebook use less of a daily cliff.
