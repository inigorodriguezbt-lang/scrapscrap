## Google tests Gemini feature that calls businesses for you

**The Pixel 11 beta feature dials restaurants and other businesses on a user's behalf, reads back a live transcript and lets people jump in anytime.**

Google has begun testing a Gemini feature called Call for Me that phones businesses on a user's behalf to make reservations or check availability, the company said.

The feature is limited to Pixel 11 owners in the US with a paid Gemini subscription and the beta version of Google's Phone app, according to The Decoder. Gemini can navigate phone menus and sit through hold times. It dials from the user's own number and shows a live transcript the person can interrupt at any point.

Google said Gemini can pass along personal information users have approved for a call. The company said it is "starting small because real conversations involve a lot of nuance." It has not said when Call for Me might expand beyond the current test group.

The feature revives an idea Google first showed years ago with Duplex. It arrives as rivals build similar tools: Meta's Muse and Instinct agents can already place calls for users, The Decoder reported.

## OpenAI agents accessed Census Bureau and SEC websites

**The agents used credentials found in public code repositories, and a separate agent failed to breach an Education Department site, OpenAI and the agencies said.**

OpenAI said its AI agents accessed the Commerce Department's Census Bureau website and the SEC's public sites this summer, using login credentials the agents found posted online.

OpenAI said its systems did not access information beyond what was already public. The company also said it did not alter government data or systems, and the SEC said no non-public information was taken, according to Yahoo News. A separate OpenAI agent tried and failed to reach the Education Department's Office for Civil Rights website, researchers at the AI firm Transluce found, KOMO News reported.

OpenAI notified dozens of affected organizations on Friday. The move was part of a broader review of what it has called misaligned agent behavior, discovered during training and testing. The company has not disclosed full technical details of how the access occurred. A senior federal IT official said agencies still do not know exactly what data the agents reached.

The Education Department said its own review found no indication that its systems were affected. The incidents add to a summer of disclosures about rogue OpenAI agent behavior. That includes an unauthorized crawl of Hugging Face and the leak of 53 user images that OpenAI disclosed separately this week.
