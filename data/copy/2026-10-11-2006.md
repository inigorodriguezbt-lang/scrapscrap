## Developer behind free Adobe clones releases open-source Office copies

**Brandon Thomas of ArtCraft has published WordCraft, GridCraft and DeckCraft, free Rust rebuilds of Microsoft Office. The company says GridCraft already covers 513 of Excel's 523 functions.**

Brandon Thomas, the developer behind ArtCraft, has published three open-source rebuilds of Microsoft Office apps: WordCraft, GridCraft and DeckCraft. Tom's Hardware reported the release on Sunday, 11 October, and the commentator mark_k posted about it the same day. Thomas earlier published free alternatives to Photoshop, Premiere and Lightroom, which PetaPixel covered on 7 October.

https://x.com/mark_k/status/2109355818671812844

According to Paul Thurrott, who wrote about the project on 9 October, all three are described as clean-room reimplementations in pure Rust. He says ArtCraft claims WordCraft is about 87 percent complete on ribbon commands and 62 percent on real feature parity, and that DeckCraft is in early development. Version 0.3.0 of each is listed on GitHub, according to Thurrott.

GridCraft has the most detail public. Its GitHub wiki describes a spreadsheet that reads and writes XLSX, with native builds for Windows, macOS, Linux and BSD and a browser build via WebAssembly. The project says it has 500-plus functions, PivotTables, 17 chart types and dynamic arrays, and is dual-licensed MIT or Apache-2.0. It calls itself alpha and puts itself at about 50 percent ready for real work.

The wiki also says every command can be driven through an MCP server, a command-line tool or a JSON control channel, so coding agents can operate the app. Those figures are the project's own estimates, and we have not tested the software. Coverage of the Excel ribbon is put at about 89 percent by ArtCraft, a number nobody independent has checked.

The AI angle is the build method. Mark_k says Thomas used Claude to write the apps, and TweakTown says the Adobe-style tools were built with Claude Opus 5.5. Thurrott states only that the Adobe apps used Claude and does not say the Office apps did. Thurrott also reports that download links appear to have been removed from ArtCraft's website, though the GitHub releases remain.
