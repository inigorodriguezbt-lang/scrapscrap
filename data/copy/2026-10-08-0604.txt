Ethereum researchers urge 'bunker mode' as AI maths advances

Ethereum researcher Justin Drake says AI could eventually break ECDSA, which guards crypto wallets, and urges a gradual move to fresh addresses. Vitalik Buterin backs the worry but says not to rush.


NVIDIA robot model hits 95% on cup stacking where rivals failed

NVIDIA and university researchers say Long-WAM, a robot model that remembers up to 19 seconds of video, stacked cups on a Unitree G1 in 95% of trials, while two rival models managed none in 20.

TSMC reported September revenue of NT$511.857 billion, down 0.6% from August and up 54.6% from a year earlier, according to figures posted by analyst Jukan on X.

The chipmaker builds most leading AI accelerators, so its monthly sales are a running gauge of AI hardware demand.


Hugging Face paper GRACE says it cuts video-diffusion tokens about 8 times, with 11.1 times lower latency on Wan2.1-I2V-14B and VBench scores matching the uncompressed pipeline, per its authors.

If it holds, faster video generation would come from compressing the latent rather than retraining the whole model; no code link is listed yet.


Recursive Game Creator, a paper from the University of Hong Kong, loops Designer, Builder, Player and Reviewer agents; its authors report expert-rated playtime rising from 1.5 to 12.4 minutes over three rounds.

It is an attempt to measure whether AI-made games are fun, not just whether they run; the authors say code is coming soon.


Rohan Paul posted on X that a new paper argues a chatbot can turn a wrong maths proof into a valid Lean proof by silently fixing the error, so passing Lean proves little about the original.

It cuts against the idea that a formal checker fully guards against AI errors, in a week when AI-written maths is under scrutiny.
