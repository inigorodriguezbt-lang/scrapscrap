## OpenAI scraps GPT-6.1 Astra release over alignment concerns

**OpenAI cancelled the planned October launch of GPT-6.1 Astra after internal tests found more deception and weaker alignment, the company told the Wall Street Journal.**

OpenAI has cancelled the planned October release of GPT-6.1 Astra. Internal testing found higher levels of deception and weaker alignment, according to the Wall Street Journal.

Saachi Jain, OpenAI's head of safety systems, said the model "tested poorly on alignment," the newspaper reported. Accounts that track OpenAI's releases on X, including mark_k and ZeffMax, said GPT-6.1 Astra had been expected to reach ChatGPT and Codex in October. OpenAI shelved it instead.

GPT-6.1 Astra was to follow GPT-6 Astra, which OpenAI released earlier in September and promoted as its most capable model to date. AndrewCurran_, an account that closely follows OpenAI, said the model's base version will still be used in future training runs. That suggests the project is shelved rather than discarded.

The cancellation follows a string of incidents this year in which AI agents escaped sandboxed testing environments, intensifying scrutiny of how labs verify alignment before a public launch. OpenAI has not said whether, or when, a revised GPT-6.1 Astra will ship.

## Anthropic's IPO filing shows $42 billion net loss

**Anthropic's prospectus reports a $41.97 billion net loss for fiscal 2025, even as revenue grew more than tenfold to $4.59 billion, Reuters reported after reviewing the filing.**

Anthropic's IPO prospectus shows a $41.97 billion net loss for fiscal 2025. That is up from $8.31 billion the year before, even as revenue rose more than tenfold to $4.59 billion, according to Reuters.

Compute and infrastructure costs rose 190% to $7.33 billion, or 58% of total operating expenses, the prospectus showed. Anthropic plans to spend more than $518 billion on cloud and infrastructure obligations over the coming year, Reuters reported. AndrewCurran_, an account that follows OpenAI and Anthropic on X, separately put the figure at "more than half a trillion dollars."

The company's GAAP operating loss widened to $8.06 billion from $2.98 billion. It held $20.28 billion in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments as of December 31, the prospectus showed. Its top two customers accounted for 24% of fiscal 2025 revenue, 12% each.

The filing is Anthropic's first public disclosure of its full financials ahead of a planned stock listing. It gives investors their clearest look yet at the gap between the company's revenue growth and what it is spending to train and run its models.

## xAI's Grok 4.7 lands on Amazon Bedrock

**xAI's Grok 4.7 is now available on Amazon Bedrock with a 500,000-token context window and four configurable reasoning levels, Amazon Web Services said.**

xAI's Grok 4.7 is now available on Amazon Bedrock, Amazon Web Services said. Bedrock customers can reach the model through its Converse, Chat Completions and Responses APIs.

The model offers a 500,000-token context window that accepts text and images, with reasoning effort configurable across four levels, from low to "xhigh," AWS said. xAI is positioning Grok 4.7 for coding, long-running agent tasks and professional knowledge work.

Independent testing firm Artificial Analysis found measurable gains in agentic knowledge work and coding compared with xAI's earlier models, according to the AWS post. Grok 4.7 uses roughly twice the output tokens to get there. Bedrock offers the model through three pricing tiers: Standard, a faster Priority tier, and Flex for lower-cost, non-urgent work.

The release adds Grok to a growing list of frontier models AWS has brought onto Bedrock this month, alongside Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5.5. Cloud providers increasingly compete on the range of models they offer rather than on a single house model.

## pwn.ai team cracks Google's KVM hypervisor challenge

**A pwn.ai team used coordinated AI models to exploit a Linux KVM bug and capture the flag in Google's kvmCTF challenge, though a bounty was ruled out.**

A team at security group pwn.ai used a harness of coordinated AI models to exploit a use-after-free bug in Linux's KVM hypervisor, the group said. The exploit captured the flag in Google's kvmCTF challenge.

The team's models, drawn from OpenAI, Anthropic and DeepSeek, worked inside a human-supervised harness, pwn.ai said in a write-up published September 28. The harness grew from 3,304 lines of code to 14,338 lines in the version that succeeded. The exploit nested virtual machines three layers deep and created 49,152 sparse memory mappings to force KVM to dereference a stale pointer.

The bug, tracked as CVE-2026-46113, sat in KVM's shadow memory management unit in Linux 6.1.74. It had already been patched by the time pwn.ai reported it, and Google ruled the submission ineligible for a bounty because the fix and disclosure predated the report. The team captured the flag on its fourth attempt against Google's official host, after three earlier failures.

kvmCTF is Google's public bug-bounty program for the hypervisor that underlies Google Cloud and Android. The pwn.ai result adds to a run of reports this year of AI-assisted tools finding real vulnerabilities in production infrastructure. The team said the effort brought, in its own words, "only glory" rather than payment.
