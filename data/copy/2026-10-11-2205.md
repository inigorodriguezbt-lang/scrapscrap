## Google's unreleased Gemini 4 Argon reportedly turns up in Antigravity tests

**TestingCatalog reports that some users are being served Argon, which Google says scores 77.9% on the DeepSWE coding benchmark, behind other model names. Google has not released it, and the sightings are unconfirmed.**

Some users of Google's Antigravity development environment are reportedly getting Gemini 4 Argon, an unreleased model, through A/B tests and model routing, TestingCatalog reported on Sunday. Google has not made the model public. The outlet's account rests on posts by users on X, which it has not verified, and TestingCatalog says Google's own benchmark figures should be taken with a grain of salt.

According to TestingCatalog, Google announced Argon on 30 September and has kept access to a select group since. Google claims a top score of 77.9% on DeepSWE, a long software-engineering benchmark, and first place on the Vals Index for finance, legal and tax work. Those are Google's numbers. TestingCatalog notes Google's own table shows Argon trailing Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra on some coding tests.

The routing claims come from X user auricxofficial, who says Gemini 3.8 Flash routes to Argon, and that Gemini 3.1 Pro in Antigravity points to 3.8 Flash under the model ID gemini-3.8-flash-exp-a, per TestingCatalog. The user also shared a recording of a 3D shooter made with Argon. Another user, leo114119, posted a one-shot palace render and a side-by-side of weather-app screens from Gemini 3.8 Flash and Argon, in which Argon's looks more polished, TestingCatalog says.

Leo suggested his main account missed the test while a sub-account in a family group received it, though he caveated that the theory is unreliable, and said a similar pattern occurred with Opus 5.5. TestingCatalog also notes Business Insider reported that a checkpoint called Barium-B is the one picked for public release, while employees test a separate checkpoint, Carbon. Any of these could be what people are seeing.

Antigravity builds reportedly show Ultra upgrade prompts and Argon-only context options of 256K, 512K and 900K tokens, TestingCatalog says. Google says paid API customers and AI Ultra subscribers come next, with no date. If the benchmarks hold, TestingCatalog says, Argon should compete with Anthropic's Fable 5.1 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, though it adds that it does not know what has improved. No independent tests have been published.
