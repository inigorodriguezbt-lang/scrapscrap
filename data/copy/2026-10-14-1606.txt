Ling team open-sources 560B Ling-3.1-flash under MIT licence

Ling-3.1-flash has about 560B parameters, 25B active per token and a 1M-token context, and the Ling team says it turns a rough brief into a working prototype.

https://x.com/AntLingAGI/status/2110390124743970952


Together and Stanford researchers release agents that reason in latent space

HybridAgents think in hidden states and act through text, and the authors say they beat frontier agents by 10.4% while cutting tokens by 35% to 55%.


Alibaba's Qwen reportedly cleared for Apple Intelligence in China

China's industry ministry has reportedly approved Alibaba's Qwen and Wanxiang models for Apple Intelligence, days before the iPhone Duo is due to launch there.

SNIPPETS
------------------------------------------------------------

Perplexity CEO Aravind Srinivas says its Computer agent decompiled a Windows-only keyboard app with headless Ghidra and rebuilt it as a native Rust app for Linux, lighting sync included.

It is a vendor demo of one user's project, but it shows agents being pointed at reverse engineering and porting, not only new code.

https://x.com/AravSrinivas/status/2110389625277813212


The Ling team's LLaDA2.2-flash, a 103B diffusion language model under Apache 2.0, reports 519 tokens a second on SWE-bench Verified against 303 for Ling-2.6-flash, though the baseline ran with speculative decoding.

Diffusion models generate in parallel rather than token by token, and this card is a rare agent-focused example with open weights.


The Ling team's Ming-Image-0.1-Design is a 6B MIT-licensed text-to-image model for interfaces, posters and infographics that can output transparent-background images; it has 425 likes on Hugging Face.

Text-rich design is where most image models still fail, and this one is small enough to self-host on a single 80GB GPU.


Hugging Face paper VideoJev scores video answers directly instead of generating them; its authors say the 4B model beats TimeLens2-4B by 3.3 mIoU points on grounding and answers questions 129 times faster than a thinking baseline.

The claim is the authors' own, and no code or weights are linked yet, so it is a result to watch rather than try.


Sber Robotics Center's GreenWAM is one model that serves as both a robot world model and a policy across 20 robot families; the team says it tops RoboTwin 2.0 entries, and code and weights are public.

Sharing one model between predicting the world and acting in it is a bet that could cut the cost of training robot policies.


Thomas Wolf of Hugging Face posted a nanoGPT variant that trains inside a tweet: tap and press train, which he called the GPU-poor final form.

It makes the smallest possible training loop a shareable link, aimed at people with no GPU at all.


Kimmonismus posted a photo claiming Qwen 3.8 27B runs at about 20 tokens a second on the GPU of a jailbroken PlayStation 5; the post has 218 likes and the result is unverified.

The post is a photo and a claim, not a reproducible benchmark, but it fits a run of stunts pushing big local models onto odd hardware.
