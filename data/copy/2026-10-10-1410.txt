Mathematicians split over OpenAI's 700-paper release, The Decoder reports

OpenAI put more than 700 manuscripts claiming solutions to open problems on GitHub on 6 October, and over 100 mathematicians have since answered in public.


Indie studio takes bank loan to keep voice-AI driving demo running

Easy Fox says its free Steam demo of Teach My Little Sister How To Drive costs over $1,000 a day in AI fees after players grew twentyfold.

SNIPPETS
------------------------------------------------------------

Talorys, an MIT-licensed personal AI agent that deploys to a user's own Cloudflare account with one npx command, reached 80 points on the Hacker News front page.

It runs on Cloudflare's free tier with the GLM-4.7-Flash model on Workers AI, so a self-hosted agent costs nothing to try.

The Wall Street Journal reports Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown used Republican ties to end a June standoff over model safety and win over Elon Musk, brokering a $1.25 billion-a-month SpaceX compute deal.

It is the first account of how Anthropic came to buy compute from Musk's company; the Journal's piece is paywalled and Anthropic's side was not seen.

Wired reports dozens of staff at HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster and Hachette say publishers are using AI for back-cover copy and cover art without author consent, and that staff are pushing back.

It is one of the first looks inside big publishing houses at routine AI use, as opposed to authors' own use of the tools.

Wired reports that anti-cybercrime groups are increasingly using lifelike AI bots that pose as victims to waste scammers' time and gather intelligence on them.

It turns the usual picture around: AI chatbots are being used to talk to fraudsters at scale instead of by them.

Ars Technica reports a study finding AI coding agents produce more code but not more finished software, with efficiency gains absorbed by a human code-review bottleneck.

It adds data to the argument over whether coding agents speed up teams, pointing at review capacity rather than writing speed as the limit.

Nikon disqualified the winner of its Small World in Motion video contest, saying the clip did not comply with its rules on generative AI; the BBC reports the video claimed to show cilia in a child's airway.

A new winner has since been named, and the case shows judges struggling to tell AI-generated science footage from real microscopy.

Bloomberg reports Anthropic books gross sales made through cloud partners and records the partner's cut as an expense, while OpenAI records only its net share, which clouds comparisons of their revenue.

It helps explain why OpenAI's run rate was put near $50 billion after the earlier $70 billion figure, which Axios says was recalculated to match Anthropic's method.
