Bloomberg reports SpaceX is in talks with banks and investors to raise $40 billion to buy Nvidia chips, citing people familiar, in what would be among the biggest AI debt financings.

Per Reuters citing the FT, Apollo is arranging about $10 billion in loans and $30 billion in debt, with closing expected in 2027.


PhAI Labs researchers say JEPA-Anything extends Yann LeCun's JEPA world-model design across seven fields and cut prediction error by about 35% on a simplified Pong task, The Decoder reports.

The team also reports an IL-18 plus CD73 blockade combination that killed more liver cancer cells in lab samples, though the authors say causality is unproven.


AlphaXiv highlights H-JEPA, a paper advised by Yann LeCun that stacks JEPA world models across several timescales so higher levels predict farther ahead for visual planning.

Planning in one latent space at one timescale is costly over long horizons, which is the problem the hierarchy is meant to ease.


South Korean media report that Samsung's 12-high HBM4E memory has passed qualification tests at Nvidia and other customers, according to chip-industry account Jukan.

HBM supply is the bottleneck for AI accelerators, so a qualified Samsung part matters to every maker waiting on memory; the report is unconfirmed by Samsung.


Bloomberg reports AMD chief executive Lisa Su plans to meet the head of Samsung's semiconductor business on Wednesday, as the memory crunch persists.

AMD, like Nvidia, depends on scarce high-bandwidth memory, so a Samsung deal would shape how many AI accelerators it can ship.


TechCrunch reports Nvidia-backed GPU cloud Lambda is raising up to $4 billion at a $14.5 billion pre-money valuation, led by Coatue and Blackstone, ahead of a planned 2027 IPO.

It adds to the run of financing for AI compute providers, as the sector leans on debt and private capital to buy chips.


LibreOffice's maker says it has no plans to add AI to the editor's default configuration, citing user privacy, TechCrunch reports, framing 'no AI' as a feature.

It marks a counter-trend to Google and Microsoft pushing assistants into every office suite, aimed at privacy-minded users.


Tom's Hardware reports Qualcomm will pay Huawei to license patents for the first time, in a multi-year cross-license covering 5G and AI, including some Huawei US patents Qualcomm is buying.

It reverses 25 years of flow in which Huawei paid Qualcomm, and shows how far Huawei's patent portfolio has grown.
