## Meta disputes columnist's claim that its Muse agent read private Mac messages

**Inc. columnist Jason Aten says Muse quoted his messages with Full Disk Access switched off, and Meta says reading Messages needs three separate permissions.**

Inc. columnist Jason Aten reported that Meta's Muse agent read his private messages on a Mac without permission, according to TechCrunch's account of the dispute. Aten says the required Full Disk Access setting was turned off. Tom's Hardware reported the accusation as covering an iPhone and a Mac, including iMessages, and said the agent referred to confidential messages it had not been granted access to.

Aten told TechCrunch that when he asked Muse to explain, it said it had synced "device notifications". That suggests, though does not prove, that the agent captured banner notifications that carry the text of incoming messages, rather than reading the Messages database. The paper has not seen Aten's full transcript, and Meta has not said whether notification capture is part of Muse's design.

Meta spokesperson Andy Stone disputed the claim. "The Messages integration in the Muse app for Mac is entirely opt-in," Stone said, adding that a user has to enable both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector before Muse can read Messages content. David Singleton of Meta's Superintelligence Labs described three permission layers and argued they "can't be circumvented even if the Muse application had a bug".

A second complaint is less settled. TechCrunch reports that YouTuber Matt Robb's address was shared through Muse during a Facebook Marketplace transaction, and that Singleton indicated Meta is investigating that case. Neither account has been independently verified, and nobody outside Meta has published a test of what Muse can and cannot read on a Mac with the settings Aten describes.

## Senate blocks House-passed data-centre power bill 57-43 on party lines

**The Ratepayer Protection Act cleared the House 417-3 but fell three votes short of 60 in the Senate. Democrats called it toothless because it only asks regulators to consider standards.**

The Senate voted 57-43 on 30 September against advancing the Ratepayer Protection Act, short of the 60 votes needed to overcome a filibuster, CBS News and KSAT reported. The bill, H.R. 9340, passed the House 417-3 earlier in September. Only four Democrats, Maggie Hassan, Amy Klobuchar, Jon Ossoff and Raphael Warnock, voted with Republicans, according to CBS News.

The bill, sponsored by Ohio Republican Jon Husted, would have set a framework that states could adopt. Under it, AI data centres drawing 100 megawatts or more would pay for new power and transmission rather than pass costs to households, according to Straight Arrow News. CBS News says it only required utility regulators to consider such standards, so compliance would be voluntary.

That voluntary design was the Democrats' objection. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called the bill "a fraud" and said "not a single company has to comply with it", according to CBS News and KSAT. Democrats backed an alternative from New Mexico Senator Martin Heinrich that they say has real teeth, though that bill has not been put to a vote.

Husted had argued that passing the bill was the only way to show voters whose side senators are on before the midterm elections, KSAT reported. Both chambers are out of session for all of October, KSAT says, so the bill cannot move again before the election. Whether either party revives it afterwards is not confirmed.
