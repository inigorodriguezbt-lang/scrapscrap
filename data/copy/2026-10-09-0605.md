## Anthropic is hiring a lead to teach 2028 presidential candidates about AI

**A job posting for a political programs lead, paying $295,000 to $345,000, describes a team that would brief candidates in both parties and run Anthropic's political giving, Fortune reported.**

Anthropic is building a "presidential engagement" programme for the 2028 US election, Fortune reported on Thursday, based on a job posting for a political programs lead. The role would map the likely fields in both parties, offer AI policy education to candidates and advisers, and prepare Anthropic executives for those conversations. Anthropic did not respond to Fortune's request for comment.

The posting lists a salary range of $295,000 to $345,000 and asks for at least 12 years in campaigns, party committees or political organisations, plus a record of working with both parties. The hire would also plan for the platform, convention and transition periods, and run the day-to-day of the company's political giving. The posting says decisions made over the next two years will shape the future enormously.

The effort sits on top of existing political activity, according to Fortune. Anthropic launched AnthroPAC, an employee-funded committee with a $5,000 annual cap per person, in April. In July, chief executive Dario Amodei gave $1 million to Public First, a pro-regulation super PAC. Fortune also notes that OpenAI said in June it has no employee-funded PACs.

Outside voices were split on what it means. Bruce Freed of the Center for Political Accountability told Fortune he had not seen such a formal structure before and reads it as an effort to shape or stave off regulation. Katie Harbath, a former Facebook policy director, said the role resembles those at other tech firms, though running PAC money is unusual.

Senator Alex Padilla, a California Democrat, told Fortune that education and influence are different things and the public should know where the line falls. He said AI rules should be set by elected representatives. Fortune places the move before the November midterms and a possible Anthropic share sale; the company has not commented on either.
