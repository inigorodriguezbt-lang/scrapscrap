Nvidia says RTX Spark laptops ship Friday with a 74B open model

Nvidia says its Nemotron 3.5 Bolt, a 74-billion-parameter open model with training data and recipes, runs in 24GB through 2-bit quantisation on RTX Spark laptops that launch on 16 October.

Nvidia opens up the code for its Run AI GPU scheduler

Nvidia says the full Run:ai platform, which schedules and splits GPUs on Kubernetes, is now open source on GitHub, with no paid subscription needed but no Nvidia support either.

Nvidia says it is committing $250 million in computing access over five years to UK research on AI agent safety and security, and joining the Earthshot Prize as a founding alliance partner.

The pledge lands a week before Nvidia's GTC Berlin keynote on 21 October, and the post does not name who receives the compute.

Nvidia says Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B, a 4GB third-generation ACE game-agent model, arrives on 23 October and will appear in the SkyrimNET mod the same day.

It would put a small open language model inside a modded game running on a local GPU, rather than calling a cloud service.


Artificial Analysis says it added DeepSeek V4.1 Flash to its AA-AgentPerf benchmark, and that Nvidia's GB300 leads at 100 tokens per second per user with up to 261 million tokens per second per megawatt.

The benchmark replays real agentic coding sessions, so it measures hardware on the workload that is now driving demand for inference chips.


Commentator kimmonismus says The Information reported that OpenAI diverted about 10 percent of its compute to safety, and that its next flagship, reportedly using loop transformers, may face 30 extra days of US government testing.

The account is a secondhand summary of a paywalled report, and OpenAI has not confirmed the figures in the posts seen here.


OpenAI's Codex app threads now take up to 60 percent less disk space, says reach_vb, who says the team also changed of how Codex reads data from disk.

Long-running agent threads are the main thing eating storage in coding agents, so the change should help people who keep sessions open for days.
