STORIES
------------------------------------------------------------

arXiv caps preprint submitters at 2 papers a month as volume doubles

arXiv now allows two new submissions a month per submitter, after September brought 40,363 papers, roughly double the 20,569 of September 2024, it said.


Google says Starship needs about 1,800 launches to make space data centres work

TechCrunch reports a Google white paper puts the figure near 180 launches a year for a decade, assuming 200 tonnes per flight and launch costs around $200 a kilogram by 2035.


Time reports Trump consulted Grok for hours before the Venezuela invasion

Time cites an unnamed source saying Trump spent hours questioning Elon Musk's Grok about Venezuela in December, weeks before the US invaded on 3 January.


Boston Dynamics gives Atlas a four-fingered hand with no pinky

The new hand has 13 degrees of freedom, up from seven, and an Atlas engineer says a pinky was not worth three extra degrees of freedom of complexity.


Ideogram says its 4.5 model edits one region and leaves the rest alone

Ideogram says 4.5 changes only what a user asks it to and outputs at native 2K, at $0.008 to $0.22 an image across four quality tiers.


Marketing firm says Opus 5.5 writes 'this matters' 116 times more than people

Graphite compared 10,000 pre-ChatGPT articles with model rewrites and says Opus 5.5 favours 'dependable' and 'this matters', while GPT-6 Astra leans on 'not simply'.

SNIPPETS
------------------------------------------------------------

Ataraxos, built by researchers at Carnegie Mellon, NYU, Stanford and MIT, beat four-time world champion Pim Niemeijer 15 games to 1 with 4 draws in a 20-game Stratego series, The Decoder reports.

The Decoder says it trained for a week on 16 Nvidia H100s for under $8,000, about a five-hundredth of what DeepMind's DeepNash cost.


9to5Google reports, citing Bloomberg, that some Google employees say Gemini 4 does less well in real work and struggles with certain coding tasks; Google says calling it underperforming in coding would be inaccurate.

The employee claims are unnamed and untested; Google's reply points to its position at the frontier, and the two accounts have not been reconciled.


Ai2 released Olmo-core 3, an open-source training framework for large mixture-of-experts models, saying a 47-billion-parameter MoE reached 52,000 tokens per second per GPU on eight B300s, 2.7 times its predecessor.

Ai2 says the code is on GitHub at allenai/olmo-core, was benchmarked past one trillion parameters, and will train Ai2's upcoming Olmo MoE model.


Shopify launched Canvas, which lets merchants build and edit stores by chatting with its Sidekick agent, with changes rendered in real code, TechCrunch reports. It is desktop-only for now.

TechCrunch says it does not yet support third-party themes, app blocks or extensions, and competes with builders from Wix, Squarespace, Webflow and Framer.


Legato launched Frames, $999 AI hearing glasses for up to moderate hearing loss that separate voices from background noise, with all processing done on the device, TechCrunch reports.

The 34-gram frames run 10 to 12 hours, have no cameras, and were founded by two former Bose staff; Legato says they qualify for vision insurance.


Weizmann Institute researchers led by Michal Irani built a tool that reconstructs images people are viewing from fMRI scans, needing one hour of calibration per subject instead of 40, MIT Technology Review reports.

Irani concedes failures, such as a cake rebuilt as a sandwich and a dog as a goat; the team wants to extend it to video, audio and imagined images.


A Tokyo court ruled that using voice actor Kenjiro Tsuda's voice without permission infringes his publicity rights, in what Engadget describes as Japan's first case over AI voice cloning.

A TikTok account cloned his voice from July 2024 to mid-2025 and reached 200,000 followers; the court refused a takedown order because the account was already deleted.


DeepSeek Harness, an MIT-licensed, plugin-based agent framework with macOS and Windows desktop apps and a web UI launched by npx @deepseek-ai/dsh web, has 100 points on the Hacker News front page.

DeepSeek's page calls it a public preview, and its README labels it a developer preview that expects compatibility-breaking changes.


ServiceNow's CoreAI team says AutoSynthData, which finds a model's failures and generates synthetic tasks to fix them, lifted a Gemma-4-26B-A4B model's Pass@1 by 7.2 points on a hybrid domain.

ServiceNow says it generated about 2,000 samples in 18 hours; the results come from its own evaluations and have not been independently reproduced.


SpaceXAI released Grok for Intune, a separate iOS app for organisations that manage devices with Microsoft Intune, with Grok Imagine, voice mode and web and X search, 9to5Mac reports.

Per 9to5Mac, IT teams deploy it through Intune, it honours app protection policies on saving and sharing, and it has no in-app purchases.


Apple's LoopCD paper says it can halve the recurrent loops of a looped language model while matching or beating full-depth baselines, lifting AIME 2024 pass@1 from 61.88% to 73.33%, per @arankomatsuzaki.

The numbers are the authors' own, taken from a summary post with an attached figure; we have not read the paper itself.
