## Anthropic releases Claude Haiku 5.5 with an adjustable effort setting

**Anthropic says Haiku 5.5 is its first Haiku with an effort dial, aimed at high-volume work under 100,000 tokens, and it is live on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure from today.**

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on Wednesday, posting the launch as a thread on its Claude account. The company calls it a significant step up over Haiku 4.5 across coding, computer use and knowledge work. It says the model is available now on all platforms, including Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure.

https://x.com/claudeai/status/2107894042235166750

Haiku 5.5 is the first Haiku with an adjustable effort setting, Anthropic says, so users can choose whether to optimise for cost or intelligence on each task. The company describes the model as built for high-volume, cost-sensitive work such as summaries and classification, and says it is fast enough for live customer support and browser use.

Anthropic also says it pairs well with Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 as a subagent on coding work. It calls the model especially good value on tasks under 100,000 tokens, around 90 per cent of requests to its previous Haiku model. These are the company's claims, and no outside benchmark has appeared.

On pricing, the commentator kimmonismus said Haiku 5.5 would match OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna rates for prompts up to 100,000 tokens: $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output. Anthropic's thread, as read, does not state prices, so treat the figures as unconfirmed.

Anthropic also said it is halving the price of cache reads on Claude Sonnet 5.5, to $0.10 per million tokens, which it says makes Sonnet 5.5 around 20 per cent cheaper on most long-running work. On alignment, the company says Haiku 5.5 shows far fewer instances of misaligned behaviour than Haiku 4.5 across almost all of its evaluations.

## Perplexity open-sources embedding models that search PDF pages without OCR

**Perplexity says its new 9B and 0.6B pplx-embed-v2-late models share one embedding space for text and images, and score 92.4 per cent on the MADQA document benchmark.**

Perplexity released two open-weights embedding models on Wednesday, pplx-embed-v2-late, with 9 billion and 0.6 billion parameters. Chief executive Aravind Srinivas said the weights are on Hugging Face now. The company says both models handle text, images and whole pages in a single shared embedding space, so one can query across them.

https://x.com/AravSrinivas/status/2107871834205196784

Srinivas said the larger model is meant for indexing multimodal data and the small one for querying on a device. He said the pair also lets users search over PDF pages with no OCR, the step that turns page images into text. Perplexity describes the models as multi-vector, or late-interaction, rather than producing one vector per item.

Perplexity says the models reach frontier performance. Srinivas gave two figures: 92.4 per cent on MADQA, a document question-answering benchmark, and 64 per cent on BrowseComp+, a web-research test. Both are the company's own numbers, and nobody outside Perplexity has reproduced them yet.

The release follows Perplexity's earlier pplx-embed work. We have not opened the licence terms or run the models, so how they compare with Google's recently released EmbeddingGemma 2 is untested, and the company has not said which models it measured against.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-pplx-embed-v2-late/

## Biohub leads $1.8 billion push to train AI that predicts how cells behave

**Meta, Google DeepMind and Isomorphic Labs put in $300 million and the US Department of Energy over $500 million, and Biohub says a first open dataset is about a year away.**

Biohub, the research organisation backed by Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan, is coordinating a $1.8 billion initiative to train AI models that predict cell behaviour, The Decoder reports. The Verge, citing earlier Reuters reporting, describes the aim as a "virtual cell" that researchers can use to fight disease.

Meta, Google DeepMind and Isomorphic Labs are jointly investing $300 million, according to The Decoder. It says the US Department of Energy is adding over $500 million for lab measurements and computing, and Biohub itself $500 million, pledged in April. The National Institutes of Health is coordinating datasets built with prior federal money.

The Decoder reports that commercial funders get one year of exclusive access to the data they fund before it is released publicly, while government-funded research is unrestricted. Biohub research lead Alex Rives told the outlet that "a first dataset should be ready in about a year".

The Decoder places the effort among other AI-for-biology projects, including an AI-guided drug development lab at Anthropic and a $125 million OpenAI Foundation commitment to biological datasets. Whether a predictive cell model can be built from the planned data is not established, because the announcement describes funding and goals, not a working model.

## Microsoft lets Copilot act on local files across Windows

**At its Windows and Surface event in San Francisco, Microsoft showed Copilot taking actions on files on your PC, in a mix of local and cloud models it calls Hybrid Intelligence.**

Microsoft showed an upgrade on Wednesday that lets Copilot work with local files on a PC and take actions across Windows, The Verge reports. Engadget says Copilot will carry out actions based on local files with the user's permission. Microsoft calls the approach Hybrid Intelligence, in which apps and tools rely on a mix of local and cloud AI.

Engadget's roundup says models will run on the device while drawing on cloud resources for extra power, and that Microsoft is adding the ability to generate apps and widgets. The company stressed making Windows "the most secure platform for AI agents", according to Engadget. Neither outlet gave a release date for the Copilot changes.

Starting this fall, taskbar search will offer thousands of quick actions, Engadget reports, such as switching on dark mode, taking a screenshot or adjusting the volume, and it will open the new Copilot app. Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang attended the event in San Francisco.

Ahead of the event, trade outlets expected a Surface laptop built on an Nvidia RTX Spark chip. We could not confirm its specifications, price or ship date from the coverage we read, so those are left out until Microsoft or Nvidia publishes them.

## Telling models to write in 1866 telegraph style cut tokens up to 49 per cent

**A blogger's test of Gemma, Qwen, Nemotron and GLM says 'cablese' notes saved 40 to 49 per cent of tokens while other models read them back with no accuracy loss.**

A blog post published on 4 October reports that instructing language models to write compressed records in telegraph style, or "cablese", cut token use by 40 to 49 per cent. The post, on Five Minutes Forward, reached the Hacker News front page with 67 points. The results are the author's own and have not been replicated.

The author tested model families Gemma, Qwen, Nemotron and GLM-5.3-Flash, plus GPT-5-mini, as both writers of compressed records and readers answering questions from them. The set was 50 passages with about 1,300 questions whose answers can be checked, such as dates, names and counts.

Readers working from the compressed text kept accuracy ratios of 0.99 to 1.10 against plain text, where 1.00 is parity, the post says. It also reports that models never shown cablese examples could still read it, and that a codebook-substitution approach saved only about 10 per cent.

The recommended instruction, as the author puts it, is to write a complete record in telegraphese, drop articles and filler, abbreviate, and keep every fact, number and proper noun verbatim. The savings apply where a machine, not a person, consumes the output, and the sample of 50 passages is small.

## Lean proof of the best packing of 11 squares reaches Hacker News

**A GitHub repo says an AI system's formal proof that 11 squares pack into a side of about 3.877 passed Lean verification across 7,920 modules with no unproved steps.**

A GitHub repository called 11SquaresFormalized, posted on 6 October, claims a complete, machine-checked proof of the optimal packing of 11 squares. It reached the Hacker News front page with 74 points and 35 comments. No independent mathematician has confirmed the result in anything we read.

The repo says the construction gives a side length of about 3.877, from a formula involving a root of an eighth-degree polynomial. The proof is written in Lean 4 against the Mathlib library. It credits EvolvingPrograms, a person posting as ctjlewis, and other contributors, with details in an acknowledgements file.

According to the repository, verification accepted all 7,920 local Lean modules with zero admissions, meaning no step was left unproved. Expensive numerical checks use Lean's native_decide, which trusts the compiled checker. The authors provide scripts so anyone can rerun the verification locally.

The repository's claims are its own. Lean checks proofs mechanically, but a reader would still need to confirm that the statement being proved is the one mathematicians care about, and that the numerical certificates are sound. We have not seen an independent review.

Read it on the site: https://theaipost.net/story/find-11-squares-lean/
