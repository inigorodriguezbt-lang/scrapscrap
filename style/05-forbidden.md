# 05 · Forbidden

Two lists. The first is journalism's; the second is specific to machine-written
prose, and is the one that will actually catch you.

## Banned vocabulary

Observed rate of hype words in professional copy: **0.4 per thousand.** In a
300-word story that is one word every eight stories. Treat as zero.

**Hype:** unprecedented · revolutionary · game-changing · groundbreaking ·
landmark · seismic · historic · monumental · transformative · disruptive ·
cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · next-generation · breakthrough

**Intensifiers** (observed 0.2 per thousand): very · extremely · incredibly ·
remarkably · highly · significantly · substantially · dramatically · massively

**Editorialising verbs:** slammed · blasted · touted · boasted · admitted ·
revealed · unveiled · shattered · dominated · crushed

**Vague abstractions:** the landscape · the space · the ecosystem · the arena ·
players · offerings · solutions · leverage (as a verb) · robust · seamless ·
scalable (unless quoting)

## The AI tells

These are the constructions that mark text as machine-written. They are more
damaging than the hype words because writers do not notice them.

| Tell | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| `In a move that could reshape…` | editorial framing before any fact |
| `This underscores the importance of…` | tells the reader what to think |
| `It's worth noting that…` | if it were worth noting you would have noted it |
| `As the industry continues to evolve…` | true of every industry, every day |
| `marks a significant milestone` | significance asserted, not shown |
| `signals a broader shift toward…` | unfalsifiable |
| `raises important questions about…` | name the question or cut it |
| `delve into` / `dive deep into` | not news vocabulary |
| `a testament to` | pure evaluation |
| `stands as` / `serves as` | padding for `is` |
| `remains to be seen` | ends a paragraph with nothing |
| `only time will tell` | delete on sight |
| `Meanwhile,` opening a paragraph | fake transition |
| `Furthermore,` / `Moreover,` / `Additionally,` | news paragraphs don't need glue |

## Structural bans

**The rule of three.** `faster, cheaper, and more capable` — the tricolon is a
rhetorical reflex, not reporting. Two items, or a specific list of five.

**The rhetorical question.** Never in a headline. Never opening a paragraph.

**The em-dash aside for drama.** Em dashes appear at 2.8 per thousand in real
copy, so they are not banned — but they appear as parenthetical punctuation,
not as suspense. `The result — and this is the remarkable part — was…` is not
news writing.

**The semicolon.** Observed **zero** times in 5,017 words. If you reach for one,
you have two sentences.

**Elegant variation.** Having called it `the model`, do not switch to `the
system`, `the offering`, `the platform`. Repeat the noun. Variation makes the
reader wonder whether a new thing was introduced.

**The summarising final sentence.** `The announcement represents the latest
development in an increasingly competitive market.` Adds nothing, appears in
every machine draft, always cuttable.

## One permitted exception

Hedges are **not** on this list and must not be edited out. `could`, `may`,
`appears`, `is expected to`, `according to` are load-bearing. Suppressing them
to sound confident is the single worst thing you can do to a news story.
