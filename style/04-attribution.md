# 04 · Attribution and certainty

The measurement that should change how you write: in professional news copy,
**hedge words appear more often than `said`** — 5.8 per thousand against 5.2.
Marking uncertainty is not a garnish on reporting. It is most of the job.

## The three tiers

Every factual claim in this paper sits in one of three tiers, and the sentence
must make clear which.

### Tier 1 — Established
Multiple independent sources, or a matter of record. State it plainly.

> Mistral raised $600 million in a round led by General Catalyst.

### Tier 2 — Asserted by an interested party
A company's claim about its own product, a benchmark it ran, a figure only it
can see. **Never state this in the paper's voice.**

> Nvidia said the part delivers 2.4 times the inference throughput of the
> prior generation. The figure is the company's own, measured on workloads it
> selected.

The second sentence is optional but often the most valuable in the story.

### Tier 3 — Reported but unconfirmed
One outlet has it; nobody else does yet.

> The Information reported that the model was trained on a 40,000-chip cluster.
> Neither the company nor its cloud provider confirmed the figure.

## The attribution verb

Use **`said`**. It is invisible, which is the point — the reader's attention
goes to the claim, not the verb.

Do not use: `revealed`, `admitted`, `boasted`, `touted`, `unveiled`, `confessed`,
`slammed`, `blasted`, `shared`. Each smuggles in a judgment. `Revealed` implies
concealment. `Admitted` implies guilt. `Unveiled` is a press-release word.

`Confirmed` is permitted and precise — but only when the thing was previously
reported and is now being verified. It is not a synonym for `said`.

`Declined to comment` is reporting. Use it. A company's refusal to give a number
is itself a fact about the number.

## Placement

Mid-sentence or end. Front-loading attribution buries the news behind the
source:

> **Weak:** According to a statement released by the company on Tuesday, the
> model is now available in the API.
>
> **Better:** The model is now available in the API, the company said Tuesday.

## Laundering — the failure to watch for

The characteristic failure of automated news writing is taking a vendor's claim
and restating it as established fact. It happens through deletion: the source
clause gets dropped as redundant, and a marketing number becomes the paper's
finding.

| Laundered | Reported |
|---|---|
| Orion-2 outperforms all competing models on reasoning. | OpenAI said Orion-2 scores above competing models on three reasoning benchmarks it selected. |
| The chip delivers 2.4x throughput. | Nvidia put the gain at 2.4 times, measured on workloads it chose. |
| The round values the company at $14 billion. | The round values the company at $14 billion, according to two people briefed on the terms. |

Test every sentence: **who would have to be right for this to be true?** If the
answer is "the company selling it," the sentence needs a source clause.

## Disagreement is the story

When sources conflict, that conflict is usually the most newsworthy thing
available. Do not average it away, and do not pick a side.

> The two accounts differ on timing. The company said shipments began Tuesday;
> two partners said they had not yet received units as of Thursday.

## Anonymous sourcing

If the material came from a post by a named account, name the account. This
paper's sources are public by construction — there is no reason to write
`sources said` when the source is a public statement with a URL. `Sources said`
in this paper is a bug.
